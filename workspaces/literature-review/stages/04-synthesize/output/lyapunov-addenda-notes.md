Question put to each paper: does it give a discrete SVD (or QR) algorithm for a product of map Jacobians that lyap-svd-stabilized could use at general d? The spec holds that algorithm and implements d = 2 only (`lyap-svd-stabilized-spec.md`, Algorithm item 5 and open point P1).

## Per-paper annotations

- **Stewart1997: yes, a general-n discrete product SVD algorithm.** Algorithm (2.1), p.31, takes p square (n x n) factors A(1)...A(p) and computes the singular values and vectors of their product without forming it (Eq. 1.4, p.30). It is the Cholesky-LR (LRCH, Rutishauser and Schwartz) iteration written as repeated product QR factorizations by treppen-iteration, the sweep (2.4), p.32. Each iteration sweeps the factors from last to first and replaces them by the transposed triangular factors, so all p factors are stored. The product of the triangular factors converges to the diagonal matrix of singular values (Sect. 3, Eq. 3.1, p.33), and the singular vectors are accumulated by (2.5), p.32. Cost is about n^3 (7p/3 - 2) flops per iteration (p.32). Convergence is geometric at a rate set by the singular-value ratios; when those ratios are far below the unit roundoff only a few iterations are needed, and for Lyapunov products with hundreds or thousands of factors the author reports that two iterations suffice (p.34). Shifts by hyperbolic rotations (Sect. 8, pp.40-41) accelerate the case of close singular values. Backward error: Theorem 1, pp.35-36, says the computed singular values are exact for factors perturbed by O(N n^(3/2) u ||A(i)||_F), N being the number of iterations. Stability caveat: high relative accuracy is not automatic. Theorem 2, p.37, covers outer multiplicative perturbations only, the counterexample is on pp.37-38, and "stable product" is Definition 1, p.38 (the author points to hyperbolicity results on p.39). Numerical evidence: Lorenz products of 1000 and 10,000 RK4 factors (Examples 4 and 5, p.43; Table 9.1, p.44). The singular values there span about 10^3941 down to 10^-63295 and are reported to eight digits (p.43), but the paper does not say how the exponent is carried in the unshifted code (a separate exponent is mentioned only for the shifting step, p.41). In exact arithmetic the method equals the QR algorithm on A^T A used by Abarbanel, Brown and Kennel (p.31). Points the paper does not settle for lyap-svd-stabilized, all derived here and not in the paper: the sweep order is last factor first and the product order is A(1)...A(p), against Y_n = M_{n-1}...M_0 in the spec, so one would feed the transposed factors (same singular values); the factors would be window matrices of the GS stream rather than single Jacobians; and the storage of p factors conflicts with the streaming reducer contract. Verdict: this is the algorithm the spec's P1 asks for; using it needs a stored-factor mode or a stated memory trade-off, an owner decision.
- **OliveiraStewart2000 (not yet supplied, record only):** its Crossref reference list cites Stewart1997 as ETNA 5, 29-47. No content read. Wanted.
- **DieciVanVleck2008 (item 2, preprint pages): no SVD algorithm; QR error theory for flows.** Gives global error bounds for the computed orthogonal factor Q_k that do not depend on the length of the interval (abstract, p.1). The set-up is the discrete QR technique applied to the transition matrices of an integrated ODE, Eqs. (2.1)-(2.4), p.3, with the backward error form (2.5), (2.6), p.3: the computed Q_k and R_k are exact for transition matrices perturbed by E_j, each bounded by the local error. A first bound by diagonalising the triangular problem is Theorem 3.2, Eq. (3.12), p.6, penalised by cond(Z). The sharper route, treating the triangular term directly, is Theorem 4.2, Eq. (4.21), p.10, and needs integral separation of the diagonal. For the Batch B specs it offers a frame for the window-factorisation tolerance: round-off in the Jacobians (and for flows the integration error) plays the role of E_j in (2.6), and the Q error stays bounded for integrally separated problems. It gives no algorithm for map products, and its hypotheses (bounded A(t), integral separation) are not checked for the standard map. Relevant to the QR side only.
- **VuNguyenPhan2025 (item 3, printed pages): discrete and continuous QR for semi-linear DAEs; no map algorithm.** The discrete QR method, Eqs. (15)-(19), pp.7-8, and Algorithm 3.1, pp.8-9, advance the linearised system with an embedded Runge-Kutta pair under local error control (step rule p.8) and update the exponent sums by adding the log of the diagonal of each R factor, Eq. (19), p.8. That is the same sum S_i as in the GS spec, but for an integrated DAE with adaptive steps. The continuous method is Algorithm 3.3, p.11. The error analysis is mostly by reference to earlier work (p.12; Theorem 4.1, p.13, treats the linearisation). It offers an example of error-controlled stepping of the variational equation; for maps there is no step to control, so nothing is usable for lyap-svd-stabilized.
- **DieciElia2006 (item 7, journal pages 502-531): theory of the SVD method; no algorithm.** Proves that SVD techniques are sound for systems with stable and distinct Lyapunov exponents (Theorem 4.2, p.518) and extracts growth directions (Theorem 5.8, p.526). Section 4, p.516, describes the discrete SVD method in words: keep the fundamental matrix as a product of transition matrices on short subintervals and take its SVD without forming it, with "a well written algorithmic description" in Stewart1997 (their ref. [26]). It cites Oliveira and Stewart 2000 for the linear-algebra side (p.504) and a companion paper on SVD algorithms (their ref. [8], then submitted). The only equations given for computing are the continuous smooth-SVD ODEs (23)-(25), p.517, so flows only. Lead for the owner, not part of this run: the companion paper is on Crossref as doi:10.1016/j.matcom.2008.03.005, "SVD algorithms to approximate spectra of dynamical systems", Mathematics and Computers in Simulation 79 (2008) 1235-1254 (found by the title search of this run, not read).
- **DieciEliaLopez2004 (item 8, printed pages 255-264): continuous smooth SVD on the Lorentz group only.** Builds a smooth SVD for X in the Lorentz group (X^T D X = D, Eq. 1, p.255) from its polar factor (structure in Eqs. 5 and 6, p.258) and gives ODEs for the factors (Eq. 11, p.260; the reduced form (17), p.261, for the m negative exponents), integrated by Runge-Kutta with orthogonal projection (p.262). Examples are small linear systems with known exponents (Tables 1 and 2, pp.262-263). It is a continuous method tied to the Lorentz structure; the Jacobian of a symplectic map is not in that group, and no discrete product algorithm appears. Not usable for lyap-svd-stabilized.
- **DieciVanVleck2002 (item 9, preprint pages): continuous SVD ODEs; no discrete algorithm.** Section 7 (pp.12-15) justifies the continuous SVD approach: exponents from the diagonal of G = U^T A U by (7.1) and (7.2), p.12; the factor ODEs (7.3) with H and K in (7.4), p.13; equivalence of the two exponent definitions under integral separation, Theorem 7.2, p.13. The numerical section (8.1, p.16) works with the continuous QR change of variables, with Householder and Givens only for orthonormal integration of Q, not for a product of map Jacobians. Nothing here for lyap-svd-stabilized.

## Open questions and conflicts

- Stewart1997 needs all factors stored for every sweep, while the GS reducer contract (spec Algorithm item 5) is streaming. Owner choice: a stored-factor mode for runs that ask for general-d SVD exponents, or keep L9 at d = 2.
- Stewart1997 gives high relative accuracy only for stable products (Definition 1, p.38). Whether a standard-map Jacobian product is stable in that sense is not shown by the paper.
- The Geist1990 CPU ratios come from one 1990 vector machine and one system; they support the ordering (GS cheapest, continuous QR far slower), not the ratios in Julia.
- Dieci and Elia 2008 (doi:10.1016/j.matcom.2008.03.005) is the companion algorithm paper named by DieciElia2006; it is not part of this run.
- Item 4 (Shevchenko2024) and item 5 (CincottaGiordanoShevchenko2022) are not annotated: kept for later papers.
