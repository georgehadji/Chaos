# Notes: lyapunov-algorithms

Stage 04 (synthesize), targeted run dated 2026-10-12: the focus is L-SQ1 papers (published Lyapunov values for the standard map) with open-access full text. Five per-paper notes were merged: SalaArtuso2015, Shevchenko2014, HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019, PradoReynoso2022, Tarnopolski2015. The other included papers of this topic were not read in this stage (owner's decision: targeted stage 04).

## Per-paper annotations

- **SalaArtuso2015**: Studies the geometry of invariant directions of standard-like maps (McMillan form of the Chirikov map) and shows that the finite-time exponent is an orbit average of a one-step slope term plus an initial-vector transient that fades like 1/n. The exponent is computed with a scalar Moebius slope recursion (not Benettin or QR) over single orbits of 10^9 iterations. As a P1 target it gives only two single-orbit values, at K = pi/2 and K = 2 pi, with no error bar and no stated precision.
- **Shevchenko2014**: A paper on resonance multiplets in perturbed pendulums, where the standard map appears as the limit of infinitely many equal harmonics. Its useful content for P1 is a set of piecewise closed-form fits of lambda(K) for 0 < K < 10 (Eqs. 25-26), plus the large-K law ln(K/2) + 1/K^2. The underlying standard-map data come from earlier work and no run length is given, so the fits are smoothed reference curves with visible joins, not measurements.
- **HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019**: Compares Lyapunov, recurrence and stickiness times in the standard map and gives empirical fits of the converged exponent (called LCN) against K and against the available chaotic area. It uses a closed-form tangent-slope (stretching number) iteration, with no stated renormalization, precision or initial deviation vector. The fits ln(0.55 + 0.46 K) and 0.925 ln K carry no error bars, and the paper's main subject is characteristic times, so it is a rough sanity target and a source of run-length evidence for convergence in sticky zones.
- **PradoReynoso2022**: The main subject is the growth rate of out-of-time-ordered correlators (OTOC) and its gap to the Lyapunov exponent (about ln(sqrt 2) at large K); it is not a Lyapunov-algorithm paper. For the standard map with K >= 4 it prints an analytic one-step-Jacobian estimate of the exponent and uses ln(K/2) as the large-K asymptote. It offers nothing for K < 4 and no numerical run settings, so it is a weak P1 target usable only for large K.
- **Tarnopolski2015**: The main subject is the correlation between the Hurst exponent of the p series and the maximal Lyapunov exponent, not the accuracy of exponent algorithms. The exponent is computed from the full Jacobian product over 10^4 iterations with no renormalization (largest eigenvalue of the product's Gram matrix), at p0 = 0 on a K x x0 grid. It prints no lambda(K) values, only a few aggregate numbers, and is a poor regression target; its value lies in the contrast between the Jacobian-product and Wolf estimators and in the finite-time floor for regular orbits.

## Open questions and conflicts

1. **Eq. 26 discontinuities (Shevchenko2014).** By the note's arithmetic on the printed formulas, the first and second branches give 0.1466 and 0.118 at K = 1.1, and the second and third give about 0.860 and 0.841 at K = 4.4. Any test using Eq. 26 as a reference curve needs a tolerance of a few hundredths near the joins; Eq. 25 (cuts at 1 and 4.5) is superseded, and its intermediate branch is not recorded in the notes.
2. **Two large-K fits disagree (HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019).** The note's arithmetic gives 3.84 for ln(0.55 + 0.46 K) and 4.26 for 0.925 ln K at K = 100, and the paper states no crossover K. Against Shevchenko2014 at K = 100, ln(K/2) + 1/K^2 is about 3.91 (arithmetic on the printed fit), so the 0.925 ln K fit sits about 0.35 above it. None of the three is validated against the others in the papers.
3. **Unit ambiguity of A and c (HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019).** The text calls A a percentage while Eq. 9 and Fig. 1c read as a fraction (axis to 0.5), so c = 0.16 cannot be applied safely; the same doubt applies to c = 0.89 in Eq. 11. The individual converged exponents of the four K = 0.95 regions are only in Fig. 1b,c and are not tabulated.
4. **Printed ratios do not match (HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019, Sect. IV, p. 10).** From the printed times at K = 6.608, t_r/t_s for orbit B is about 2.8 x 10^3 (printed 3.2 x 10^3) and for orbit C about 2.6 x 10^2 (printed 2.6 x 10^3); only orbit A (8 x 10^3) matches. Check against the original (their reference [16]) before using.
5. **Single-orbit values versus fits at the same K (cross-paper).** By arithmetic on the printed fits: at K = pi/2, SalaArtuso2015 gives 0.298, Shevchenko2014 Eq. 26 gives about 0.343 and ln(0.55 + 0.46 K) from HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019 gives about 0.241, a spread of about 0.1. At K = 2 pi, SalaArtuso2015 gives 1.172 and Shevchenko2014 about 1.170 (close agreement), while the Harsoula ln-fit gives about 1.24 and 0.925 ln K gives about 1.70. The mixed phase space at K = pi/2 means a single orbit may sample one component only, whereas fits may mix components.
6. **Value near K_c (cross-paper).** Shevchenko2014 states L about 0.13 at K = 1 (the fit, not a measurement) and the slope fit gives about 0.13 at K_c, while HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019 reports LCN(K_c) about 0.1 with no precision. They are plausibly consistent but differ by about 30 percent; the Shevchenko slope is a smoothed or upper-envelope description, and the Harsoula value is "approx" without method.
7. **Jacobian-product versus Wolf estimator (Tarnopolski2015).** The paper reports a largest exponent of about 1.92 against about 1.2 in earlier work using the Wolf et al. method, and a factor of about 6 sensitivity of the Wolf estimate to its inputs on x0 = 0. The author attributes the gap "most likely" to the algorithm and does not test it. The K at which 1.92 occurs is not given, and overflow or precision handling of the unrenormalized product is not described.
8. **Where the chaotic sea is complete (cross-paper).** PradoReynoso2022 restricts results to K >= 4 (single chaotic component, citing standard text), HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019 states A is close to 100 percent only above K = 7, and SalaArtuso2015 notes small accelerator-mode islands at K = 2 pi. The K range over which one orbit's value represents the whole chaotic sea is therefore not settled; P1 initial conditions need to be tied to a component.
9. **Missing method details.** SalaArtuso2015: floating-point precision, error bars, second initial conditions, and the "standard techniques" check mentioned once. HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019: renormalization scheme, update rule for the deviation slope, initial slope, precision, and run lengths for K = 0.95. Shevchenko2014: iteration count, tolerances, initial conditions and chaotic-component selection (data come from earlier work, not read here). PradoReynoso2022: trajectory counts, lengths, precision and Ehrenfest times for the classical numerics, and no validation of the one-step integral against a Benettin value. Tarnopolski2015: floating-point precision (Mathematica), a single fixed N = 10^4 justified by a few spot checks, and 189 grid points dropped as indeterminate.
10. **Secondary values to verify against originals.** (a) The large-K law ln(K/2) is cited by Shevchenko2014 as the Chirikov asymptote and used by PradoReynoso2022 as lambda_SM; its original derivation was not read, so it is not a row above. (b) K_c = 0.9716354 (last invariant curve) is quoted in Tarnopolski2015 (citing Greene and MacKay), Shevchenko2014 and HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019; verify against the original work. (c) The earlier value of about 1.2 for the largest exponent (Manchein and Beims 2013, their ref. 18 in Tarnopolski2015) and the data behind Figs. 4 and 5 of Shevchenko2014 (his refs 23 and 24) are quoted, not computed here. (d) The statement that only one chaotic component exists for K > 4 is cited from Lichtenberg and Lieberman by PradoReynoso2022. None of these sources is in the verified bibliography under a key.
11. **Parameter mapping.** PradoReynoso2022, Tarnopolski2015 and HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019 use the unit-torus form with kick (K/2 pi) sin(2 pi x); the identity with the Chirikov K is a reader inference in two of the notes (PradoReynoso2022, HarsoulaKaramanosContopoulos2019) and implied in Tarnopolski2015. SalaArtuso2015 uses the McMillan form with the derived initial point p = -10^-3, x = 10^-3 (reader derivation from Eq. 3). A P1 test must fix one convention and convert initial conditions explicitly.
12. **Papers not usable for the P1 target despite relevance flags.** PradoReynoso2022 (OTOC gap of about 0.3466, not an error in the exponent) and Tarnopolski2015 (Hurst exponent) have a different main subject; their numbers must not be confused with algorithm errors. The Hurst and OTOC papers give no information on L3-L7 or renormalization intervals.
