# Notes: standard-map-stickiness

Stage 04 (targeted: M-SQ1 papers with open-access full text), 2026-10-12. Read (14 keys): AltmannTel2008, ChirikovShepelyansky1999, CristadoroKetzmerick2008, Venegeroles2009, CederAgam2013, AltmannMotterKantz2006, ContopoulosHarsoula2008, AltmannKantz2007, AlusFishmanMeiss2014, daSilvaMancheinBeims2018, DatserisDiakonosSchmelcher2015, MartinsdeOliveiraCiroCaldas2020, Meiss2015, HomanMeiss2026. Included M-SQ1 papers not read because no open-access text was found: Balescu1997, Zaslavsky2004, ZaslavskyEdelman2005, MiguelSimoVieiro2022, ArtusoCavallascaCristadoro2008, AbuddeCarvalho2013. This file merges the per-paper notes only; nothing was added from outside them.

## Per-paper annotations

- **AltmannTel2008**: Shows that Poincare recurrence times and escape times from a leaked system share the same exponential decay rate, set by the conditionally invariant measure of the leak, followed by a power-law tail from sticking near the island. Standard map in unit-torus form with coefficient 0.52 (one central island, square leak outside it); gives gamma, mu_c, mu for one leak size but no tail exponent and no Lyapunov values. Useful to the P1/P2 panel as a model for how long finite-time windows must be before sticky behaviour dominates.
- **ChirikovShepelyansky1999**: Argues and tests that at the golden critical curve of the standard map the recurrence density decays as tau^-3 (correlation tau^-2), but only above about 1e6 iterations, with an effective exponent near 1 before that. Gives exit-time scales tau_n = tau_g q_n for orbits started at unstable fixed points of the q = 1 and q = 2 resonances, so each such orbit carries an expected trapping time. The strongest source for labelling sticky orbits at K_g, but the asymptotic regime lies far beyond ordinary panel run lengths.
- **CristadoroKetzmerick2008**: Random-scaling Markov-tree model and an ensemble of 100 cubic-potential area-preserving maps, conjecturing a universal tail exponent of about 1.57 (cumulative form). It does not run the Chirikov standard map, so it gives no standard-map values; it is relevant only as the warning that single-system finite-time exponents scatter around a common limit.
- **Venegeroles2009**: Analytical letter linking the recurrence-tail exponent gamma and the superdiffusion exponent beta through gamma + beta = 3, conjecturing gamma = beta = 3/2 for trapping at accelerator-mode islands and confirming gamma = 3, beta = 0 at the golden torus. Covers the standard map as the case f = sin; no new simulations, and the standard-map numbers (K = 6.476939, 6.716809) are quoted from other authors. Points the panel at two sticky regimes: K just above 2 pi l (accelerator modes) and K at or just above the golden-torus breakup.
- **CederAgam2013**: Shows within the Meiss-Ott tree model that the approach to the power-law tail is extremely slow and that the local slope fluctuates between samples. Its standard-map experiment is a randomised map with an absorbing line at K = 0.971635406, not the deterministic map, and Fig. 1 states no K. A warning about reading tail exponents from single curves; gives no panel values.
- **AltmannMotterKantz2006**: Shows that sharply divided phase space (piecewise-linear maps, mushroom billiards) gives a cumulative recurrence tail exponent of 2 through marginally unstable periodic orbits, while a hierarchical border gives oscillating, slower decay. No sine standard-map run is shown, so no standard-map values; the sawtooth K values (3/2, 2) belong to piecewise-linear maps and do not transfer.
- **AltmannKantz2007**: Standard map with one visible island (coefficient 0.52 in the unit-torus form) plus weak noise, and coupled standard maps, with three recurrence-time regimes and crossover scalings. Almost all numbers are for noise-perturbed or coupled systems, not the deterministic two-dimensional map, so they give no deterministic benchmark; it does flag K_1 = 1.07 as an accelerator-mode parameter and the island and start locations of its coupled setup.
- **ContopoulosHarsoula2008**: Describes stickiness at K = 5 as organised by the stable and unstable manifolds of the unstable 4/9 orbit around island O1 and of the central orbit, with escape-time maps and finite-time stretching curves. Gives orbit coordinates, eigenvalues and escape-time bins, but no power-law exponents. Provides a concrete K = 5 sticky window and labelling logic (fast-escape versus slow-escape strips); the finite-time Lyapunov units are ambiguous.
- **AlusFishmanMeiss2014**: Statistics of the island-around-island hierarchy, computed for the Henon quadratic map, not the standard map. Finds continued-fraction coefficients that deviate from Gauss-Kuzmin and two apparently universal flux-ratio distributions. It measures no tail exponent and gives no standard-map values; the standard map enters only through an earlier study it recalls.
- **daSilvaMancheinBeims2018**: Standard map in unit-torus form with additive noise at K = 3.28 and K = 4.23. The noiseless runs (D = 0) give cumulative recurrence tail exponents of 1.55 (K = 3.28, in the text) and 1.58 (K = 4.23, figure label only); the noisy results belong to a different system. Also shows stickiness is stronger when the central fixed point is elliptic (K < 4) than hyperbolic, useful for choosing panel K values.
- **DatserisDiakonosSchmelcher2015**: Localized finite-time correlation functions of the standard map for k up to 2, with three dynamically separated chaotic zones for 0.85 < k <= 1.2 and very long zone-to-zone passage times. Gives median passage times at k = 1, a laminar-length exponent 1.85 at k = k_c (in symbolic units), and example intermittent orbits at k = 0.95. It computes no Lyapunov exponent or indicator, so it supplies time-scale labels only.
- **MartinsdeOliveiraCiroCaldas2020**: Explains trapping in the open, area-preserving Henon map through crossings of the invariant manifolds of three saddles; gives two extreme transit times for Henon parameters a = 0.298 and a = 0.338. It studies a different (scattering) map, so it gives no standard-map values, only the qualitative lesson that sticky orbits sit next to a detached resonant island chain.
- **Meiss2015**: Survey of turnstiles, cantori and transport that defines exit, return and transit times and the exponent relations. Its one original computation is a standard-map orbit at k = 0.975 that looks confined for 1e6 steps and escapes after 2.3e9; all other numbers are quoted from cited work and are treated as secondary here.
- **HomanMeiss2026**: Recurrence and stickiness in the noisy Harper map, with fitted power-law and exponential rates. It studies a different map (Harper), so it gives no standard-map values; it offers a recipe (recurrence time plus trapping time inside island boxes) for labelling an orbit as sticky.

## Open questions and conflicts

- Exponent conventions clash across papers. ChirikovShepelyansky1999 writes the recurrence density as P(tau) ~ tau^-p with p = 3 and C ~ tau^-(p-1). Meiss2015 lists gamma = 3 for the same result inside a list of cumulative exponents (P(t) ~ t^-gamma, density t^-(gamma+1)), and Venegeroles2009 also uses gamma = 3 at the golden torus. CristadoroKetzmerick2008 and AltmannMotterKantz2006 use the cumulative form (z = z_I + 1 shift between initial-condition types in the former), AltmannKantz2007 uses the density with gamma_c = gamma - 1, CederAgam2013 uses F(t) ~ t^-gamma_0 with P_1 ~ t^-(1+gamma_0). Whether the 3 of ChirikovShepelyansky1999 is cumulative or density must be checked against the original before it is used as a target.
- daSilvaMancheinBeims2018 uses the symbol gamma for both the cumulative and the density exponent without saying so; if 1.55 and 1.58 are cumulative, the density equivalents differ by one under the stated relation. Check the convention before treating them as test values.
- Pre-asymptotic scatter: ChirikovShepelyansky1999 reports p about 1 below tau_g (and earlier numerics near 1.5), daSilvaMancheinBeims2018 reports 1.55, Meiss2015 and Venegeroles2009 cite about 1.5, while CederAgam2013 labels local slopes from 0.75 to 2.6 on one curve (figure labels only, K not stated). Tail exponents are run-length dependent, so they should not be pass/fail thresholds.
- The asymptotic exponent is disputed between models: 3 (ChirikovShepelyansky1999), 1.96 and 2.25 and 3.05 (tree and island models as listed in Meiss2015), 2 for sharp borders (AltmannMotterKantz2006), 1.57 as a conjectured universal value (CristadoroKetzmerick2008, ensemble of cubic maps, not the standard map). Meiss2015 and AlusFishmanMeiss2014 both state that it is unsettled whether the decay is a true power law and whether gamma is universal.
- CederAgam2013: the tree model predicts fluctuations of the local exponent decaying with epsilon = 0.174, but the randomised standard-map ensemble shows epsilon about 0. The K = 0.971635406 used there is unexplained in the paper and the map is noisy with an absorbing line, so it is not a deterministic benchmark.
- ChirikovShepelyansky1999 gives two estimates of the exit-time scale, tau_n about 1.4e5 q_n (summing transitions) and tau_g = 2.11e5 (Eq. 3). The note does not say which is used where; the K > K_g result p = 4/3 rests on a garbled formula.
- ContopoulosHarsoula2008 internal inconsistencies: the eigenvalue of the 4/9 orbit is 13.298 (p.8) versus 13.6 (p.11); the Fig. 9 caption point count (500000) does not match 20000 points over 15 iterates; the Fig. 11 caption disagrees with the p.12 text and cites a nonexistent "Fig.13a"; equation label (3) is used twice.
- ContopoulosHarsoula2008 finite-time units: the values 2.588, 13.298 and the sea limit about 17.0 appear tied to the 18-iteration return to P1, and the meaning of t (map steps or 18-step blocks) is not stated, so the per-step sea value is not given. The reader's inference that 17.0 is per 18 steps is unverified.
- DatserisDiakonosSchmelcher2015: the plateau value tau(k) about 650 is described ambiguously and was not recorded as a benchmark; the passage "times" are medians; alpha = 1.85 is in symbolic units of 4 to 7 iterations, so it is not directly comparable with iteration-count exponents.
- Origin not stated in the notes (so not recorded as benchmarks, to be checked in the originals): the destruction of the second-last spanning curve at k about 0.9164 and the three-zone range 0.85 < k <= 1.2 (DatserisDiakonosSchmelcher2015, p.7); K about 4.35, K = 4.8765 and K > 0.97 (ContopoulosHarsoula2008, p.3).
- Secondary values to verify against their originals before any use: K_g = 0.97163540631 (printed by ChirikovShepelyansky1999 and Venegeroles2009, attributed to Greene 1979 by Meiss2015 and DatserisDiakonosSchmelcher2015; Greene is not in the verified .bib); the crossing-time divergence at k about 0.989 (Chirikov 1979) and exponent 3.05 (cited as MMP84b) in Meiss2015; the model exponents 1.96, 2.25, 2, 1.57 and the ratios w_1 = 0.053112 and w_2 = 0.014158 in Meiss2015; D about 0.1 (K - K_c)^3 (Rechester et al.) and the sensitivity requirement of Weiss et al. in Venegeroles2009; the standard-map exponents at K = 6.716809 (gamma 1.8, beta 1.2) and K = 6.476939 (gamma 1.6, beta 1.4) from Zumofen and Klafter, quoted in Venegeroles2009; the Meiss-Ott rate parameters behind gamma_0 = 1.96424 in CederAgam2013; gamma about 1.60 for the K = 2.6 map, taken by daSilvaMancheinBeims2018 from its own reference list; the Greene-MacKay-Stark continued-fraction bound recalled in AlusFishmanMeiss2014.
- Venegeroles2009 internal inconsistencies: Table I lists 1.44 for the quadratic and whisker maps while the text says they agree with 3/2; the wind-tree entry is beta in the table but gamma in the text. The paper also argues that K = 6.716809 fits the universal law worse because it is farther from 2 pi.
- AlusFishmanMeiss2014 reports inner continued-fraction coefficients up to 48 for the Henon map, against a bound of 5 conjectured by Greene et al. for the standard map; whether this contradiction carries over to the standard map is untested there.
- Parameter-convention issues. The unit-torus forms (ChirikovShepelyansky1999, ContopoulosHarsoula2008, daSilvaMancheinBeims2018, Meiss2015) carry K/(2 pi) in front of sin(2 pi x), and the equivalence to the project's p' = p + K sin x with the same K is stated only by the note readers, not by the papers (Meiss2015 also has the opposite kick sign). AltmannTel2008 and AltmannKantz2007 put the coefficient directly on sin(2 pi x), so the Chirikov K would be 2 pi times it (arithmetic from the AltmannKantz2007 note: 0.52 gives about 3.27 and 1.07 gives about 6.72); K is not printed in Chirikov form in AltmannTel2008. An unverified observation: 3.27 is close to the K = 3.28 of daSilvaMancheinBeims2018, and 6.72 is close to K = 6.716809 cited by Venegeroles2009, which may indicate the same island regimes. Venegeroles2009 (Eq. 5) and DatserisDiakonosSchmelcher2015 use the Chirikov form directly with the stated K or k; the Harper-map paper HomanMeiss2026 contrasts a 2 pi-scaled form (background only).
- AltmannTel2008: the Fig. 4 geometry (eps = 0.2) differs from the eps = 0.1 behind gamma, mu_c and mu; the initial conditions and precision of the recurrence orbit are not stated; the paper's printed inequality for the Henon leak (mu_c = 0.0535 < mu = 0.0377) is arithmetically wrong, so the Henon mu(I) should not be reused.
- daSilvaMancheinBeims2018: the enhanced-trapping noise range differs between Sec. IV (D from 1e-5 to 1e-3) and the Conclusions (1e-5 to 1e-4); the recurrence region is not given in coordinates; the chaotic-sea Lyapunov exponent is not printed as a number (colour scale only), so no Lyapunov target can be taken from it.
- Meiss2015: the Fig. 1 orbit is said to start near the hyperbolic fixed point (0.5, 0.0), which is itself a fixed point, so the offset matters and is not stated; an escape at 2.3e9 iterations lies far beyond feasible panel run lengths, so such an orbit will look regular for a whole run.
- HomanMeiss2026 internal inconsistency in the Harper-map survivor counts (968 attributed to sigma = 1e-2 in the text but labelled sigma^2 = 1e-4 in Fig. 10(d)); the paper's numbers are for the Harper map and are not used here.
- Papers read but yielding no standard-map benchmark rows (different systems or no deterministic standard-map value): CristadoroKetzmerick2008, CederAgam2013, AltmannMotterKantz2006, AltmannKantz2007, AlusFishmanMeiss2014, MartinsdeOliveiraCiroCaldas2020, HomanMeiss2026. None of the 14 papers computes a Lyapunov exponent or chaos indicator for a standard-map sticky orbit, so there is no direct target for L1 or I2-I10 in this set (ContopoulosHarsoula2008 gives finite-time stretching only in ambiguous units).
