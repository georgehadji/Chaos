## Scope

- Catalog row L8 (`_config/catalogs/lyapunov.md`, section B): the continuous QR method for Hamiltonian flows: an ordinary differential equation for the orthonormal factor Q of the deviation matrix on the orthogonal group (or Stiefel manifold for k < d) and a differential equation for ln R_ii, integrated together with the orbit; full spectrum primary (k = d), the k largest exponents (1 <= k < d) as the same method with the form of Skokos2010 Eq. (92). Row L10 (continuous SVD) stays catalog only: no equation of it is written here.
- Prerequisites (catalog and commit status read 2026-11-18, the stage 04 reports not re-read): L1 Benettin, L4/L5 discrete QR (Householder and Gram-Schmidt spectra) `verified`; F1 oscillator, F3 Henon-Heiles, F4 pendulum `verified`; the integrator-catalog rows (fixed-step symplectic splitting methods) and henon-heiles `verified`. The module is not a reducer of the tangent stream (the stream carries W by the tangent of the one-step map, which this method does not use); it runs its own per-orbit loop, as the two-trajectory estimator does (P11).
- In scope: the equations C4 to C9 of the source and their derived consequences (C10 to C21); the choice of the scheme that advances Q (P2 to P4); the log accumulation by the integral of (Q^T A Q)_ii (Skokos2010 Eq. (87)); the orthogonality-defect dynamics and the penalty term (C13, C14); the cost model against the discrete QR (C21); closed forms and identities that need no variational reference (C17 to C19).
- Out of scope: continuous SVD (L10) and the Rangarajan angle parametrisation (Skokos2010 p.49; bibliography key Rangarajan1998 not read, no equation used); the strongly skew reformulation cited at Skokos2010 p.53 (Bridges and Reich 2001, not in the bibliography); computing or storing the triangular factor R beyond its log diagonal (clv-ginelli, P12); adaptive step or adaptive Q-step control; extended phase space integrators (no flow direction, P11 of the ofli spec); maps (the method needs a vector field, Skokos2010 p.48: "such methods can only be formulated for continuous dynamical systems").
- No catalog row is added. The linear flows of the tests (constant Hamiltonian matrices, the inverted oscillator) are suite fixtures, not systems.

### Findings for the owner

- F1. Sources. The four primary sources of row L8 (Goldhirsch1987, Dieci1997, ChristiansenRugh1997, Geist1990) are not on disk (checked with find over `data/literature/`, which holds only the directories fli-ofli, gali, megno, naff-fma, recurrence-indicators, rli, sali, unassigned, weighted-birkhoff); no content is cited from them. The only text read is the review Skokos2010 Sect. 6.4 (printed pp.48 to 53), which prints the equations (84) to (92) and attributes the method to Goldhirsch et al. (p.48), the weak skew-symmetry observation to Dieci and Van Vleck 1995 (p.53, bibliography key DieciVanVleck1995, not on disk) and the stabilising dissipation term to Christiansen and Rugh (p.53, "an appropriate dissipation term", not printed). Every equation below is the review's, as reported, or derived here. The catalog and the benchmarks file record Geist1990 Table III p.886 as "read"; that text is not on disk and was not re-read.
- F2. The form of the stabilising term is not available. The catalog writes "a nu (Q^T Q - I) correction". The review prints no formula. The form used here, -nu Q (Q^T Q - I), is derived and analysed (C13): the orthogonality defect obeys a closed equation, the penalty contracts it, and the condition on nu follows. The catalog's "projection" and "Cayley" variants are in no read text; they appear only as alternatives (P4).
- F3. The two forms are not equivalent off the orthogonal manifold. Eq. (84) (k = d) makes the defect E = Q^T Q - I obey dE/dt = E S - S E exactly (derived, C13): the Frobenius norm of E is conserved by the exact differential equation, so errors do not grow, and any nu > 0 makes E decay. Eq. (92) (k < d, and k = d if used) gives dE/dt = -(E U + U^T E), which can grow at rate up to 2 ||U||; nu >= ||U|| is then needed. This matches the review's remark (p.53) that usual integrators fail to preserve Q^T Q = I for p < 2N. Recommendation P1: Eq. (84) for k = d, Eq. (92) for k < d.
- F4. Coupling to the symplectic integrators is not what the catalog implies. The splitting integrator carries the tangent as the exact derivative of its one-step map (integrator-catalog Equations item 17), which is the discrete QR. The continuous method needs the Jacobian A(t) of the vector field along the orbit and an ordinary differential equation scheme for Q. A Q-update that is exact on each elementary piece of the splitting (drift, kick) is derived to be a QR factorization of the elementary Jacobian times Q (each piece has a constant generator, C4 integrates exactly to the QR of the matrix exponential), so it is the discrete QR at every substep, not a continuous method (derived, P2). Recommendation P2: keep the orbit of the delivered integrator and advance Q by an explicit Runge-Kutta scheme whose nodes are orbit points: order 2 on one step, order 4 on two steps, equal cost per orbit step (2 evaluations of the right-hand side per step in both).
- F5. Same-orbit differences with the discrete QR are discretization differences, not rounding. The discrete QR uses the exact tangent of the numerical map; this method uses the exact field Jacobian at the numerical orbit points and its own Q-scheme error O(Delta^q). The pathwise bound of L1 S3 does not apply; the allowance is c h^m with m = min(p, q) (C20), a proposal; stage 04 reports the realized ratios. Consequence: with the default order-8 orbit table the exponent error is set by the Q-scheme, so the order-4 scheme is the default and the order-2 scheme is for tests (estimate at h = 1/20: the order-2 error in ln R is of order 0.1 over t = 60 for a chaotic orbit, hence too large for studies).
- F6. Cost. The benchmarks file records the relative CPU time of the continuous QR as 40 (Geist1990 Table III p.886, Toda chain d = 29, GS = 1; "up to a factor 40", reproduction target, never an oracle; the machine and the step control are 1990). A count on the equations here (C21, estimate) gives about 2 to 5 times the cost of the discrete QR on the same orbit at the same step, because both are O(d k^2) per step and no iteration is needed. The factor 40 is not expected to reproduce; the measured ratio is reported (R1), not scored.
- F7. Degenerate and zero exponents are no obstacle. The right-hand sides (85), (86), (92) are polynomial in Q and A: there is no division by a difference of exponents (derived by inspection), unlike the continuous SVD (catalog L10 note). A Hamiltonian flow has pairs of zero exponents on every torus (Skokos2010 Eqs. (47), (48) p.28 and the following text): the exponents converge as ln t / t (Skokos2010 Eq. (62) p.35) and the frame inside a degenerate cluster does not converge, which affects no exponent. Closed forms with exactly degenerate exponents exist and are tested (criteria 2, 3).
- F8. Two exact identities need no variational reference and no external package: the flow direction gives ln R_11 = ln(||f(z_t)|| / ||f(z_0)||) for the frame whose first column is f(z_0)/||f(z_0)|| (C17, from ofli O4 (i)), and the energy covector gives ln R_11 = ln |grad H(z_0) . q_1(0)| - ln |grad H(z_t) . q_1(t)| for any frame (C18, from ofli O4 (ii)); the second ties the log accumulation to the Q column. Both are derived here and used as criteria 7 and 8.
- F9. No external continuous-QR oracle exists. ChaosTools 3.5.4 in `verify/Manifest.toml` has only discrete QR (its `lyapunovspectrum` is method "H2" of Geist1990 with a step Delta t; installed source read 2026-11-18); TaylorIntegration 0.18.19 `lyap_taylorinteg` is a Gram-Schmidt per Taylor step. The oracles are closed forms, the identities of F8, the delivered discrete spectrum on the identical orbit, and a BigFloat variational reference (Oracle).

## Equations

Notation. Flow dz/dt = f(z), f = J grad H, z in R^d (J canonical, J^T = -J, J orthogonal). Orbit z(t). Jacobian of the field A(t) = Df(z(t)) = J D^2H(z(t)) (Skokos2010 Eq. (8) p.8; note that Skokos names it A, the catalog L8 row writes J, which is the canonical matrix everywhere else). For a separable flow H = |p|^2/2 + V(q), A = [[0, I], [-Hess V(q), 0]], so A depends on q only. Y(t) the fundamental matrix, Y' = A Y (Eq. (10) p.9). A frame is a d x k matrix with orthonormal columns; Y_k(t) = Y(t) Q_0 (k columns, Eq. (90) p.52). U = Q^T A Q (k x k), L the strictly lower triangle of U, S = L - L^T, E = Q^T Q - I, u_i = U_ii.

C1. Variational equation, fundamental matrix: w' = A w, w(t) = Y(t) w(0), Y' = A Y, Y(0) = I. Skokos2010 Eqs. (8), (9), (10), pp.8 to 9.

C2. QR factorization: a matrix with linearly independent columns is uniquely Q R, Q^T Q = I_k, R upper triangular with positive diagonal. Skokos2010 Theorem 4 p.46. For k < d, Q^T Q = I holds but not Q Q^T = I (p.52).

C3. Exponents: chi_i = lim (1/t) ln R_ii(t), R from Y_k(t) = Q(t) R(t). Skokos2010 p.51 (the display after Eq. (86)); discrete form Eq. (82) p.47. The finite-time estimate is lambda_i(t) = l_i(t) / t with l_i = ln R_ii (per unit time; GS G6 gives the per step form).

C4. Differentiation of Y_k = Q R: Q^T Q' + R' R^-1 = Q^T A Q (p.51, display above Eq. (84), for k = d) and R' R^-1 = Q^T A Q - S for k < d (p.52, where S = Q^T Q' is a k x k matrix). Q^T Q' is skew (Q^T Q = I) and R' R^-1 is upper triangular, so S is fixed by the strictly lower triangle of U.

C5. Complete spectrum (k = d): Q' = Q S (Eq. (84) p.51), S skew with S_ij = (Q^T A Q)_ij for i > j, S_ii = 0, S_ij = -(Q^T A Q)_ji for i < j (Eq. (85) p.51).

C6. Growth of the diagonal: R'_pp / R_pp = (Q^T A Q)_pp (Eq. (86) p.51), that is d(ln R_pp)/dt = q_p . A q_p = u_p (Eq. (87) p.51), q_p the p-th column of Q. R itself is not needed (p.51).

C7. Time average: chi_p = lim (1/t) integral_0^t q_p . A q_p dt (p.51). Running mean over intervals of length tau: X_p((k+1) tau) = (k/(k+1)) X_p(k tau) + (1/((k+1) tau)) integral_{k tau}^{(k+1) tau} q_p . A q_p dt, chi_p = lim X_p(k tau) (Eqs. (88), (89) p.52). Here tau is the interval of the source; it is not the handoff interval of L1 S1.

C8. First k < d columns: Y' = A Y with Y(0)^T Y(0) = I_k (Eq. (90) p.52), R R^-1 relation above, Q' = A Q - Q R' R^-1 (Eq. (91) p.52), and Q' = H(Q, t) Q with H(Q, t) = A - Q Q^T A + Q S Q^T (Eq. (92) p.53); for k = d it reduces to Eq. (84) (p.53). The exponents again follow from Eqs. (86) to (89) (p.53).

C9. Weak skew-symmetry, reported: Q^T (H + H^T) Q = 0 whenever Q^T Q = I_k (p.53, a "central observation" of Dieci and Van Vleck 1995, key DieciVanVleck1995, not on disk). Reported: H is not skew-symmetric for k < d, the term Q Q^T is responsible, and usual integrators fail to preserve the orthogonality of Q there (p.53); Christiansen and Rugh stabilise by a dissipation term (p.53, ref. 26 of the review, key ChristiansenRugh1997, not on disk, form not printed).

C10. Single expression of the right-hand side, derived. With U = Q^T A Q, Eq. (92) applied to Q reads H Q = A Q - Q U + Q S (Q^T Q) (use Q^T Q = I + E; H Q = A Q - Q Q^T A Q + Q S Q^T Q). The two terms A Q - Q U = (I - Q Q^T) A Q are the part of A Q outside the span of Q (zero on the manifold when k = d), Q S the rotation inside the span.

C11. One column (k = 1), derived: S = 0 (1 x 1 skew), Q' = q' = A q - (q . A q) q and l' = q . A q. This is the projective equation of the unit vector w / ||w|| and its log growth, so l_1(t) = ln ||Y(t) q(0)|| (the volume identity C16 with one column); the Benettin estimator is its discrete form (L1 E3).

C12. Right-hand side with penalty nu >= 0 (derived; the form of the penalty is the proposal of F2): form (84): G_84(Q; A, nu) = Q S - nu Q E (k = d); form (92): G_92(Q; A, nu) = A Q - Q U + Q S (I + E) - nu Q E (any k). On the manifold E = 0 both reduce to C5 and C8: the penalty does not change the exact solution, only the drift off the manifold. Augmented system: Q' = G, l_i' = u_i (C6).

C13. Orthogonality defect dynamics, derived (S is skew for every Q by construction, E symmetric). Form (84): E' = E S - S E - 2 nu E (I + E); then d||E||_F^2/dt = -4 nu (||E||_F^2 + tr E^3) <= -4 nu (1 - ||E||_2) ||E||_F^2, because tr(E [E, S]) = 0: the commutator is norm-neutral, so without penalty ||E||_F is conserved and with any nu > 0 it decays at rate at least 2 nu (1 - ||E||_2). Form (92): Q^T H Q = -E U + S + E S + S E + E S E, hence Q^T (H + H^T) Q = -(E U + U^T E) (the terms in S cancel pairwise because S^T = -S) and E' = -(E U + U^T E) - 2 nu E (I + E); then d||E||_F/dt <= -2 [nu (1 - ||E||_2) - ||U||_2] ||E||_F, with ||U||_2 <= (1 + ||E||_2) ||A||_2 (the nuclear norm of E^2 is ||E||_F^2, so |tr(E^2 U)| <= ||U||_2 ||E||_F^2). Hence form (92) contracts the defect when nu (1 - ||E||_2) > (1 + ||E||_2) ||A||_2; at nu = 0 it can grow at rate 2 ||U||_2, the instability reported at p.53 (C9). The first-order statement E' = -(E U + U^T E) - 2 nu E is the weak skew-symmetry C9 with the penalty.

C14. Penalty rule and stability limits, derived. nu(t) = c_nu ||A(z(t))||_F with c_nu >= 1 (k = d, form (84): any nu > 0 suffices, c_nu = 1; k < d, form (92): c_nu = 2 gives rate at least 2 ||A||_F (1 - 3 ||E||_2) since ||A||_2 <= ||A||_F). For the Henon-Heiles flow A Q has ||A||_F^2 = 4 + 8 |q|^2 (derived from Hess V = I + 2 [[q2, q1], [q1, -q2]], whose eigenvalues are 1 +- 2 |q|), at most 12 inside the region |q| < 1 that an orbit with E < 1/6 never leaves (henon-heiles Equation 6), so nu <= 2 sqrt 12 = 6.9. The linear part of the explicit scheme must be stable: nu Delta <= 1 for the order-2 scheme and nu Delta <= 1.39 for the order-4 scheme (the real-axis stability limits 2 and 2.785 of the two Runge-Kutta schemes applied to E' = -2 nu E; standard, derived here, not cited, stage 02 checks them numerically). At h = 1/20 and c_nu = 2 these hold (nu Delta <= 0.69). Effect on the estimate: u_i = (Q^T A Q)_ii differs from the manifold value by O(||E||), so the exponent bias is O(||E||_equilibrium ||A||) with ||E||_equilibrium of the order (defect rate) / (2 nu) and defect rate O(Delta^q ||A||^(q+1)) (estimate, measured in R2).

C15. Sum identity, derived. For k = d, sum_i u_i = tr(Q^T A Q) = tr(A Q Q^T) = tr A + tr(A (Q Q^T - I)), tr A = tr(J D^2H) = 0 (J skew, D^2H symmetric: Skokos2010 Eq. (47) p.28, volume preservation), and Q Q^T has the spectrum of Q^T Q, so ||Q Q^T - I||_F = ||E||_F and |sum_i u_i| <= ||A||_F ||E||_F. In the scheme the stage values of Q are not orthonormal (a stage Q + Delta Q S has E = Delta^2 S^T S), so the computed sum of the l_i is a pure discretization error of order Delta^q per unit time (criterion 12), not an exact zero.

C16. Volume identity, derived (GS G7 for maps, the same proof for flows). Y(t) Q_0 = Q(t) R(t) with R upper triangular with positive diagonal and Y(t) Q_0 the exact solution of C1 (uniqueness, C2), so sum_{i<=m} l_i(t) = ln vol_m(Y(t) Q_{0,m}) = (1/2) ln det(X^T Y^T Y X), X the first m columns of Q_0, m = 1..k; l_1(t) = ln ||Y(t) q_1(0)||. Column i of the result depends only on the first i columns of Q_0; the series does not depend on the integration method in exact arithmetic.

C17. Flow-direction identity, derived (ofli O4 (i): Y(t) f(z_0) = f(z(t)), from differentiating Phi_t Phi_s = Phi_s Phi_t in s at s = 0). For a frame with q_1(0) = f(z_0) / ||f(z_0)||: l_1(t) = ln (||f(z(t))|| / ||f(z_0)||) by C16, bounded on a bounded orbit away from equilibria. For the numerical series the identity holds up to the defect of the numerical orbit against the flow (order h^p, ofli O10) plus the Q-scheme error O(Delta^q).

C18. Energy covector identity, derived (ofli O4 (ii): grad H(z(t))^T Y(t) = grad H(z_0)^T). With Y Q_0 = Q R: grad H(z(t))^T Q(t) = (grad H(z_0)^T Q_0) R^-1, whose first component is grad H(z(t)) . q_1(t) = (grad H(z_0) . q_1(0)) / R_11, so l_1(t) = ln |grad H(z_0) . q_1(0)| - ln |grad H(z(t)) . q_1(t)|. It holds for every frame with grad H(z_0) . q_1(0) not zero. In floating point the right-hand side loses relative accuracy once |grad H(z(t)) . q_1(t)| / ||grad H(z(t))|| drops to about 1e3 eps (the unstable direction becomes tangent to the energy surface, l_1 about 30 for Float64), which sets the horizon T_valid of criterion 8.

C19. Closed forms of fixtures, derived (constant A, so the orbit does not enter). (a) A upper triangular (including diagonal): U = Q^T A Q has zero strict lower triangle at Q = I, so S = 0, G(I) = 0 and Q = I for all t exactly; l_i = A_ii t. Hamiltonian examples: H = a q p + b p^2/2 (A = [[a, b], [0, -a]]), and diag(l1, l2, -l2, -l1) as a sum of two such systems. (b) A = diag(lam, -lam), Q_0 = rotation by theta (c = cos theta, s = sin theta, 0 < theta < pi/2): Q(t) = rotation by phi(t), phi' = -lam sin 2 phi (from S_21 = U_21 = -lam sin 2 phi), tan phi = e^(-2 lam t) tan theta, and l_1(t) = lam t + (1/2) ln (c^2 + s^2 e^(-4 lam t)), l_2 = -l_1 (checked by differentiating: l_1' = u_1 = lam cos 2 phi). (c) Inverted oscillator H = p^2/2 - lam^2 q^2/2 (a separable flow with constant A = [[0, 1], [lam^2, 0]]): Y(t) = [[cosh lam t, sinh(lam t)/lam], [lam sinh lam t, cosh lam t]], and C16 gives l_1 for any frame. Oscillator F1 with frequencies omega (A = [[0, I], [-Omega^2, 0]]): Y from the exact flow matrix of the integrator-catalog spec. (d) A skew (F1 with all omega = 1, A = [[0, I], [-I, 0]]): u_i = q_i^T A q_i = 0 for every Q, so l_i = 0 identically, and for k = d the exact solution is Q(t) = exp(A t) Q_0 (S = U, Q Q^T = I). (e) Coupled hyperbolic: A = T^-1 A_0 T with A_0 a direct sum of inverted oscillators with lam = (lam_1, lam_2) and T = [[I, B], [0, I]], B symmetric (T symplectic, so A Hamiltonian): Y = T^-1 exp(A_0 t) T, exponents (lam_1, lam_2, -lam_2, -lam_1) in the limit, finite-time values from C16; for lam_1 = lam_2 the exponents are degenerate pairs. The standard statement that the exponents of dz/dt = A z are the real parts of the eigenvalues of A is not used as an oracle: C16 against the exact Y is the oracle.

C20. Discrete versus continuous on the identical orbit, derived estimate. The discrete QR is the exact QR of the tangent of the numerical map; the continuous one integrates C12 with the exact field Jacobian at the numerical orbit points. Their linear systems differ by O(h^p) (ofli O10: the defect delta_k of the numerical map against the flow is O(h^(p+1)) per step) and the Q-scheme adds O(Delta^q), so over a fixed horizon the difference of the exponents is O(h^m), m = min(p, q), with a constant of the order of the sensitivity of the finite-time exponent to the Jacobian (not bounded in this spec; allowance c h^m with c a proposal).

C21. Cost counts, derived. Per orbit step: one Jacobian of the field (at the new orbit point; reused as the first node of the next Q-step), and 2 evaluations of the right-hand side (order-2 scheme: 2 per step; order-4 scheme: 4 per two steps). One right-hand side evaluation costs one product A Q (O(N^2 k) when A is the separable block form, O(d^2 k) in general), U = Q^T (A Q) and Q S and E = Q^T Q (each O(d k^2)). The discrete QR with tau = 1 costs one tangent step (O(stage count x N^2 k) for the splitting) plus one factorization (about 2 d k^2 for Gram-Schmidt, twice that for repeated Gram-Schmidt). Estimate of the ratio at d = k = 4: 2 to 5 (F6). Memory per orbit (Float64, order-4 scheme, k = d = 4, separable F3, N = 2), proposal: sizeof(T) (d + 4 d k + 2 k + 3 N^2) + 16 = 8 (4 + 64 + 8 + 12) + 16 = 720 bytes (state, four k-sized stage buffers, the compensated sums l_i and their error terms, the Jacobian at three nodes, the integrator state and a counter), against 240 for the tangent core (8 (4 + 16 + 8) + 16) plus reducer state.

## Algorithm

Per orbit (ensemble member, orbit id j, run seed s), flow f, orbit integrator of order p with step h (a table of the integrator catalog; the same one-step map every other estimator uses), scheme order q in {2, 4} (Delta = (q/2) h), form 84 or 92 (k = d or k < d, P1), penalty factor c_nu (default per C14, proposal), orthogonality tolerance E_max (proposal 1e-3, P5), horizon N steps, N a positive multiple of q/2:
1. Initialisation. A seeded orthonormal d x k frame Q_0 (the frame of the tangent stream, orthonormal to 8 k eps), compensated sums l_i = 0 with error terms, z_0 from the caller; A_0 = Df(z_0); health check of z_0 and A_0 (finite). E_0 from the frame (diagnostics D4: largest absolute entry of Q^T Q - I).
2. One Q-step (order 2: one orbit step; order 4: two). Advance the orbit by the integrator (one or two steps), evaluate the Jacobian at each new orbit point (one per point, cached for the next Q-step). Order 2 (Heun, nodes A_n, A_(n+1), step h): K1 = G(Q; A_n); Q* = Q + h K1; K2 = G(Q*; A_(n+1)); Q+ = Q + (h/2)(K1 + K2); the increment of l_i is (h/2)(u_i(Q; A_n) + u_i(Q*; A_(n+1))). Order 4 (classical Runge-Kutta, step Delta = 2h, nodes A_n, A_(n+1), A_(n+1), A_(n+2)): K1 = G(Q; A_n), K2 = G(Q + h K1; A_(n+1)), K3 = G(Q + h K2; A_(n+1)), K4 = G(Q + Delta K3; A_(n+2)); Q+ = Q + (Delta/6)(K1 + 2 K2 + 2 K3 + K4); the increment of l_i is (Delta/6)(u_i^1 + 2 u_i^2 + 2 u_i^3 + u_i^4), the u_i evaluated at the stage matrices. nu at each stage is c_nu ||A||_F at the stage's node. The augmented system (Q, l) is integrated with one scheme, so the weights of the two parts agree.
3. Accumulate each increment into l_i with compensated summation (the rule of the tangent-core spec, "Accumulated logarithms"). Increments are of either sign (u_i is a growth rate, not a norm); there is no logarithm and no overflow.
4. Orthogonality guard: E = largest absolute entry of Q^T Q - I after the Q-step; if E is not finite or exceeds E_max, a numerical health error of kind "orthogonality" (the diagnostics name list), with orbit id and step, nothing stored (GPU rule: recorded in the state, raised on the host).
5. Emit lambda_i(t) = l_i(t) / t at the caller's schedule (multiples of Delta), and at the end l_i with compensation terms, the final Q, E_max seen, the orbit step count, h, p, q, the form, c_nu, k, the flow identifier. Replacement of Q never happens (no renormalisation): Q is a state of the integration, not a frame to restart.
6. The orbit never depends on the estimator: the orbit points are bitwise equal to those of the integrator alone and of every other estimator on the same flow, step and start (L1 S2). The initial frame, seed and orbit id conventions are those of the tangent stream.
Parameters recorded per run: h, the integrator table and its order p, q, the form, c_nu, E_max, element type, k, seed, backend, flow identifier (the flow the module holds is the one the integrator advances: P11).

## Invariants

- I1. Exact solution on the manifold: Q^T Q = I (E = 0) for the exact solution of C12; the scheme keeps E at an equilibrium of order (defect rate) / (2 nu) (C13, C14), recorded as E_max seen and bounded by the health tolerance.
- I2. Sum (k = d): sum_i l_i(t) = 0 for the exact solution (C15); the computed sum is a discretization error of order Delta^q per unit time, bounded in criterion 12.
- I3. Volume and frame independence in exact arithmetic (C16): sum_{i<=m} l_i is the log volume of Y Q_{0,m}; the l_i of column i depends only on the first i columns of Q_0; the same for any k: the first k' < k columns of a k-column run equal a k'-column run (to the scheme error, not bitwise: E and the penalty differ).
- I4. Fixed point: Q = I is an exact solution of the scheme for A upper triangular with Q_0 = I (C19 (a)); A skew gives l_i = 0 identically (C19 (d)).
- I5. Flow direction and energy identities (C17, C18) hold for the exact solution; for the series up to the error of C20.
- I6. Orbit unchanged by the module and by k (L1 S2); orbit independence and execution invariance: orbit j's series depends only on (flow, parameters, initial point, seed, j, h, table, q, c_nu, N, T, frame), not on threads, chunk or position in the ensemble; across backends only to criterion 22; the hot loop allocates nothing after warm-up (BigFloat excepted).
- I7. Exponents are per unit time and independent of the unit of the step: the series at t = n h does not change if the same orbit is sampled with a Q-step that divides n h (up to the scheme error).
- I8. Index order is column order, never re-sorted (GS G6).

## Compute

Policy: `_config/architecture.md`, "Compute" (D17, D25). Per orbit, data parallel: the orbit step, the Jacobian, the stage evaluations and the compensated sums fuse into one per-orbit loop (the module is not a reducer of the tangent stream, so it has its own kernel as the two-trajectory estimator has). All work is small dense products on k x k and d x k matrices (static sizes for d <= 8): no factorization, no LAPACK, so no "reducer is CPU-only" fallback reason. GPU candidate for Float64; Double64 does not compile for the device (risk K20): fallback reason "kernel does not compile for the element type"; BigFloat is CPU-only with the same reason. The local device is the RTX 5060 Laptop GPU (`_config/environment.md`, "Local GPU"; risk K15 closed 2026-10-17); `auto` follows the per-method record of `data/gpu-benchmark.toml` (D29, D54): at present all Float64 kernel methods resolve to the CPU (risk K19), so the module ships with the GPU path tested (criterion 22) and the CPU default unless its own record says `gpu_faster`. Memory per orbit: C21 (about 720 bytes, Float64, k = d = 4, order-4 scheme), proposed, added to the stream formula; chunk size from free memory (policy); BLAS threads set to 1 under Julia threads (policy). CPU-vs-GPU: in the form of sali Amendment D68 and ofli Amendment D119 from the start: each backend against its own reference at its realized orbit points, the gap only where both orbits agree within 1e-9 (regular class), with the state-gate term added (criterion 22).

## Assumptions

- A1. The order-2 scheme (Heun with nodes on two orbit points) is second order and the classical four-stage scheme on two orbit steps is fourth order for the nonautonomous system Q' = G(Q; A(t)), with A(t) known at the nodes; the orders are standard Runge-Kutta orders, not cited, verified by criterion 4. A(t) between the nodes is not needed because the stage nodes coincide with orbit points.
- A2. The orbit integrator is the delivered symplectic splitting method of order p and its orbit approximates the flow to O(h^p) over the horizons used (ofli O10, O11 under its A1); the global orbit error grows with time for chaotic orbits, hence the pointwise comparisons are limited to t = 60 and the longer ones are pathwise on the same orbit points.
- A3. The orbit stays in a bounded region where ||A||_F is bounded (F3: E < 1/6, |q| < 1, C14); a flow that escapes (E > 1/6) is outside the scope of the criteria; the module itself has no escape rule.

## Inconsistencies

(a) The catalog row L8 writes the diagonal growth as "(Q^T J Q)_ii" with J for the Jacobian; Skokos2010 Eq. (86) writes A for the Jacobian, and J is the canonical matrix in the rest of the repository (flow notation, ofli spec). The row also writes only "dQ/dt = Q S", the complete-spectrum form (84); the k < d form (92) is needed for part of the spectrum and has a different orthogonality behaviour (F3). Stage 05 wording (P15).
(b) The catalog row lists "projection, Cayley or a nu (Q^T Q - I) correction" as the orthogonality remedies. Only the dissipation term of Christiansen and Rugh is mentioned in the read text (Skokos2010 p.53), without a formula; projection and Cayley are in no read text.
(c) The bibliography file `lyapunov.md` says "Full text on disk" for DieciVanVleck2008 (author preprint), DieciElia2006 and Stewart1997; `data/literature/` holds no file for any of them (only the nine topic directories listed in F1; find for dieci, vleck, stewart finds nothing). The earlier Lyapunov specs cite `data/literature/lyapunov-algorithms/Skokos2010.txt`; the directory does not exist and the same text is `data/literature/sali/arxiv_0811.0882.txt`, which this spec cites. The earlier specs' Skokos page numbers (for example Eqs. (47), (48) at p.28) agree with the printed pages used here.
(d) `_config/architecture.md`, "Layering", lists Integrators below Tangent below Lyapunov. In the code, the Lyapunov module imports Foundation, Systems and Tangent only, and the integrators reach it as a map built in Integrators; no estimator imports Integrators. This module needs the orbit step (Integrators) and the field Jacobian (Systems): Lyapunov importing Integrators is allowed by the documented layering and is a new edge in the code (P11); the Tangent module is not needed.
(e) Two meanings of tau (integrator-catalog step size; handoff interval in L1 S1 and the run key `renorm_interval_tau`) and a third here: the interval of the running mean in Skokos2010 Eq. (88). This spec writes h for the step, Delta for the Q-step, and "interval of the source" for the third.
(f) The delivered discrete estimators and the tangent-core memory formula count map steps; flows need per unit time. The conversion is division by h, applied by the caller; not stated in the integrator-catalog spec.
(g) The ofli spec (Compute) names the GTX 1050, compute capability 6.1, and risk K15 as open; K15 is closed (2026-10-17) and `_config/environment.md` gives the RTX 5060 Laptop GPU as the device of every local GPU criterion (D46). This spec uses the latter.
(h) The benchmarks file records Geist1990 Table III p.886 and Table II p.885 as "read"; the text is not on disk (F1), so the numbers cannot be re-verified from this repository.

## Open points

- P1. Scope in k and form. Recommended: one module generic in k from 1 to d; k = d primary with form (84) plus the penalty (the defect equation is a commutator, any nu > 0 stabilises, F3); k < d with form (92) and c_nu = 2 (C14); k = 1 is the continuous Benettin equation (C11). Alternative: k = d only; rejected, because the largest few exponents are the common use and the code is the same with one more term.
- P2. Coupling to the symplectic integrators (F4). Recommended: the orbit is advanced by the delivered integrator (identical orbit points for all estimators, L1 S2), the Jacobian of the field is evaluated at each orbit point, and Q is advanced by an explicit scheme whose nodes are orbit points: Heun on one step (order 2), classical Runge-Kutta on two steps (order 4); equal cost per orbit step (2 evaluations). Alternatives: (i) co-integrate (z, Q) by one Runge-Kutta scheme: the orbit is no longer the integrator's, not symplectic, no pathwise comparison with the other estimators; (ii) a Q-update exact on each elementary piece of the splitting: derived to be the discrete QR at every substep, not a continuous method (F4); (iii) orbit points at spacing h/2 or other dense output: needs another orbit.
- P3. Q-scheme order and defaults. Recommended: orders 2 and 4 delivered, order 4 the default (the order-2 error is too large for studies, F5; same cost), the horizon a multiple of two steps for order 4. Alternative: only order 2 (minimal); only order 4 (no cheap test scheme); both retained because criterion 4 needs an order ladder.
- P4. Orthogonality control. Recommended: the penalty of C12 with the stability rule of C14 and a health tolerance on E (source-aligned: the reported remedy of Christiansen and Rugh; works for k < d; defect measurable). Alternatives held, not built: (i) Cayley update for k = d (exactly orthogonal in exact arithmetic for any skew S; order 2 without a predictor; not in a read text); (ii) projection of Q to its polar factor each step (hides the defect, costs one more factorization; makes the method a discrete reorthonormalisation of Q with the growth still taken from the integral); (iii) no control: rejected for k < d (the instability of p.53; test R5).
- P5. Defaults and keys. Recommended (proposals): c_nu = 1 (k = d), 2 (k < d); E_max = 1e-3 (orthogonality health tolerance); scheme order 4. Proposed keys of `numerics-defaults.toml`, filled by this spec: `cqr_penalty_factor`, `cqr_orthogonality_tol`, `cqr_scheme_order`, each with a comment citing this spec. Alternative: no keys, run parameters only; rejected because the run record needs defaults.
- P6. Cost (F6). Recommended: record only (R1); no promise of a ratio. The measured ratio decides whether any study uses the method; the reported 40 of Geist1990 is not reproduced (the Toda chain is not built, the machine and the step control are 1990).
- P7. Degenerate and zero exponents (F7). Recommended: no special handling; the closed forms with exactly degenerate exponents (criteria 2, 3 (c)) are the evidence; continuous SVD (L10) stays catalog only.
- P8. Precision. Recommended: Float64 and Double64 on the CPU, BigFloat(256) for oracle runs, Float32 out (D24). The rounding bound B_C of criterion 14 is a derived worst case with a proposed constant (the device of L1 Amendment D35); stage 04 reports the realized ratios and an amendment replaces the form if it is loose or fails.
- P9. GPU. Recommended: Float64 kernel path built and tested (criterion 22); default backend follows the measured per-method record (D54), so the CPU unless the record says otherwise; Double64 CPU only (K20). Alternative: CPU only, no GPU path; rejected, the policy is GPU first (D17) and the module is a per-orbit loop of small products.
- P10. Chaotic-class gate on the GPU. Recommended: report the gap and the orbit divergence for the chaotic class, gate only the regular class and each backend against its own reference (criterion 22). The orbit-divergence term of sali Amendment D68 was derived for a map and for a maximiser pair (ofli); a flow version for an integral estimator (the sensitivity of l_i to the field Jacobian along the orbit) is not written. Alternative: derive it at stage 02 and gate; offered if the owner wants the chaotic class gated.
- P11. Module home and imports (Inconsistencies (d)). Recommended: in the Lyapunov module, importing Integrators (allowed by the documented layering) and Systems, not Tangent; the orbit step enters as the integrator's one-step map, and the vector field and its Jacobian are taken from the flow it is built with. The run cannot check that the flow held is the one the integrator advances (as ofli P10): the run record carries the flow identifier, the experiment layer compares. Alternative: construct the estimator from the one-step map; rejected for now, the map type does not expose the flow.
- P12. The triangular factor R and the clv-ginelli module. Recommended: out of scope; the final Q and the log diagonal are delivered; if clv-ginelli needs R, the equation R' = (Q^T A Q - S) R (Skokos2010 p.52, C4) is integrated in log-diagonal and ratio form by an amendment. Alternative: deliver R now; rejected, overflow handling of an upper triangle is a separate design.
- P13. Tolerances. All numbers marked proposal (C_q = 1, B_col, B_dir, B_en, c_d, C_s, E_max, delta_ext, the constants 5 and 2e-2 of criteria 10 and 11, the slope interval, B_C, the 1e-9 gate) are to be calibrated by a drafting run at stage 02; the closed forms and the algebra criteria (1, 2 (the l_i part), 5 (the algebra), 16, 17 (bitwise), 18 (counts), 19) are exact within rounding and are not negotiable.
- P14. Time unit. Recommended: lambda_i per unit time, with h recorded; the conversion from the discrete estimators (per step) is division by h (Inconsistencies (f)). Alternative: per step; rejected, the flows of the catalog and the sources (Skokos2010 Eqs. (86), (87)) are in time.
- P15. Wording of catalog row L8 at stage 05: Jacobian written A, form (84) for k = d and (92) for k < d, remedies "dissipation term (reported), Cayley and projection (alternatives, not built)"; benchmark row for the Geist1990 time ratio kept as "reproduction target"; status `verified` with a note that the first-order accuracy is set by the Q-scheme (F5).
