## Scope

- Catalog row I8 (`_config/catalogs/indicators.md`, section A): the Mean Exponential Growth factor of Nearby Orbits, Y(n) and its mean, for the 2D symplectic maps of the P2 panel (Y1 standard map, Y5 linear maps), in the weighted-sum form for maps (M4, M5). Delivered per deviation column: Y(n) and Ybar(n) of the standard form, and Ybar_(2,0)(n), the form that the only map study among the sources uses (F5). Flows, MEGNO of higher order (m, q) beyond these two, and the Lyapunov estimate by least-squares fit of Ybar (CincottaSimo2000 p.215, a post-processing of the retained series) are not delivered.
- Prerequisites (catalog statuses read 2026-10-22): tangent-core, standard-map, precision, L1 and L2, sali (I2) and fli-ofli (I5) are `verified`. The stream, seeded frame, health errors, scaled norms (Foundation, D69), compensated sums and the D66 / D71 / D72 chain rules are used as they are, with the one chain question P1.
- The module is a reducer that never changes the orbit. M1 to M5 and the algebra M6, M7 hold for any dimension and any number of unit columns; M8 to M13 use d = 2 and det M = 1 where stated.
- Out of scope: classification thresholds (pre-registered per study, `_config/numerics-policy.md`; the module never stops or caps an orbit; Y has no saturation), the smoothing of FroeschleLega2000-type running averages, SElLCE (Maffione2013), flows (M5).
- Keys: no `numerics-defaults.toml` key proposed. tau stays `renorm_interval_tau`, default 1. As for FLI, the result depends on tau (M12), so tau is a recorded parameter of the indicator.

### Findings for the owner

- F1. Definition for maps. The discrete-time MEGNO is printed in Mestre2011 Eq. (5): Y(t) = (2/t) sum over k = 1..t of k ln(delta(k)/delta(k - 1)), attributed there to Cincotta2003 (not read); it weights the log stretch of step k by k, the time at the end of the step, which is the convention of M5 (P3 is therefore settled for tau = 1). Flows: Eq. (4) there, CincottaSimo2000 Eq. (22). The mean of Y for maps is not printed in Mestre2011 (its Eqs. (2), (3) are averages of the FLI); the mean as a sum over samples is CincottaSimo2000 Eq. (26) and the (m, q) form is SanderMeiss2025 (secondary, one-step index shift, M4). The limits (regular 2, chaotic lambda_1 n / 2) are printed for flows (M3); Mestre2011 Eqs. (10) to (12) give the regular law for an ideal KAM orbit with delta = 1 + lambda t and the chaotic case delta = exp(sigma t) with slope sigma; as sums they are derived (M8). The readable map study prints only the regular level 0.5 of MEGNO(2,0) (M3, Maffione2011a p.7), which equals the derived limit of the (2,0) sum (M8).
- F2. Overlap with L1 and FLI (the question of D70 and D71). Printed, not only derived: Mestre2011 shows that the MEGNO is twice the difference between the FLI and its time average, Y(t) = 2 (FLI(t) - FLIbar(t)) for flows (Eq. (9), from Eqs. (4) to (8) by integration by parts), and for maps exactly Y(t) = 2 (FLI(t) - FLIbar(t)) + (2/t) ln delta(t) (Eq. (A2), Appendix A, from Eq. (5)), written Y = 2 (FLI - FLIbar) + O(sigma) in Eq. (13) because the last term tends to 2 sigma; there FLI(t) = ln norm(v(t)) (Eq. (1)), a single vector at time t, not a running maximum (M13). So Y and Ybar are weighted sums of the L1 partial sums s(k) = k tau X(k tau), k <= K (M6): Y(n) = 2 (s(K) minus the mean of s(0..K-1)). They are not functions of the L1 value at the final time: two paths with the same s(K) give different Y (criterion 1(iv): 2.05 against 1.05). They carry memory, like FLI, in a different form: the delivered FLI (fli-ofli) is the running maximum of s (Skokos2010 sup form) and differs from Mestre's FLI by the drawdown D of fli Q4; MEGNO compares s with its own running mean. They are not independent signals: MEGNO is a functional of the same path (M13). What MEGNO adds for claim C3 is a different scalar functional of the same sum: (i) an n-free regular-orbit level (2 for Ybar, 0.5 for Ybar_(2,0), M8) where the FLI behaves logarithmically and the L1 and FLI thresholds depend on the horizon (Mestre2011 p.L101: MEGNO gives the degree of chaoticity on an absolute scale and FLI only relative values; Maffione2011a Table 1; Darriba2012 Table 1); (ii) a growth rate lambda_1 / 2 read from the whole path; (iii) the source claim that deviations of the level from 2 mark periodic orbits (Maffione2011b p.5). Expect MEGNO, L1 and FLI labels to agree except near thresholds; the recorded line R1 measures it. The reducer is still needed: neither L1 nor FLI retains the weighted sums, P1.
- F3. The initial vector issue of single-vector indicators applies to the level. For a deviation norm that grows like t^nu, Ybar tends to 2 nu (CincottaSimo2000 p.215, Maffione2011b p.5). On a 2D map a vector with a component across the invariant curve has nu = 1; a vector along it has nu = 0, so Y is identically 0 for the K = 0 start along the invariant line (M10(a)) and the regular level is not 2. With the seeded random frame the probability of near-tangency is small but the transient is long near tangency (recorded line R5). The module therefore delivers both columns of the orthonormal frame, each with its own Y and Ybar, with column 1 (the L1 vector, the sources' single vector) the primary. No combination of the two columns is defined by any source and none is invented (P2).
- F4. A printed inconsistency. CincottaSimo2000 Eq. (25) prints the mean for chaotic orbits as sigma T, while its text says the slope of the mean "would be underestimated in a factor 2" and Maffione2011b p.4 prints sigma t / 2. From Eq. (22) with delta = exp(sigma t), Y = sigma t and the mean is sigma t / 2 (M3, M8). This spec and catalog row I8 use lambda_1 n / 2. Benchmarks rows "MEGNO" can say the definition is now verified (Eq. (22), Maffione2011b p.3); not edited here.
- F5. Which MEGNO the map study used. Maffione2011a p.4: "The version of the MEGNO here considered is the MEGNO(2,0) (see Cincotta et al. 2003)", Table 1 (p.4) threshold 0.5, and p.7: it "tends to a theoretically deduced fixed value for the regular component, namely 0.5". SanderMeiss2025 p.4 names (m, q) = (1, -1) and (2, 0) as the most useful members of the generalised MEGNO. The (2,0) sum of M4 has the regular limit 1/2 (M8, derived), which supports the identification (not proved: the defining text of Cincotta2003 was not read, P5). P2 tests the Maffione2011a recommendation "MEGNO+SALI for single orbits" (catalog section D), so the module delivers the form used there as well as the standard one.
- F6. Sources. Fetched (section Sources): CincottaSimo2000, Maffione2011b (arXiv:1108.5481), SanderMeiss2025 (arXiv:2409.08496), Hinse2010 (arXiv:0907.4886, flows, no equation cited), Mestre2011 (supplied by the owner, `data/literature/megno/Mestre2011.*`), and the already held Maffione2011a and Darriba2012. The earlier fli-ofli spec F4 and P5 (Mestre2011 unread, the continuum relation Q12 unverified) are resolved by Mestre Eqs. (8), (9): Q12 is the integration by parts of M1 (not edited there). Not obtained: Cincotta2003 (Physica D; the publisher site refuses automated access and the repository copy listed by Semantic Scholar did not accept connections from this machine; no arXiv version found by title and author queries; nothing circumvented), CincottaGiordano2016 (not fetched). No statement is taken from these except as reported in the texts read, and says so.
- F7. ChaosTools 3.5.4 has no MEGNO routine (Oracle); the external oracle line is limited to the independent tangent evolution.
- Contract questions: P1 (reducer and chain), P2 (forms and columns), P3 (index convention and averaging).

### Contract question P1: derived, or its own reducer, and where on the chain

Facts. (i) L1 retains X(n) at the schedule the caller chooses; if every handoff is stored, Y and Ybar of column 1 follow by post-processing (M6), with O(K) storage per orbit and no reducer. Column 2 has no L1 series (L1 has k = 1). (ii) The D66 / D71 rule: reducers that need the lengths read the columns before normalisation, keep their own sums, and exactly one reducer replaces the columns, placed last. (iii) FLI and sali already keep s_1, s_2; the sums are bitwise equal across reducers because the operations are the same (fli I6).
- (a) Derived from stored L1 series only: no new reducer, column 1 only, O(K) storage per orbit on the device, no (2,0) without more storage per orbit.
- (b) Recommended. A small reducer, as fli P1 (b): per column it keeps its own log sum and four weighted sums (M5), O(1) state per orbit, it reads the arriving columns before normalisation, replaces the columns by default when alone in the chain, and runs as a non-replacing reader placed before the replacer in a chain (the fli D72 item 1 option). The duplicated work is two scaled norms and two logs per handoff plus a few multiplications; its log sums are bitwise those of L1, sali and FLI. No change to a verified spec.
- (c) Fold the weighted sums into the FLI reducer: one owner of the lengths for both, but it amends a verified module and re-opens fli criteria 1 to 16. Not recommended.
- (d) A separate pass with MEGNO as the replacer on the bitwise identical orbit: no rule, a second pass for P2.
Criteria 1 to 11, 12(a), 13 and 14 hold under (b), (c) and (d); this spec writes them for (b).

## Equations

Notation. W_0 = (w_1(0), w_2(0)): the stream's seeded orthonormal frame (L1 S2); alpha_{i,k} the scaled Euclidean norm of the arriving column i at handoff k (L1 E3); a_{i,k} = ln alpha_{i,k}; s_i(k) = ln norm(w_i(k tau)) = sum of a_{i,j}, j <= k (L1 E2, E3, E8), s_i(0) = 0 (unit start); t_k = k tau; the column index i is dropped when one column is meant. L = ln sigma(K_std) is the one-step bound of L1 E14 (K_std the standard map parameter). "ref" = BigFloat(256) at the realized orbit points (L1 S3).

## Algorithm

Per orbit (ensemble member, orbit id j, run seed s), on the stream with k = 2, d = 2 (state length s >= d allowed, tangent-core Amendment D28):
1. Initialisation. The pair is the first two seeded columns, orthonormal and of unit length; all running sums are 0. A supplied frame is checked by the caller before the stream (sali Amendment D67; fli Amendment D72 item 7): the columns must be unit vectors, else a_{i,1} is not the log stretch of the first window and the convention s(0) = 0 is wrong. The weights use no s(0) beyond this.
2. At each handoff k (n = k tau), for i = 1, 2: alpha = scaled Euclidean norm of the arriving column i; if zero or not finite, a numerical health error naming the deviation matrix, with orbit id, column and handoff step (as sali Algorithm item 2). a = ln alpha. Add a to the compensated log sum s_i (the same operations as L1, sali and FLI, so s_i is bitwise equal to theirs). Update the weighted sums R1_i += t_k a, R2_i += t_k^2 a (formed in the element type, never as integers: t_k^3 passes the 64-bit integer range at about 2.1e6 handoffs). Y_i = 2 R1_i / n; Q_i += Y_i, Ybar_i = Q_i / K; Y20_i = 3 R2_i, Q2_i += Y20_i, Ybar20_i = tau Q2_i / n^3. (The equivalent forms of M6 may be used; criteria hold for either.)
3. Replacement: per the chain rule (D71, D72): the module replaces the columns by unit vectors (a diagonal S, tangent-core E7) when it is alone or last, and runs as a non-replacing reader when a later reducer replaces.
4. Emit per column Y, Ybar and Ybar_(2,0) at every handoff (storage schedule chosen by the caller, as L1 S8); at the end the final s_i with compensation terms, tau, the chain position.

Representation. Y, Ybar and Ybar_(2,0) are stored as computed, never clipped, capped or replaced; Y is unbounded (about 1.6e4 at K_std = 10, n = 1e4) and may be negative at small n. No early stop. Parameters recorded per run: tau, element type, state mode (wrapped with counts, or lifted), seed, frame source, chain position, and the convention tag "weights at the end of the window, mean over the handoffs 1..K" (P3). The frame source and chain order are recorded by Experiments (sali Amendment D67 item 2).

## Invariants

- I1 Linearity in s (M6): each series is a fixed linear combination of the sums s(0..K); Y and Ybar are unchanged by a constant added to s(0..K); Y is not a function of s(K) alone (M7).
- I2 Exact laws for the sums: constant increments lambda give Y = lambda (n + tau), Ybar = lambda tau (K + 3)/2, Ybar_(2,0) = lambda tau (K + 1)^2 (K + 2)/(4 K^2) (M8); bounded s gives bounded Y (M8(c)).
- I3 The orbit is unchanged by the module (L1 S2).
- I4 Column independence and symmetry: column i's outputs depend only on column i (the columns evolve independently, tangent-core E5); exchanging the two columns exchanges the outputs bitwise; a sign change of a column changes nothing bitwise.
- I5 Column sums: s_1, s_2 of the module are bitwise equal to the sums of sali, FLI and of an L1 run per column.
- I6 Schedule: Y_tau and Y_1 differ by at most (tau - 1) L (M12).
- I7 Relation to FLI: Y(n) >= -2 D(n) (M13).
- I8 Orbit independence and execution invariance: orbit j's series depends only on (system, parameters, initial point, seed, j, tau, N, T, state mode, frame), not on threads, chunk or position in the ensemble; across backends only to criterion 15. The hot loop allocates nothing after warm-up (BigFloat excepted).
- I9 No floor: each series is within B_M of the reference at every n (M11).

## Compute

Policy `_config/architecture.md`, "Compute" (D17, D25). Per-orbit, data-parallel, fused into the stream's single kernel: per handoff two scaled norms, two logs, per column a compensated add and four weighted accumulations; no dense factorization, so no "reducer is CPU-only" fallback reason. Reducer state per orbit, proposed: per column five compensated sums (s, R1, Q, R2, Q2: 10 values) and one integer shared by both columns: 168 bytes in Float64, 328 in Double64, added to the tangent-core memory formula as reducer bytes (sali R6; stage 03 reads the real size, as for FLI R6 where 96 bytes replaced the 64 proposed). GPU candidate for Float64 and Double64 (isbits); BigFloat is CPU-only with the reason "kernel does not compile for the element type" on a GPU request; GTX 1050, compute capability 6.1, weak Float64 (K15): GPU is the default for an element type only after the benchmark records `gpu_faster` (tangent-core AC13; per-method record D54). The step-chunked relaunch of tangent-core Amendment D44 must carry the five sums per column and the handoff index across launches (P9). CPU-vs-GPU agreement: criterion 15 (D68 form). CI has no GPU: CPU backend.

## Oracle

Package suite (analytic, invariant, cross-method, self-contained; D21): the closed forms M8, M10 in BigFloat(256) (the exact norms of L1 E10 to E13 give exact s_cf, and the two algebraic forms M5 and M6 are evaluated independently from them: the weighted sum and the Abel form must agree); the BigFloat(256) norm-form reference of M11 at the realized orbit points; the identities M7, M12, M13 against the L1, FLI and sali sums; health; execution invariance. The closed forms and the reference are the primary oracles.

External oracle, verification environment `verify/` (D21, D27). ChaosTools 3.5.4 (`verify/Manifest.toml`; installed source `~/.julia/packages/ChaosTools/0SbsT/`, read 2026-10-22): there is no MEGNO routine. A case-insensitive search of `src/`, `docs/` and `test/` for "megno" and "mean exponential" finds nothing, and no other package in the depot has it. `docs/src/chaos_detection.md` lists `gali`, `predictability`, the 0-1 test and expansion entropy. The nearest routines: `lyapunovspectrum` (tangent QR with the identity frame only, L1 Amendment D43; it returns the final exponent, so S(N) = N lambda(N) for one N per run), `gali` (a loop of `step!`, normalise, `current_deviations`: an independent tangent evolution with a hand-coded Jacobian and, as sali criterion 16, a supplied seeded frame), and `local_growth_rates` (finite perturbations, not the tangent). So the only external line is the oracle's independent tangent evolution: the script steps a one-column tangent system of the oracle at Delta t = 1 with the hand-coded Jacobian of the lifted rule, reads the deviation norm after each step, and forms the three series by the definition M5 in BigFloat from the oracle's own log-norm increments (criterion 14). It tests the stretch series and the weighting against an independent code path, not the definition itself, which has no independent implementation for maps; REBOUND's MEGNO is N-body only (project P6). The definition is tested by the closed forms, by the two algebraic forms and by the printed laws (M3, M8).

Printed algebra as an independent oracle: Mestre2011 Eq. (A2) is an independent derivation of M6 from M5 (the discrete MEGNO as 2 (FLI - FLIbar) + (2/t) FLI), used in criterion 7 against the stored L1 series and the verified FLI reducer.
