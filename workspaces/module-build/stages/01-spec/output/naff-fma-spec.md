# Spec: naff-fma

Library layer: Indicators (`_config/architecture.md`, "Module map" row Indicators, which lists NAFF/FMA, WBA and REM), with one contract question that the verified reducer protocol does not answer: how an indicator that works on the orbit itself, not on a deviation matrix, gets its samples (P1). Milestone M4 (`_config/build-order.md`, row M4). Status: approved at the stage 01 checkpoint, 2026-10-27 (D80): P1 and P2 to P14 as recommended; the owner also approved fetching Laskar2003 (arXiv math/0305364) to source the NAFF iteration (Amendment D80). Shared definitions are cited, not repeated: the Benettin spec as "L1 S1 to S8" (conventions, identical orbit S2, panel and starts S7 with the K_std = 0.5 regular-class screening, retained outputs S8); the standard-map spec (item 9, the K_std = 0 map; item 10, the linear symplectic blocks Y5); the experiments spec (R2 recording grid, R3 chunks and memory); the stretching-numbers spec for the per-orbit workspace of unsigned integers (D78) and the form of the open points; the rli spec for the "recorded lines, not thresholds" rule (D77); the sali spec for the CPU-GPU form (Amendment D68). Spec-local equation labels NF1 to NF15, findings F1 to F11, open points P1 to P14, recorded lines R1 to R8. Element types: Float64, Double64 (effective epsilon 2^-100) and BigFloat(256) for oracle runs; Float32 is out (D24). "eps" is the machine epsilon of the element type. "N" counts samples of one window; a sample is taken at a handoff, so with the renormalisation interval tau the sampling interval is tau map steps and frequencies are in cycles per sample. "K_std" is the standard map parameter. Page numbers are pages of the PDFs as fetched: arXiv PDF pages, equal to the printed pages, for Bazzani2023 (`data/literature/naff-fma/arxiv_2304.08340.*`, arXiv v1), Das2017 (`data/literature/naff-fma/arxiv_1601.06051.*`, arXiv v3 of 31 Jul 2017, titled "Quantitative Quasiperiodicity", same authors as the bibliography entry; the published Nonlinearity text was not compared) and Panichi2017 (`data/literature/naff-fma/arxiv_1703.10596.*`, arXiv v1). The two arXiv files of Das2017 and Panichi2017 were downloaded for this draft, with title and authors checked against the bibliography entries.

## Scope

- Catalog rows. Recommended scope of this run: I12 (frequency map analysis: the fundamental frequency of each of two consecutive windows of an orbit series, the distance between the two frequency vectors, and its decimal logarithm) and I12b (windowed and refined variants, as far as the held sources print them: the Hanning weight and the weighted Birkhoff weight family; the closed-form interpolation of Bartolini1996 and the higher-order Hanning family of Laskar are not printed in any held source and stay open, P5). Two estimator families are inside I12: E1, the windowed Fourier estimator (the frequency at the maximum of a weighted Fourier sum over the window; needs the whole window) and E2, the weighted mean phase advance (a weighted Birkhoff average of the phase increments; needs only running sums when the window length is fixed in advance). E2 is the estimator Bazzani2023 used for its FMA (pp.3, 19); E1 is the NAFF-type estimator of the catalog row. I12c, I12d, I13 and I14 are not in the recommended scope; each has a recommendation in P2.
- Prerequisites (catalog statuses read 2026-10-27): tangent-core, standard-map (Y1 lifted and wrapped, Y5), precision, experiments, and the verified Indicators reducers (sali, fli-ofli, megno, rli, stretching-numbers) are `verified`. The stream, its handoff protocol, the winding counts of the wrapped state (D28, D31), the per-orbit integer workspace (D78) and the one-replacer rule (D71, D72) are used as they are. New: nothing in Systems. Y2 (catalog systems.md, Hénon area-preserving quadratic map, M4, planned) is not needed by any gated criterion (P12).
- The module never changes the orbit. E1 is a pure function of a series the caller supplies; E2, if included, is a reader of the stream and never replaces deviation columns.
- Out of scope: classification thresholds (Bazzani2023 pp.12 to 13 derive theirs from a kernel density minimum, a study-level choice; thresholds are pre-registered per study, `_config/numerics-policy.md`); the secondary frequencies of the quasi-periodic decomposition and the orthogonalisation of the found terms (not needed for the first frequency, P7); diffusion maps over many windows (a study forms them from per-window frequencies); Hamiltonian flows (M5); the 4D coupled standard map (catalog Y3, M5) and Bazzani's 4D modulated cubic map (no catalog row, P12).
- Keys: six proposed for `numerics-defaults.toml` (P10), none sourced from a held text; the window length is not a key but a preregistered run parameter (P3).

### Findings for the owner

- F1. What the held source prints and what it does not. Bazzani2023 defines FMA_n as the Euclidean distance between the fundamental-frequency vectors nu_1, nu_2 evaluated over [0, n/2] and [n/2, n] of the orbit (p.3, unnumbered text after Eq. (2)); a KAM orbit has FMA_n tending to zero, a chaotic orbit a value bounded away from zero (p.3). It names three ways of obtaining a frequency: an FFT, the average phase advance (APA), and the method of Bartolini et al., an FFT with a Hanning filter and an interpolation step that gives a closed-form formula (p.3); then states that APA improved by weighted Birkhoff averaging "will be used in the sequel" (p.3) and that the FMA of the paper was evaluated this way for parallel hardware, because it keeps only the weighted mean phase advance and not the orbit (p.19). It prints the weight function (Eq. (11), p.4) but no equation for the frequency estimate itself, no window pair other than [0, n/2] and [n/2, n], no phase convention and no normalisation of the weights (Eq. (12), p.5, prints the weighted sum without a normalising denominator; the normalised weights are Eq. (4) of Das2017, p.3). So the held source fixes the index and the weight, not the estimator.
- F2. The NAFF algorithm is not available. No held source prints the iterative extraction of terms by maximising a windowed Fourier integral, the Gram-Schmidt step on the found terms, the Hanning window of order p, or the interpolation formula of Bartolini. Laskar1990, LaskarFroeschleCelletti1992, Laskar1993, Gomez2010a, Gomez2010b, Bartolini1996, Simo2014 and Chandre2003 are not held, and nothing of them is cited here by equation or page. What the held sources say about them, as reported: Das2017 p.3 states that Laskar uses a Hanning data weighting cos^2(x) on (-1, 1) over iterates from -N to N, with convergence "of order 1/N^2 or 1/N^4" and, for the analogue of the sin^2 weight, 1/N^3 (the passage cites page 136 of Laskar's "Introduction to Frequency Map Analysis", bibliography key Laskar1999, not held; the "or" is as printed and the extracted sentence is ambiguous about which weight has which order); and that a C-infinity filter appears in Remark 2 of that chapter's appendix (p.146) but is not used there. NF2 is therefore a working definition derived here, not Laskar's algorithm (P5, P14).
- F3. Not a tangent indicator, and no overlap with the verified modules. SALI, FLI, MEGNO, RLI and the stretching numbers read the deviation matrix; this module reads the orbit. The only existing frequency-like quantity is the second-half rotation number of the P1 study labels, (x_N - x_h) / (2 pi (N - h)) at the recorded step h nearest N/2 (study p1-le-benchmark findings, P5 there), an equal-weight average of the lifted increments (error of order 1/N, Das2017 p.10, text around Eq. (13)); the weighted version of that average is E2 (NF5). The weighted Birkhoff version of FLI (Bazzani2023 Eq. (12), p.5) is a weighted sum of the per-step log growth that the verified L1 sum already accumulates; it belongs to I13 (P2). A case-insensitive search of the ChaosTools sources read at drafting finds no windowed refined frequency, weighted Birkhoff average or reversibility error routine (Oracle).
- F4. Windows and resolution. A window of N samples resolves lines separated by about 1/N (the Fourier bin) before any refinement; the refined frequency of an isolated line is much better than 1/N. Printed orders: Hanning NAFF error of order N^-2 (as reported, Das2017 p.3, not read); weighted Birkhoff with a C-infinity weight, error below any power of N for a smooth observable on a quasi-periodic orbit with a Diophantine rotation vector (Das2017 Theorem 3.1, Eq. (12), p.9, and p.3), weights w_quad about N^-2 and w_sin2 about N^-3 (p.11), and no improvement for chaotic orbits (p.3). Derived and checked in drafting: maximising the amplitude |phi| alone fixes the location only to about sqrt(eps)/N, because the maximum is quadratic; the stationary point of |phi|^2 (the zero of its derivative) reaches eps. The definition NF2 therefore asks for the stationary point (drafting: error 1e-11 for the amplitude search against 1e-17 for the derivative form on a pure line, N = 1000). For close lines the Hanning leakage dominates: two lines of amplitude ratio 0.1 and separation 0.0113 cycles per sample gave frequency errors of 1.7e-8, 1.4e-10, 5.2e-13 and 2.1e-15 at N = 1000, 4000, 16000 and 64000 (the separation in bins grows with N), faster than N^-2.
- F5. The observable is a choice with consequences on the standard map. The catalog writes z_j = q_j + i p_j. On the cylinder the lifted q grows linearly for a rotational orbit (no line spectrum: inadmissible, derived); on the torus the wrapped q is a sawtooth, whose harmonics decay only as 1/k (derived), and p is a nonzero constant for a rotational orbit, so the series has a large zero-frequency term and the largest line can be the mean (derived). An angle-like observable, exp(i q) with q wrapped, is smooth, has the rotation number as its fundamental line for a rotational circle (modulo 1) and the oscillation frequency for an island orbit. Drafting observations (Oracle, drafting checks): with exp(i q) minus its window mean, an island orbit (K_std = 0.5, start (2.0, 0.9)) returned lines at -0.0624997 and +0.0624999 in the two windows at N = 1000, because a libration has a spectrum symmetric about zero, so the sign of the largest line is ambiguous and the index jumps to 10^-0.9 for a regular orbit; chaotic starts (K_std = 5) returned largest lines within about 1.5e-3 of zero, where the leakage of the removed mean sits, so their index was of order 1e-3, not of order one. Both are properties of the observable and the peak rule, not of the orbit (P4).
- F6. Sticky orbits look quasiperiodic. A window pair that lies inside a long sticky episode sees one quasi-periodic orbit and returns a small distance; the index cannot see an escape that happens after 2N. Drafting: a K_std = 0.9 start near the chaotic layer (start (1.0, 3.0)) gave distances 2.1e-8 (Hanning) and 7.1e-9 (weighted) at N = 1000 and 8.4e-2 and 6.9e-2 at N = 4000. Bazzani2023 p.10 shows the same finite-time ambiguity for FLI (orbits that decrease like regular ones until about 10^6 turns, then jump). The window length is the sticky-orbit horizon; the index is reported per window pair, never as a limit (P3, R4).
- F7. What the printed performance says. On the 4D modulated Hénon-like map, FMA is the weakest of the seven indicators compared: ranked last or nearly last in the performance estimate (Table 1 and the text under it, p.16: GALI(4) is the highest scorer in all cases, REM follows, FMA tends to be last), with a three-mode distribution of log10 FMA (Fig. 7 and its text, pp.13, 14, 20: one mode at values close to numerical precision, one above about 1e-5, and a populated ensemble connecting them), a false-positive error nearly independent of the turn number (p.17) and a jump of that error when the false-negative error drops (p.17). Numerical entries of Table 1 are not reproduced here: the extracted table text interleaves its columns. These are statements about one map, one threshold algorithm and the APA estimator; they are not a limit on E1.
- F8. The cost of the series. E1 needs the series of the window pair: 16 bytes per complex Float64 sample (32 in Double64), so 2N samples cost 32 N bytes per orbit: 320 kB at N = 10^4, 3.2 MB at N = 10^5, 160 MB at a total length of 10^7 samples, 1.6 GB at 10^8 (the horizons of the P1 study, experiments spec R2). With the launch capacity formula of experiments spec R3 (f = 0.5 of free memory, 8 GB device, `_config/environment.md`) a 4 GB budget holds about 12 500 orbits at N = 10^4 against about 4.2e7 orbits for the 96-byte two-column stream (orientation, derived, not measured). Bazzani2023 p.19 makes the same point: FFT-based frequencies need the whole orbit stored while the APA form does not. E2 holds a handful of running sums for one declared window length; a log grid of H horizons needs one set per horizon, because the weights depend on the window length (F9).
- F9. E2 facts, derived unless cited. The weights w(t) = exp(-1/(t(1-t))) depend on t = j/N, so N must be known at the first sample; a grid of horizons costs one accumulator set per horizon (about 64 bytes in Float64 each: the weight sum and the weighted phase sum of two windows, each a compensated pair), 12.8 kB at H = 200, which does not fit a small isbits state and needs a floating-point workspace (the same Tangent amendment as P1 option (b)); a single declared horizon needs about 96 bytes (Float64), the scale of the verified reducers. The phase increment of a complex series is the principal argument of z_{j+1} / z_j and is correct only for |nu| < 1/2 per sample and for a series that winds around the origin of the observable (P4). For the lifted angle of a map on the cylinder the increment is exact: for the standard map in the project form the lifted q increases by the post-kick p, so the weighted rotation number is the weighted mean of p / (2 pi) (derived from standard-map spec item 9; Das2017 Eqs. (13), (14), p.10). A rotation number obtained this way is zero for an island orbit (no net winding), so the two windows agree trivially there: E2 on the lifted angle detects rotational circles only (P4).
- F10. Y2 is not the map of Bazzani2023, and Y5 gives exact oracles. Bazzani2023 Eq. (26), p.7, is a 4D map: a 4 x 4 rotation by the modulated linear frequencies (Eqs. (27), (28), p.7) applied to (x, p_x + x^2 - y^2 + mu (x^3 - 3 x y^2), y, p_y - 2 x y + mu (y^3 - 3 y x^2)), with the Hénon map cited to a CERN yellow report (ref. [51]), not to the 1969 paper. Setting y = p_y = 0 leaves the x-plane invariant (reader inference from Eq. (26) as printed): a 2D map of rotation by the modulated frequency applied to (x, p_x + x^2 + mu x^3); with mu = 0 and no modulation this is the quadratic area-preserving Hénon map in rotation form. So Y2 is a 2D relative of Bazzani's map, not the map. For gated criteria the verified Y5 gives what Y2 would: a rotation block is an exact rotation of z = q + i p by a known angle (z' = exp(-i theta) z with the block of standard-map spec item 10), and a direct sum of two blocks gives a 4D orbit with two known frequencies.
- F11. The other rows. I12c: the catalog source is Simo2014, but the bibliography annotation of that entry is "Hénon-Heiles variants in 2 and 3 DOF; fraction of positive-Lyapunov initial conditions vs energy" and the entry is a preprint not read, so the annotation does not support a power-spectrum test (P2, P14). I12d: Chandre2003 (not held) is the wavelet analysis source. I13: Das2017 is now held and prints the weights, the normalised average and the rotation-number estimate (NF4, NF5). I14: Panichi2017 is held: the reversibility error is the norm of the displacement from the initial condition after n forward and n backward steps, d_n = M^-n(M^n(x_0)) - x_0 (Eq. (2), p.2), with finite-precision rounding replacing the average (Eq. (3), p.3, and the text below it); it grows as exp(alpha t) for chaotic orbits and as a power t^beta for regular ones (abstract, p.1); Bazzani2023 prints the squared form REM^2 = (1/2)(y_2n - x)(y_2n - x) (Eq. (22), p.6) and notes that it needs only the orbit computed twice (p.6, p.18). It needs the inverse map and is precision-dependent by design (P2).

### Contract question P1: how an orbit-series indicator gets its samples

Facts. (i) A reducer is called at every handoff (every tau steps) with the step count, the orbit point x (in wrapped mode the wrapped coordinates followed by the integer winding counts) and the deviation matrix W of one orbit; its state is an immutable isbits value, failures are recorded in the state and raised by the host, and its host summary is formed at the end (Tangent, reducer protocol). (ii) The per-orbit workspace added by D78 holds unsigned 32-bit integers only; it is allocated once per chunk, zeroed at the start, carried across relaunches and read back with the final; at most one reducer of a chain requests one. (iii) The stream requires at least one deviation column (checked at drafting in the Tangent source), so an orbit-only reducer on the stream still pays for one tangent column. (iv) There is exactly one per-step update implementation (tangent-core AC14); estimators compared "on the identical orbit" are bitwise equal because the stream advances the orbit independently of the reducers (L1 S2). (v) The recorder of the study runner stores, at each step of its grid, the orbit point (wrapped coordinates and winding counts) together with the finite-time exponents of the method and the diagnostic maxima; the grid is every step up to first_steps, then a logarithmic grid, plus the preregistered fractions (experiments spec R2 items 1, 2); the series bytes are 8 x (columns per grid step) x (grid steps) per orbit (R3 item 3). (vi) E1 needs the whole window; E2 needs running sums if N is known at the first sample (F8, F9).

- (a) Pure estimator functions on a series. The estimator takes a complex series (and, for the 4D case, one series per plane) and returns the frequency, the peak amplitude and a status; it never sees the stream. The series comes from a feed in Experiments. (a1) The existing recorder with first_steps set to 2N holds every sample, but stores, per step, the orbit point and the method's other columns in the run store: about 3 to 10 columns of 8 bytes against 16 bytes needed, written to HDF5 for every orbit (about 0.5 to 1.6 MB per orbit at N = 10^4), no new code, acceptable for a pilot at N up to about 10^3, not for the panel. (a2) A transient window feed: the recorder loop (which already runs on the shared tangent body, so the orbit stays the identical orbit) copies the observable at each handoff of the window pair into a per-chunk buffer of 32 N bytes per orbit (Float64), the host transform extracts the frequencies, and only the frequencies and flags are stored (about 50 bytes per orbit and horizon, P11). Cost: a new construct in Experiments, none in Tangent; the transform runs on the CPU (an FFT is not a kernel of the stream), so the chunk capacity is set by host memory and the series bytes (F8).
- (b) A stream reducer holding a series buffer. The D78 workspace is unsigned 32-bit integers, so a buffer of complex Float64 or Double64 samples would need a second per-orbit workspace of the element type (or bit-casting into integer words, which hides the type): an additive amendment to the verified tangent-core, with its own memory term (32 N bytes per orbit in Float64, 64 N in Double64, added to the capacity formula), the zeroing and relaunch-carry rules of D78, and the same one-owner rule. It also keeps the transform on the host, so the benefit over (a2) is only that the series and the deviation columns share one pass. The cost is a device launch that holds 320 kB per orbit at N = 10^4 (about 3300 times fewer orbits per launch than the stream alone, F8), and one tangent column carried for nothing (fact (iii)).
- (c) O(1) streaming reducers. A reducer reading only the orbit point, with running sums, for estimators that need no stored window: the weighted mean phase advance (E2), the weighted Birkhoff average of any observable (I13), the weighted sum of the log growth (the weighted FLI of Bazzani2023 Eq. (12)). Constraint: the window length is fixed when the reducer is built; the state is a few compensated sums per window and per horizon (F9). Cost: none in Tangent for one horizon (the reducer is a reader, so it can sit in any chain); a grid of horizons needs the floating-point workspace of (b).

Per estimator, recommended:

| Estimator | Needs | Recommendation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| I12 E1, windowed Fourier frequency | whole window, transform | (a2); (a1) only as the zero-code pilot |
| I12b refinements (weights, interpolation) | whole window | (a2), the same estimator with options |
| I12 E2, weighted mean phase advance | N in advance, running sums | (c) for one declared horizon; horizon grids wait for the floating-point workspace, or are run as several windows |
| I13 weighted Birkhoff averages, weighted FLI | N in advance | (c), in its own small module (P2) |
| I12c power spectrum | whole window or segment averages | (a2), M6 |
| I12d time-frequency | whole series | (a2) with a stored series, M6 |
| I14 REM | forward then backward orbit, inverse map, no series | neither: an orbit-only forward-backward pass; needs the inverse map in Systems, deferred (P2) |

Recommended: (a2) for E1 and I12b, (c) for E2, (b) rejected for M4. The only new contract construct is the transient window feed in Experiments, which changes no verified module; the owner decides at the checkpoint whether E2 is built in this run (P2). With (a2) alone the module is a set of pure functions plus a documented obligation on the study runner, as the stretching-numbers module was.

### Open points

- P2. Scope and the other rows. Recommended: this run builds I12 (both estimators) and I12b as far as printed. Needed by the P2 study (`_config/research-question.md`, claims C1 to C3, joint paper D65): only I12; the research-question row P2 lists "FMA/NAFF comparator", and none of I12c, I12d, I13, I14 is in it. Per row. I13 (weighted Birkhoff averages, weighted FLI): a separate small module `weighted-birkhoff` after this run, because its running sums are the same as E2's and its hypotheses (research idea 25, the literature topic `weighted-birkhoff` of D65) are not part of P2; alternative: fold it into this run, which is cheap if E2 is built here (one set of sums, one weight function) and widens the checkpoint. I14 (REM): a separate module `rem` in M5 with the integrator catalog, because it needs the inverse step in Systems, is precision-dependent by design, and Bazzani2023 reports it second best (p.16), so it deserves its own spec; alternative: bring it into P2 if the owner wants a second non-tangent comparator. I12c and I12d: defer to M6 with a decision-log entry (qualitative, no threshold, one unsupported source for I12c, F11); alternative: a `spectral-tests` module. E2 inside this run or inside `weighted-birkhoff`: recommended here, since Bazzani2023's FMA is E2 and it is the only FMA estimator that scales to the P1 horizons (F8); alternative: E1 only here, E2 at the weighted-birkhoff run.
- P3. Window length and the two-window convention. The catalog writes [0, T] and [T, 2T]; Bazzani2023 writes [0, n/2] and [n/2, n] (p.3), the same pair with n = 2T. Recommended: windows of N samples each, [0, N) and [N, 2N), index at horizon n = 2N samples; N is a preregistered run parameter, never a default; the start offset is zero (no transient is discarded, a study may start later); the sampling interval is tau map steps and tau = 1 is the default (for tau > 1 frequencies alias above 1/2 cycle per sample; the caller must keep |nu| < 1/2 per sample). The minimum window is 16 samples (a key, P10). Horizon grids: the series path evaluates any set of window pairs from one stored series (the cost is the maximum horizon); E2 pays one accumulator set per horizon (F9). Alternative: overlapping windows (several pairs per orbit), left to the study.
- P4. Observable, cylinder and torus. Recommended: the observable is a caller-recorded choice; the module takes a complex series. Defaults for the standard map: for E1, exp(i q) built from the wrapped coordinate (exact reduction, D28) with the window mean removed; for E2, the lifted increments (rotation number). The z = q + i p of the catalog row is recorded as an arm (R3), allowed with mean removal; for planar elliptic maps (Y5 rotation blocks, Y2) z = q + i p about the fixed point is the natural observable and needs no mean removal. Mean removal is an option recorded with the run; without it a zero-frequency line can dominate (F5). Sign ambiguity: for an orbit whose spectrum is symmetric about zero (island orbits, F5) the sign of the largest line is not determined; recommended: the module reports the amplitude at the mirror frequency, raises a "mirror" flag when it exceeds a fraction of the peak amplitude (0.9, a key), and then evaluates the distance as the smaller of the distances to nu_2 and to -nu_2. Alternative: fold every frequency to [0, 1/2] always (loses the sign for planar rotation). A peak within 2/N of zero is flagged "near zero" (F5). Lifted observables: the argument of exp(i q_lifted) loses about |q| eps; the wrapped form does not (criterion 7(c)).
- P5. Weight and Hanning order. Held: the Hanning weight cos^2 on (-1, 1), equal to sin^2(pi t) on (0, 1) (Das2017 p.3 as reported, Eq. (15) p.10), and the weighted Birkhoff family w^[p](t) = exp(-1/(t^p (1-t)^p)) (Eq. (16), p.11; p = 1 is Eq. (3), p.2, and Bazzani2023 Eq. (11), p.4; p = 2 converges faster at 30-digit precision "but not in 15-digit precision", Das2017 p.11). Not held: the Hanning window of order p of Laskar and its stated error orders, and the interpolation formula of Bartolini. Recommended: E1 with the p = 1 Hanning weight, sample positions (j + 1/2) / N (symmetric about the window centre); E2 with w^[1] and the normalised weights of Das2017 Eq. (4); the sample-position convention (t = j/N as in Das2017 p.3, or t = (j + 1/2)/N) is recorded in the output, and the chirp invariant NF11 holds for both. Higher orders and the interpolation formula wait for Laskar1990, Laskar2003 or Bartolini1996 (P14); I12b is then closed by an amendment. Alternative: ship w^[2] now (printed, Das2017 Eq. (16)) as a second weight; recommended only for the Double64 arm.
- P6. Resolution floor. No held source prints a floor for the frequency difference of two windows. Reported (not read): Hanning error of order 1/N^2 (Das2017 p.3). Derived: the Fourier bin 1/N, a main lobe of about 2/N half-width for the Hann weight, lines closer than about 4/N unresolved; a rounding floor of about eps for the stationary-point form and about sqrt(eps)/N for the amplitude-only form (F4). Recommended: the module states no floor of its own; the floor is the index floor of P8; the estimator-limited error is a recorded line (R2), not a gate; the spec gates only exact signals (criteria 1 to 3).
- P7. How the frequency vector is formed. 2D map: one complex series, one frequency, the distance is the circle distance of two scalars (NF9). 4D map: one complex series per plane (q_i + i p_i or the angle-like form), one frequency per plane, the Euclidean norm of the per-plane circle distances (the Euclidean distance of Bazzani2023 p.3); this needs no term extraction because each plane's largest line is its fundamental frequency in the uncoupled and weakly coupled regimes (reader inference). The Gram-Schmidt orthogonalisation of found terms and the extraction of secondary frequencies in NAFF are not needed for the first term and are not printed in a held source: recommended deferred to an amendment (P14); until then a plane with several strong lines returns the largest and the module reports the ratio to the second largest local maximum outside the main lobe. Y3 (M5) is the first map that exercises the 4D vector; the 4D criterion here uses two Y5 blocks (criterion 6).
- P8. The index and its floor. NF1 gives delta = ||nu_1 - nu_2||; the index is D = log10 delta (catalog I12; Bazzani2023 plots log10 FMA, p.11, with values "close to numerical precision" for regular orbits, p.20, and an axis down to -15 in Fig. 7, p.14). delta = 0 exactly has no logarithm, and two windows of a pure line differ by rounding. Recommended: delta is always returned unclipped; D = log10 max(delta, delta_floor) with delta_floor = 32 eps (derived: each frequency of a pure line is within 16 eps of the truth, criterion 1), and a "below floor" flag when delta <= delta_floor; never a stop, never a cap above. The flag marks the regular plateau, it is not a class label. Alternative: D = -infinity for delta = 0 (rejected: it breaks tables and quantiles).
- P9. Precision. The frequency error is estimator-limited, not eps-limited: for E1 on a generic quasi-periodic signal the printed order is N^-2 (as reported, F2), so at N = 10^4 about 1e-8 against eps = 2.2e-16 for Float64; Double64 does not help there and helps only for exact lines. For E2 with a C-infinity weight the truncation error falls below any power of N and the floor is rounding: Das2017 p.11 reports errors below 1e-31 in quadruple arithmetic that then grow with N from accumulated round-off (also the Fig. 6 caption, p.12); drafting in Float64: the weighted rotation number of a perturbed rigid rotation was within 5.6e-17 of the exact value from N = 250 (Oracle). So the index floor is 32 eps of the element type, and Double64 moves it from about 7e-15 to about 3e-29 for E2 but not for E1. ## Equations

Notation. A window is N consecutive samples z_j (j = 0..N-1) of an observable of the orbit, complex (P4); the pair of windows is [0, N) and [N, 2N), so the index horizon is n = 2N samples (P3). Frequencies are in cycles per sample, reduced to [-1/2, 1/2). phi(nu) = sum_j chi_j z_j exp(-2 pi i nu j) with weights chi_j > 0. For E2 the window holds N phase increments Delta_j = (angle of z_{j+1}) - (angle of z_j), j = 0..N-1, so a window pair uses 2N + 1 samples and shares the sample at index N. "Window centre" is the point (N-1)/2 (sample positions (j + 1/2)/N) or N/2 (positions j/N).

## Algorithm

Inputs of an evaluation, validated before any work: the observable series per plane (E1) or the declared window length and the observable (E2); N >= the minimum window; the pair of windows lies inside the series; the weight; the sample-position convention; the mean-removal option; the grid pad. An invalid argument is an argument error naming the parameter. Non-finite samples are recorded as a failure with the plane, the window and the sample index (the protocol records failures, it does not throw in a kernel; the host raises).

E1, per plane and per window (series path):
1. Optionally subtract the window mean (P4). A series that is constant afterwards (all samples equal) has no line: status "degenerate", frequency undefined, delta undefined, recorded.
2. Weights chi_j = sin^2(pi (j + 1/2)/N) (NF3, P5), a positive symmetric sequence.
3. Coarse search: |phi| at the M = pad x N frequencies of a uniform grid on [-1/2, 1/2); the largest grid value locates nu_c (the lowest index wins a tie, flagged).
4. Refinement: the stationary point of |phi|^2 inside [nu_c - 1/M, nu_c + 1/M] with negative second derivative, found by a derivative method to the accuracy of the element type (NF2). If none exists, the coarse value is kept and the window is flagged "refinement failed" (recorded, the index is then resolution-limited to 1/M).
5. Outputs: nu, the amplitude |phi(nu)| / sum chi, the mirror amplitude |phi(-nu)| / sum chi (taken as 0 when -nu lies within 4/N of nu on the circle, that is near 0 and near 1/2, where the mirror line is the line itself), the ratio of the largest local maximum of |phi| on the coarse grid outside [nu - 4/N, nu + 4/N] to the peak (a measure of a second strong line), the flags "near zero" (|nu| < 2/N), "mirror" (mirror amplitude above the key fraction of the amplitude).

E2, per window (running sums, one declared N): at sample j of the window form Delta_j (NF6), accumulate A += w(j/N) Delta_j and B += w(j/N) with compensated sums; at the end of the window nu = A / (2 pi B). The weight is evaluated in the element type at each sample (no table). The second window restarts the sums at sample N with t = (j - N)/N.

Pair and index, both families: nu^(1), nu^(2) per plane; if either window of a plane carries the "mirror" flag the plane distance is the smaller of dist(nu_1, nu_2) and dist(nu_1, -nu_2) (P4); delta = sqrt(sum of squared plane distances) (NF9); D = log10 max(delta, 32 eps) with the "below floor" flag (NF10). A plane or a window with a failure or a degenerate series gives no delta for the pair; the status names which.

Horizons. The series path forms any set of window pairs from one stored series. E2 keeps one pair of sums per declared horizon (F9).

## Invariants

- I1 Exactness on a pure line. For z_j = a exp(i(2 pi nu j + phi_0)) the frequency of every window is nu within 16 eps (circle distance), for every N >= the minimum window, weight, amplitude a > 0 and phase (NF2, NF3).
- I2 Covariances. Phase and amplitude factors leave nu unchanged; conjugation maps nu to -nu; multiplication by exp(2 pi i nu_0 j) shifts nu by nu_0 modulo 1; delta is symmetric in the windows (NF12).
- I3 Drift. For a linear drift the pair distance is |alpha| N, independent of nu_0 (NF11).
- I4 Known systems. The K_std = 0 map has frequency p_0/(2 pi) in every window; a Y5 rotation block has -theta/(2 pi) per plane; delta is at its floor for both (NF13, NF14).
- I5 Weights. The Hanning weights are positive and symmetric about the window centre (bitwise); the normalised Birkhoff weights sum to 1 within 4 eps and are symmetric under j <-> N - j within 4 eps relative; the weighted average of a constant is that constant within 2 eps (NF3, NF4).
- I6 Index. delta is never clipped; D >= log10(32 eps) always; the "below floor" flag is set exactly when delta <= 32 eps (NF10).
- I7 Orbit transparency. The stream reducer (E2) never changes the orbit or the deviation matrix: orbit points are bitwise those of the run without it, and the outputs of the other reducers in the chain are bitwise their stand-alone values (L1 S2, the one-replacer rule).
- I8 Execution invariance. Outputs are bitwise identical for 1 versus N threads and for different chunk sizes on the CPU backend (tangent-core T-I5 form); the series feed adds no dependence on the grouping of orbits.
- I9 Sampling conditions. N >= the minimum window; 2N samples (E1) or 2N + 1 samples (E2) available; |nu| < 1/2 per sample (for tau > 1 the frequency is that of the handoff series, aliased with period 1/tau in map-step units); a violation is an argument error before any work or, where it is a property of the data, a recorded flag.
- I10 Health. A failure is recorded in the state with orbit, plane, window and sample, the host raises with the orbit id, a failed chunk stores nothing (tangent-core AC8 form); a constant series is a status, not a failure.

## Compute

Policy `_config/architecture.md`, "Compute" (D17, D25). Data-parallel across orbits.
- E2 (reducer, option (c)): one reader in the stream's single kernel; per handoff, per window: one exponential, one division, one complex ratio argument (or one subtraction for a lifted angle), two compensated adds; constant cost, no allocation after warm-up, no dense factorization, so no "reducer is CPU-only" fallback reason. State, proposed and derived (P13): per declared horizon two windows with a compensated pair each for A and B (8 values), the previous sample (2 values, absent for the lifted-angle form), the handoff count and a failure code: about 96 bytes in Float64 and 176 in Double64 for one horizon, plus 64 and 128 bytes per extra horizon (F9); these enter the reducer bytes of the tangent-core memory formula, which has to add a floating-point workspace for grids of horizons (P1 (b)). A GPU candidate in Float64 (D25), Double64 only if the kernel compiles, else the recorded fallback reason.
- E1 (series path, option (a2)): the orbit pass is data-parallel (the recorder loop, device or CPU, as in D54), writing the observable at each handoff into a per-chunk buffer; the transform and the stationary-point search run on the CPU, threads over orbits with dynamic scheduling, the transform library's own thread count set to 1 when orbit threads exceed 1 (the BLAS rule of the architecture). What stays on CPU and why: a transform is not a kernel of the stream and the series would have to leave the device anyway; a batched device transform is a possible later measurement, not assumed. Memory per orbit: 32 N bytes in Float64 (16 per complex sample, 2N samples) and 64 N in Double64, on top of the stream's bytes; capacity = floor(f x free / (M + reducer bytes + series bytes)) with f as in tangent-core, Compute (experiments spec R3 item 3), so launches are chunked by free memory and the series of one chunk is discarded after its frequencies are extracted. Orientation, derived, not measured: at 2.47 ns per orbit-step on the device (`_config/environment.md`) the orbit pass of 2 x 10^4 samples is about 50 microseconds per orbit amortised over the ensemble, against a host transform of a grid of 8 x 10^4 points per window that is likely of the same order or larger; the host transform, not the orbit pass, may set the rate, which is a stage 04 measurement (R5).
- Backend agreement (D68 form, sali Amendment D68; risk K16): every backend is compared with its own BigFloat(256) reference evaluated at that backend's own realized samples, never one backend with the other on chaotic orbits; the tolerances are the exact-algebra bounds of criteria 8, 13 and 15, on frequencies and on the weighted sums, never on trajectories.
- Reproducibility: no random numbers in the module; the orbit starts and seeds come from the caller (L1 S7), keyed by orbit id, so results do not depend on thread count or device.

Derived here (not printed): the sampled form. For a window of N samples at positions t_j = -1 + (2 j + 1) / N (the (j + 1/2)/N convention of P5 mapped to [-1, 1]; t_j = -1 + 2 j / N for the j/N convention), the sampled scalar product is phi(nu) = (sum over j of chi_p(t_j) z_j exp(-2 pi i nu j)) divided by (sum over j of chi_p(t_j)). The constant factor of chi_p (2 for p = 1) cancels in the frequency and in the normalised amplitude; the base weight sin^2(pi (j + 1/2) / N) of NF3 is chi_1 divided by 2, the same window. For a trigonometric-polynomial weight of degree p the equispaced sum over N points equals N times the mean, exactly, when N exceeds p (the aliasing images lie at multiples of N pi), so the sum of the weights of chi_p is N up to rounding (D80b). The first zero of phi_p is at x = (p + 1) pi (Eq. (57)), that is at the distance (p + 1) / N cycles per sample from the line: the main-lobe half-width is 2/N for the Hanning window (p = 1) and 1/N for no window (p = 0).

### What these texts add, confirm or contradict

Confirmed. The window family of NF20 and the weights of D80b(i) now rest on three sources (Laskar2003 Eq. (57); Gomez Eqs. (5), (6); Bartolini Eq. (23) for p = 1), with one constant q = 2^p (p!)^2 / (2p)! and the amplitude normalised by the sum of the weights (N). The Hanning error order N^-4 has a second independent source (Bartolini Eqs. (27), (33)). The aliasing rule of NF23 and the two-sampling remedy are in Gomez (pp.46 to 47) and in LuqueVillanueva2014 (Remark 3.2, p.33). The description of the Bartolini route in Bazzani2023 p.3 (an FFT, a Hanning filter, an interpolation in closed form) is right: Eqs. (18), (25), (26).

Resolved. Item 3 of Amendment D80 left the "1/N^3" that Das2017 reports for Laskar "unconfirmed here". Bartolini p.11 (after Eq. (33)) says that Laskar's earlier estimate counted only the 1/N^3 depression of the secondary maxima, and p.9 gives that depression (the far peaks fall from 1/N to 1/N^3): the 1/N^3 is a height, not the exponent of the frequency error, which is N^-4 for Hanning (Bartolini Eq. (33), Laskar2003 Eq. (63)).

Added. The closed-form interpolation (NF28), the exact sampled Hanning spectrum (NF27, gate D81b), the printed definition, bound and plateau of the equal-weight phase advance (NF30, gate D81a), the extrapolated phase advance as a power-law competitor (NF31), the collocation method (NF32), the two-window protocol with a gap and a real observable (NF33), the printed sampled-window positions (NF25).

Differs, with no base criterion contradicted. (a) Three sources recommend three orders: Hanning is optimal and higher orders do not help (Bartolini pp.9 to 10), higher orders for regular motion, p up to 3 or 5 (Laskar2003 Sect. 7.2, p.15, Amendment D80 item 5), n_h = 2 as a starting value (Gomez p.48). The default p = 1 and the option stay (D80c); R8 records the comparison. (b) The default sample positions (j + 1/2)/N of P5 appear in no held text; all printed sampled windows use n/N (NF25). Both conventions stay available and both are tested; the default is unchanged, so P5's convention stays "derived". (c) The base pair of consecutive windows [0, N) and [N, 2N) (P3, Bazzani2023 p.3) is not what Gomez does: a gap of 3N between the windows, N up to 2^18 (NF33). The pair is a study choice, as P3 already says; nothing in the module forbids forming any pair of windows from one series. (d) Das2017 (published p.4132) states the error of LuqueVillanueva2014 as C_p N^-p, a rate N^-7.8 on the three-body example and 30 digits from about 2 million points against 20 thousand; LuqueVillanueva2014 prints O(1/N^(p+1)) (Eq. (28), p.19) and the slope -(p + 1) log10 2 (p.34). The two differ by one in the exponent; the figures quoted by Das from Figure 11 of his reference [10] were not checked (the exponent and the point counts are not used anywhere in the spec). (e) Internal differences of the Bartolini report are listed in NF30 (Eq. (9) against Eq. (A.9); (A.5) with the arctangent bound pi; the origin convention of (A.4)); none changes a criterion, because only the bound (A.5) is used. (f) Sign: the report writes the planar coordinate as x - i p_x (p.4), so its tune is positive where the spec's q + i p (NF14) gives the negative frequency; the sign is a convention of the observable and is gated by criterion 7(a), unchanged. (g) The report counts N samples and N - 1 increments (Eq. (6)); E2 here counts N increments and N + 1 samples; all conversions are marked derived. (h) Gomez works with real signals and folds frequencies to (0, 1/2); the module takes a complex series, and the mirror rule and the tie of P4 and criterion 11(d) are the same phenomenon (Hermitian symmetry, Gomez p.46).

### Criterion 15(b), restated

As worded in the stage 04 instruction, 15(b) compared CPU and GPU frequencies on the orbits whose final states agree within 1e-9, with bound B_cpu + B_gpu. It failed on the regular class (largest ratio 17.6) because the gate has no orbit-divergence term: the two backends follow orbits that differ in state by up to 3.8e-10, and the frequency of a regular orbit moves with the orbit (shear times the action difference). Each backend alone passes B_E2 against its own BigFloat(256) evaluation (largest ratio 0.052, criterion 15 as written), so the algebra of the reducer is correct; the failure is in the formulation of the cross-backend gate. The failing row stays in the log.

New 15(b), the gate: the device reducer against the CPU pure function `weighted_phase_frequency` evaluated on the same device samples (the same handoffs, read back), both windows, max over orbits of |difference| / (2 B_E2); pass if at most 1, in Float64 for both classes (K_std = 0.5 and 10, 1024 orbits, N = 1000). Measured: regular 0.033, chaotic 0.062. Implemented as the row `AC15b ... same samples, algebra only` of `verify/gpu.jl` (function `naff_d15`).

### Interface and practice changes (stage 04 review fixes M1 to M3 and LOW items)

1. `reducer_final` of `WeightedPhaseAdvance` gives a ninth field `near_pi`: the number of planar increments that entered a window with |Delta| > 0.9 pi. The value of E2 is unchanged. Reason: a planar increment near pi is ambiguous in sign and the weighted mean then depends on the branch (spec NF4, reviewer finding M1). Lifted-angle observable: 0.
2. `check_run(r, tau, N; d = d)` validates the observable indices against the state dimension `d` and raises an ArgumentError naming `observable`. The check is not in `reducer_init`, because a throw with string interpolation on a runtime branch makes the Float64 device kernel uncompilable (InvalidIRError). Caught by the GPU tag, not by the CPU suite.
3. Verification practice, for every reducer that runs in a device kernel: no throw that builds a string or can allocate is reachable from `reducer_init`, `reducer_step` or `reducer_final`; argument checks live in `check_run` or in the constructor. A GPU tag rerun after each review fix is part of the stage 04 evidence.
4. `pad >= 2` is enforced in the transform (the zero padding that the Newton bracket 1/M relies on); the bracket is 1/M.
5. The window is scaled by an exact power of two in `window_frequency`, so the frequency is scale covariant for amplitudes from 1e-300 to 1e307; `_phase_inc` falls back to power-of-two normalised samples when the product leaves the safe range (exponent read from the bits, no `exponent` call that could throw). `_accumulate` skips a weight that is exactly 0. A non-finite transform gives `:failed`.
6. Coarse search: compensated direct sums restarted every 32 samples. State size unchanged: 96 bytes Float64, 176 Double64 for one horizon, +64 and +128 per extra horizon.
