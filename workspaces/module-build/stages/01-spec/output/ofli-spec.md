## Scope

- Catalog row I6 (`_config/catalogs/indicators.md`, section A): the Orthogonal Fast Lyapunov Indicator for Hamiltonian flows, the FLI of the component of the deviation vectors orthogonal to the flow, on the flow sampled by the one-step map of a symplectic integrator. Row I7 (OFLI2, second-order variational) is out of scope: the catalog marks it "M5 (catalog only)", it needs second variational equations, and its only listed source (Barrio2005) is not on disk (F1, P7). No second-order equation is written here.
- Prerequisites (catalog and commit status read 2026-11-11): I5 FLI `verified` (fli-ofli, D71, D72); henon-heiles F3 `verified` (commit f636c55); the integrator-catalog rows and the tangent stream `verified` (as in the henon-heiles spec Scope); sali (I2) and L1 (I1) `verified`. Not re-read: `sali-verification.md`, the integrator-catalog verification report.
- In scope: the OFLI as a reducer on the tangent stream of a flow (equations O1 to O13), single-vector and full-basis forms, the log form, the discrete version at handoffs, the choice and the error of the direction removed (O10, O11), the known behaviours (periodic, quasi-periodic, chaotic) as tests.
- Out of scope: OFLI2 (above); classification thresholds and the saturation cap (a study concern, pre-registered per study, `_config/numerics-policy.md`; the sources use the threshold t and the cap 1e20 for the unlogged value, reported in O7 only); the running-average smoothing of the FLI (FroeschleLega2000, via Maffione2013 p.5); OFLI for maps (no definition exists, fli F1; the flow direction of a map is undefined); extended phase space methods (the nonseparable integrator whose state has twice the flow dimension has no defined flow direction there, P11); a new FLI variant.
- No catalog row is added. The test flows (free particle, harmonic oscillator, pendulum) are suite fixtures, not systems.

### Findings for the owner

- F1. What the readable sources define. Darriba2012 Sect. 2.2 p.6 and Maffione2013 Sect. 3.2 p.5 define the OFLI in one sentence and one display: "we take the component orthogonal to the flow for each unit i.d.v. of the basis at every time step" (Maffione2013 adds "and retain the largest component among them"), OFLI(t) = sup_t of the list of the orthogonal components. The display of Darriba2012 (checked in the page image) has the perp marks on the vectors and, unlike the FLI display above it, no norm bars; the display of Maffione2013 was read in the text extraction, where the perp glyphs are lost. Not printed in any read text: the metric of "orthogonal" (Euclidean is assumed here, P2), the formula of the projection, which vector field is used, the log form, the lower limit of the sup, whether the sup runs over time or over the basis only (F2), and anything about sampling a flow by a numerical map. Fouchard2002 (the origin; one vector, per Maffione2013 footnote 2 p.5) is available as the abstract only. Barrio2005, Barrio2006 and Barrio2016 are not on disk (checked with find over `data/literature/`), so they are not cited for content. Everything beyond the sentence above is derived here or proposed.
- F2. Running supremum versus oscillation. Darriba2012 p.6 and p.15 and Maffione2013 p.5 say the OFLI of a periodic orbit "oscillates around a constant value". A running supremum of a quantity over time is nondecreasing and cannot oscillate. Either the plotted series is the instantaneous largest orthogonal component (the sup over the basis at time t), or "oscillates" is loose language for a bounded, quickly converging supremum. The delivered FLI is the running maximum (fli Q1, Skokos2010 p.56). This spec delivers both series from the one reducer (P4): the running supremum (primary, consistent with fli Q1) and the instantaneous value, so either reading can be compared with the sources.
- F3. The direction removed matters, and the discrete choice limits the periodic-orbit result (derived, O10, O11). The numerical map has no vector field of its own. Using the exact field at the numerical state (candidate A) has an angular error against the direction the map transports, O(h^p) under assumption A1; on a periodic orbit the unprojected norm grows linearly, so the error term grows linearly too and the constant OFLI of a periodic orbit holds only up to a time t* proportional to h^-p. The candidate of carrying the flow vector as an extra deviation column (candidate B) is rejected: in a chaotic region the carried vector turns into an unstable direction and removing it removes the growth being measured. Recommended: A, with t* measured (P1, P6).
- F4. OFLI <= FLI exactly, and the delivered FLI handles k = 2 columns only (fli: the reducer needs two columns). The flow definitions of both sources use the full basis (2N vectors for N degrees of freedom, so 4 for F3). A paired FLI/OFLI comparison on F3 therefore needs the unprojected maximum for k = 4 as well; it costs nothing extra because the cumulative log norms are needed anyway (O9). Recommendation P3.
- F5. The start printed for the 5-periodic orbit of Darriba2012 Sect. 3.2 p.15, (x, y, px, py) = (0, 0.35207, 0, 0.14979) "on the energy surface h = 0.125", has H = 0.0586 for the Hamiltonian printed on p.10 (hand arithmetic: V = 0.047429, py^2/2 = 0.011219). It is not used as a start; periodic orbits here are built by a root solve on the section map (Algorithm 6). No closure of the printed start is claimed.
- F6. Naming. Maffione2013 p.5 writes "In the case of the OFLI2" above a display and a sentence that describe the OFLI of Fouchard2002 (the heading, the footnote and the following text say OFLI). Catalog row I7 "OFLI2" is the second-order variational indicator of Barrio2005 (bibliography entry). The two uses are distinct; only the first-order OFLI is defined here.
- F7. No numerical benchmark exists for the OFLI. `_config/bibliography/benchmarks.md` has the FLI growth row only. The read sources give laws, not values (O7). Quantitative oracles are therefore closed forms (O5, O6) and independent reference integrations.

## Equations

Notation. Flow dz/dt = f(z), f = J grad H the vector field of the flow (the direction removed), z in R^d, d = 2N, J the canonical matrix (J^T = -J, J orthogonal). Phi_t the flow map, DPhi_t its derivative (the tangent product). Deviation vector w(t) = DPhi_t(z0) w(0), unit start. P_z = I - f f^T / (f^T f) the Euclidean projection onto the orthogonal complement of f(z) (defined where f(z) is not zero); w_perp = P_z w; ||.|| Euclidean. A basis is d unit vectors; a single vector is the first column. theta the angle between w and f(z).

O1. Definition, reported, unlogged. Single vector (Fouchard2002 as reported in Maffione2013 footnote 2 p.5): OFLI(t) = sup over s <= t of ||w_perp(s)||, w(0) a unit vector. Basis (Darriba2012 Sect. 2.2 p.6, N vectors on an N-dimensional flow; Maffione2013 Sect. 3.2 p.5, 2N vectors on an N-degree-of-freedom Hamiltonian): the largest orthogonal component among the basis vectors at every time step, OFLI(t) = sup of ||w_perp,i(s)|| over i and over s <= t (the sup over time is the reading of the delivered FLI, fli Q1, Skokos2010 p.56; the sources' own wording is F2). The same initial basis is kept for a whole sample (Maffione2011a Sect. 2 p.4, as in fli Q2). The norm in "component" is the Euclidean norm (assumed, F1, P2).

O2. Log form, derived. Because ln is increasing, ln of the sup equals the sup of the log (fli Q3). The module stores G(t) = sup over s <= t of ln ||w_perp(s)||, never exponentiated; the unlogged value is a display conversion that reports overflow above 709.78 in Float64 (fli Q3). The sup includes the term s = 0, ln ||P_{z0} w(0)||, which is at most 0 (P5).

O3. Projection identities, derived. ||P w||^2 = ||w||^2 - (f . w)^2 / ||f||^2 = ||w||^2 sin^2 theta, so ln ||w_perp|| = ln ||w|| + ln sin theta. Hence ||w_perp|| <= ||w|| and, with the same vectors and the same sampling, OFLI <= FLI exactly (the basis forms compared on the same columns). P_z is unchanged by rescaling f. For an orthonormal full basis (d columns) the squared orthogonal norms sum to the squared Frobenius norm of P, which is the rank of P, d - 1: sum_i ||P w_i(0)||^2 = d - 1, so the s = 0 term of the basis form is at least (1/2) ln((d - 1)/d) (d = 2: -0.3466; d = 4: -0.1438).

O4. Direction and energy component, derived. (i) f(z(t)) = DPhi_t(z0) f(z0): differentiate Phi_t(Phi_s(z)) = Phi_s(Phi_t(z)) in s at s = 0. So the flow direction is itself a solution of the variational equation, with zero growth rate, and a deviation vector parallel to it stays parallel. (ii) H(Phi_t(z)) = H(z), differentiated in z: grad H(z(t)) . w(t) = grad H(z0) . w(0) for every deviation vector (the energy component is conserved). (iii) f . grad H = 0 (J antisymmetric) and ||f|| = ||grad H|| (J orthogonal). So n_E = grad H / ||grad H|| lies in the orthogonal complement of f and ||w_perp|| >= |w . n_E| = |grad H(z0) . w(0)| / ||f(z(t))|| (a lower bound for any d).

O5. One degree of freedom (d = 2), exact, derived. The orthogonal complement of f in R^2 is the line of grad H (O4 iii), so ||w_perp(t)|| = |w . n_E| = |grad H(z0) . w(0)| / ||f(z(t))|| exactly, where f is not zero. The sup over s of the log is ln |grad H(z0) . w(0)| minus the running minimum of ln ||f(z(s))||. For a libration or rotation of the pendulum (F4) or the oscillator (F1) the minimum is attained within one period, so the OFLI is exactly constant after one period (periodic orbit); the unprojected norm of the pendulum grows linearly because its period depends on the energy (O6). Free particle H = p^2/2 (d = 2, p0 not zero): w(t) = (w_q + t w_p, w_p), ||w_perp|| = |w_p| for all t, OFLI = ln |w_p| constant (the s = 0 term equals it), FLI = ln sqrt((w_q + t w_p)^2 + w_p^2) grows. Oscillator F1 with frequency omega: ||grad H(z(t))|| is bounded between omega sqrt(2 E) and sqrt(2 E), so OFLI is constant after the first quarter period at a closed-form value.

O6. Periodic orbits, derived (hypotheses A2). Let z0 lie on a periodic orbit of period T, M = DPhi_T(z0), f0 = f(z0). Hypothesis A2: the orbit belongs to a smooth family of periodic orbits parametrised by the energy E (period T(E)), and the reduced monodromy A (the action of M on the tangent space of the energy surface modulo f0, dimension d - 2) is elliptic: all eigenvalues on the unit circle and A diagonalizable (so ||A^k|| is bounded; for d = 2, A is the zero-dimensional map and A2 reduces to the family hypothesis). Then: (a) M f0 = f0 (O4 i). (b) With v = dz_E/dE (grad H(z0) . v = 1): Phi_{kT}(z_E) = Phi_{k (T - T(E))}(z_E) because Phi_{T(E)} fixes z_E, so differentiating in E at E0, M^k v = v - k T'(E0) f0. (c) By O4 (ii) M leaves the energy surface tangent space (which contains f0) invariant; for u in it, M^k u = (lift of A^k u-bar) + c_k f0 with c_k bounded under A2 (a geometric sum of bounded powers with no eigenvalue 1 in A). (d) For w0 = (grad H(z0) . w0) v + u and a time t = kT + s, w(t) = DPhi_s M^k w0 and DPhi_s f0 = f(z(s)) (O4 i), so P_{z(s)} removes every multiple of f0: ||w_perp(t)|| <= C (|grad H(z0) . w0| ||v|| + ||u-bar||) with C = sup over s of ||DPhi_s|| times the bound of A^k and the lift. Hence G(t) is bounded and nondecreasing: it converges, OFLI is bounded. (e) The unprojected norm satisfies ||w(t)|| >= k |T'(E0)| |grad H(z0) . w0| ||f(z(s))|| - C', linear growth when T'(E0) is not zero and grad H(z0) . w0 is not zero: FLI of the same vector grows like ln t. This is the statement of the sources (reported: periodic orbits give an OFLI that oscillates around a constant, Darriba2012 p.6, p.15, Maffione2013 p.5, Fouchard2002 abstract: constant FLI at periodic orbits for maps, and a new value, the orthogonal FLI, "to fully recover" it for flows). Remainder after k periods of a nonresonant rotation: the supremum of an oscillating term of phase step phi is within a factor cos(pi / K) of its limit after K periods (derived), so ln(OFLI(100 T) / OFLI(10 T)) <= -ln cos(pi / 10) = 0.0502 for one oscillating mode.

O7. Quasi-periodic and chaotic motion, reported plus consequence. Quasi-periodic: OFLI grows linearly in t, as the FLI (Darriba2012 Sect. 3.2.1 p.15: "an increment following a linear rate is seen for quasiperiodic motion, as in the case of the FLI"; Maffione2013 p.5: same behaviour as the FLI), so the log form is ln t plus a constant. Chaotic: exponential growth, as the FLI (same sources), so the log form grows linearly in t. The limit rate equal to the largest exponent lambda_1 is an expectation, not derived here: it needs the angle between w and f not to shrink exponentially, a regularity statement not in any read source; O3 gives only the upper bound limsup G(t)/t <= lambda_1 (from OFLI <= FLI and fli Q8). Tested numerically, not assumed. Dependence on the initial basis: Darriba2012 Sect. 3.2.2 p.17 reports no dependency of the OFLI on the initial basis for a quasi-periodic orbit (bases with m = 0, 1, 2 vectors tangent to the torus). Derived companion, O8: any two orthonormal frames differ by at most ln sqrt(d) (0.693 for d = 4) in the basis form. Thresholds as reported only: FLI/OFLI threshold t (unlogged, Darriba2012 Table 1 p.10), saturation 1e20 (p.19); never a pass threshold here (pre-registered per study).

O8. Basis-free value and sandwich, derived. Let sigma_1(m) be the largest singular value of P_{z_m} DPhi_{t_m}(z0) (P times the cumulative tangent product), and F*(n) the maximum of ln sigma_1(m) over the handoffs m <= n and s = 0. For an orthonormal full basis of d columns, sum_i ||P w_i||^2 = ||P DPhi||_F^2 >= sigma_1^2, so the larger column is at least sigma_1 / sqrt(d). Hence F*(n) - ln sqrt(d) <= G_B(n) <= F*(n), for every orthonormal frame, with ln sqrt(2) = 0.3466 and ln sqrt(4) = 0.6931. The single vector form has no lower bound (a start along the flow has G = minus infinity at all times: O4 i).

O9. Discrete version at handoffs, derived. The flow is sampled by the one-step map of a symplectic integrator of step h; the deviation columns are carried by the tangent of that map by the same splitting (integrator-catalog Equations item 17); handoffs at n = j tau (L1 S1). At a handoff the arriving column i is w_arr,i (a window of tau steps from a unit start), alpha_i = ||w_arr,i|| (L1 E3), S_i(n) = S_i(n - tau) + ln alpha_i, S_i(0) = 0 (fli Q1 notation). The cumulative vector is w_i(n) = exp(S_i(n - tau)) w_arr,i, and with the orbit point x_n:
 g_i(n) = ln ||P_{x_n} w_i(n)|| = S_i(n - tau) + ln beta_i(n), beta_i(n) = ||P_{x_n} w_arr,i||, equivalently g_i(n) = S_i(n) + ln sin theta_i(n);
 g_i(0) = ln ||P_{x_0} w_i(0)||;
 G_i(n) = maximum of g_i(m) over the handoffs m <= n including m = 0; OFLI_1 = G_1 (the first column), OFLI_B = max_i G_i (all k columns; the source basis when k = d); instantaneous value Ginst_B(n) = max_i g_i(n).
The projection acts on the arriving column of one window and the cumulative scale enters through S_i, so no column is stored unnormalised and the reducer can read the columns before normalisation (chain rule: Algorithm item 6). The sup runs over handoffs only, so with the same orbit OFLI_tau(n) <= OFLI_1(n) at a common n that is a multiple of tau (fewer terms in a maximum, exact, as fli Q10); the gap has no known bound for flows (fli Q10 used a standard-map constant) and is reported.

O10. Which field is removed in the discrete version, derived. The numerical map psi (one step, step h, order p: psi(x) = Phi_h(x) + h^(p+1) e(x) + O(h^(p+2)), e smooth) has no vector field. Candidates at handoff n:
 (A) the exact field at the numerical state, u_A = f(x_n) / ||f(x_n)||;
 (B) the direction transported by the numerical tangent, u_B = Y_n f(x_0), Y_n the cumulative tangent product of the map (one extra carried column);
 (C) the field of the modified Hamiltonian of the integrator: no read source gives it, not available.
Defect of one step: delta_k = f(x_k) - M_k f(x_(k-1)) with M_k the tangent of step k. Using f(Phi_h(x)) = DPhi_h(x) f(x) (O4 i), delta_k = f(psi(x)) - Dpsi(x) f(x) = h^(p+1) (Df e - De f)(x) + O(h^(p+2)) (derived: expand both terms about Phi_h(x)); it vanishes for the exact flow. With r_n = f(x_n) - Y_n f(x_0): r_n = delta_n + M_n r_(n-1), so r_n = sum_k Y_(n,k+1) delta_k, a sum of defects amplified by the tangent products. Consequences: (1) the two candidates differ in any component by |  ||P_A w|| - ||P_B w||  | <= sin(angle(u_A, u_B)) ||w|| <= (||r_n|| / ||f(x_n)||) ||w|| (the norm of the difference of two projections onto lines is the sine of the angle between the lines; rigorous). (2) In a chaotic region Y_n applied to the nonzero vector delta_k has the exponential growth of the tangent product, so u_B converges to the most expanding direction, after a time of order (1 / lambda_1) ln(1 / h^p), and then P_B removes the direction that carries the growth: OFLI_B collapses against FLI. Candidate B is therefore not a usable definition. (3) For one degree of freedom (d = 2) the symplecticity of psi gives det Y_n = 1 (up to rounding), so with candidate B, ||P_B w_n|| = |w_0 x f_0| / ||Y_n f_0|| exactly (2D cross product): the analogue of O5 for the numerical map, a test of the algebra independent of the integrator error.

O11. Error of candidate A, derived under assumption A1. A1: a symplectic integrator of order p applied with a small fixed step is, up to terms smaller than any power of h over the times used, the exact time-h flow of a modified Hamiltonian (backward error analysis; a standard statement, not printed in any read text: Reich1999 and HairerLubichWanner2006 are in the bibliography and are not on disk, so no content is cited from them). Then the numerical tangent is the exact tangent of the modified flow, the direction it transports is the modified field f~(x_n), and the angle between f(x_n) and f~(x_n) is eps_n = O(h^p). By O10 (1) with u_A against the modified direction, the absolute error of ||w_perp|| is at most sin(eps_n) ||w_n||. Regimes: (i) chaotic or generic: ||w_perp|| is of order ||w||, so the relative error of the orthogonal norm is of order eps_n, and the log form is shifted by O(h^p), negligible against the Lyapunov growth. (ii) Periodic orbit: ||w|| grows linearly (O6 e) while ||w_perp|| is bounded, so the error bound grows linearly and the plateau is lost at t* where sin(eps) ||w(t*)|| reaches ||w_perp||, t* of order h^(-p) (the constant is the measured quantity of criterion 11 and P6). After t* the computed OFLI grows linearly with a slope proportional to h^p. (iii) One degree of freedom: the computed ||w_perp|| equals |grad H(x_n) . w_n| / ||grad H(x_n)|| and grad H . w_n is no longer conserved but drifts by (grad H - grad H~) . w_n = O(h^p) ||w_n||.

O12. Rounding, proposal (constants borrowed, not measured for flows). The reference series g_ref,i(m) is the same expression in BigFloat(256) at the realized orbit points, and the maximum is taken in BigFloat. |g_i(m) - g_ref,i(m)| <= 8 eps Psi_m^(i) + 8 eps / sin theta_i(m): the first term is the sum bound of L1 S3 and L1 Amendment D35 per column, the second the cancellation in the projection (an absolute error of eps ||w_arr|| in the difference w - (f . w) f / (f . f), relative eps / sin theta). The two-point rule for the maximum of two sequences (fli Q11) gives B_G(n) = the larger of the bound at the two maximisers (computed and reference). There is no floor: when sin theta is of order eps the term is unresolved; such a handoff can attain a maximum only if its ||w_perp|| is the largest, which the criteria report.

O13. Initial frame, derived. At s = 0, ||P_{x_0} w_i(0)|| = 0 exactly when w_i(0) is parallel to f(x_0) (the log is minus infinity). In the single-vector form a start parallel to f stays parallel for all t (O4 i): its OFLI is zero (log minus infinity) for all t, which the module must refuse as an initial frame; a start within 8 eps of parallel is flagged as unresolved. In the basis form at least one column of an orthonormal frame has a nonzero component (O3: sum = d - 1), so the form stays finite.

## Algorithm

Per orbit (ensemble member, orbit id j, run seed s) on the tangent stream of the flow's one-step map with k columns, 1 <= k <= d, state at each handoff available to the reducer:
1. Initialisation. k unit columns (the seeded frame, orthonormal, or a supplied frame, canonical or rotated). The caller's frame check: unit norm within 8 eps (as fli Amendment D72 item 7); a column within 8 eps of parallel to f(x_0) is rejected for the single-vector form (O13). S_i = 0 (compensated), G_i = g_i(0) = ln ||P_{x_0} w_i(0)|| for i = 1..k, with the s = 0 term skipped when it is minus infinity.
2. At each handoff n = j tau: evaluate the exact field f(x_n) (candidate A, O10; one evaluation, d numbers; independent of k). If f(x_n) is not finite or its norm is zero or below the smallest normal number, a numerical health error naming the vector field, with orbit id and handoff step (a flow at an equilibrium has no direction to remove).
3. For each column i: alpha_i = scaled Euclidean norm of the arriving column (the operations of the FLI and sali reducers, so the sums are bitwise equal to theirs, fli Algorithm item 2); a zero or non-finite alpha_i is a health error naming the deviation matrix, with column and step. beta_i = scaled Euclidean norm of the projected column P_{x_n} w_arr,i, formed as the column minus its component along f(x_n) (a projection formed in the element type, O12); a beta_i of zero gives g_i = minus infinity, a legitimate term that never attains a maximum and is counted and reported.
4. g_i(n) = S_i(old) + ln beta_i with S_i(old) the compensated sum before this handoff; then S_i += ln alpha_i (compensated). Update the running maxima G_i; OFLI_1 = G_1, OFLI_B = max_i G_i; record the instantaneous Ginst_B(n) = max_i g_i(n); record the handoff and column at which each maximum last strictly increased (the maximisers of O12); record the sine sin theta_i = beta_i / alpha_i at the maximisers.
5. Companion output (P3): the unprojected maxima FLI_1, FLI_B of the same columns from the same S_i (fli Q1, Q2), for k = d as well as for the k = 2 of the delivered FLI.
6. Chain rule (fli Amendment D72, D71, one-replacer rule): the reducer reads the arriving columns before normalisation and keeps its own sums; exactly one reducer of a chain replaces the columns by unit vectors at a handoff and it is the last; OFLI replaces the columns only when it is last (alone in a chain, or after the others). No change to a verified module. The reducer needs the flow's vector field, which it holds from construction; it takes nothing else from the layers above it (Layering: below).
7. Emit OFLI_1(n), OFLI_B(n), Ginst_B(n), and the companion FLI values at every handoff (storage schedule by the caller, L1 S8); at the end the sums with compensation terms, the maximisers, the minimum sin theta seen, the count of minus-infinity terms, tau, h, k, the frame source, the flow identifier and the chain position.
Periodic orbit for the oracle (Algorithm item 8): Newton iteration on the section map of henon-heiles (Equations 10 to 13, fixed point of the first return map of period m), until the closure error of the orbit after m returns is at most 1e-13; the reduced monodromy A is computed from the tangent at the fixed point and the orbit used only if |tr A| < 2 (A2). The periods are the section period 1 near the centre of the central island at E = 1/8 and the period of the 5-periodic island chain if found; neither start is taken from a source (F5).

Parameters recorded per run: tau, h, element type, k, frame source, seed, chain position (replacer or not), flow identifier (the flow the reducer holds is the one the map integrates: P10).

## Invariants

- I1. Nondecreasing: G_i(n), OFLI_1, OFLI_B are nondecreasing in n; OFLI_B >= OFLI_1; OFLI_B >= (1/2) ln((d - 1)/d) for an orthonormal full basis (O3).
- I2. OFLI <= FLI: for the same columns and handoffs, G_i(n) <= the running maximum of S_i, and OFLI_B <= FLI_B exactly in exact arithmetic (O3); in the run within the rounding bound of O12.
- I3. Frame symmetry: OFLI_B is bitwise unchanged by exchanging columns or changing the sign of a column (the columns evolve independently and the projection is linear); OFLI_1 is not symmetric.
- I4. Scale invariance of the direction: replacing f by c f (c not zero) leaves every g unchanged within rounding (O3).
- I5. Basis-free sandwich: F* - ln sqrt(d) <= OFLI_B <= F* for k = d (O8), within the bound of O12.
- I6. Column independence: the S_i are bitwise equal to those of the FLI, sali and L1 on the same orbit and columns (the same operations).
- I7. Orbit unchanged by the module (L1 S2); orbit independence and execution invariance: orbit j's series depends only on (flow, parameters, initial point, seed, j, h, tau, N, T, frame), not on threads, chunk or position in the ensemble; across backends only to criterion 17; the hot loop allocates nothing after warm-up (BigFloat excepted).
- I8. Exact-flow identities (not invariants of the numerical series): O4 (ii) and O5 hold for the exact flow; for the numerical series they hold up to the error of O11, which is the measured quantity.
- I9. Schedule: OFLI_tau(n) <= OFLI_1(n) at a common n multiple of tau (O9).

## Compute

Policy: `_config/architecture.md`, "Compute" (D17, D25). Per orbit, data parallel, fused into the stream's single kernel: per handoff one vector-field evaluation (d numbers), per column one dot product, one subtraction, two scaled norms, two logs, two compensated adds, a few comparisons; no dense factorization, so no "reducer is CPU-only" fallback reason. GPU candidate for Float64 and Double64 (isbits flow parameters: the flows F1, F3 and the pendulum are isbits; a flow carrying arrays is not); BigFloat is CPU-only with the reason "kernel does not compile for the element type". GTX 1050, compute capability 6.1, weak Float64 (K15): the GPU becomes the default for an element type only after the benchmark records `gpu_faster` (D25, D29). Reducer state per orbit, proposed: k compensated log sums (2k numbers), two running maxima, the instantaneous value, the last and minimum sin theta (4 numbers in all with the companion maxima), two integers per maximum for the maximiser; Float64 with k = 4: 12 numbers of 8 bytes plus 6 integers, about 150 bytes, added to the tangent-core memory formula as reducer bytes. CPU-vs-GPU: in the form of sali Amendment D68 from the start: each backend against its own reference at its realized orbit points; the gap only where both orbits agree (regular class); chaotic class by the D68 orbit-divergence term per column (criterion 17). Chunk size from free memory (policy); BLAS threads set to 1 under Julia threads (policy).

## Inconsistencies

(a) Catalog row I6 gives the vector count "1" (one vector, the form of Fouchard2002). The two full texts read for the flow case use the full basis (N vectors on an N-dimensional flow in Darriba2012 p.6, 2N for a Hamiltonian in Maffione2013 p.5). The row should say "1 or full basis", as row I5; stage 05 wording (P8).
(b) The delivered FLI reducer accepts only two columns (fli, Algorithm), which suits the 2D maps of that module, while the flows of this module need the basis of d = 4 columns in the sources' definition (F4). A paired FLI/OFLI comparison on F3 is not possible with the delivered FLI alone.
(c) `_config/architecture.md`, "Module map", writes the reducer interface as `(init, update!, finalize)`; the code's interface is `reducer_init`, `reducer_update`, `reducer_step`, `reducer_final` (the stream hands the reducer the orbit point and the deviation matrix; it does not hand it the system). The OFLI reducer therefore carries the flow as a construction argument and the run cannot check that it is the flow the map integrates (P10). Following the code.
(d) `_config/architecture.md`, "Layering" lists Integrators below Tangent below Indicators. In the code the Tangent module and the Indicators module import only Foundation and Systems (and Tangent, for Indicators): neither imports Integrators. The vector field of a flow is in Systems, so the OFLI reducer needs no new edge: Indicators importing Systems is allowed by the layering and is already present. Nothing to change; recorded so that a reader does not look for an Integrators import.
(e) Two meanings of tau: the integrator step size in the integrator-catalog spec and in the one-step map of the code, and the number of steps between handoffs in L1 S1 and in the run key `renorm_interval_tau`. This spec writes h for the step size.
(f) The benchmarks file lists no OFLI row; the task text of this module refers to "FLI/OFLI rows". Only the FLI row exists (F7).
(g) Maffione2013 p.5 names the first-order OFLI "OFLI2" once (F6); catalog row I7 uses OFLI2 for Barrio's second-order method.
(h) Darriba2012 Sect. 3.2 p.15 start of the 5-periodic orbit does not lie on its stated energy (F5).
(i) Darriba2012 and Maffione2013 describe the OFLI as oscillating around a constant on a periodic orbit while defining it as a supremum (F2).

## Open points

- P1. Direction removed in the discrete version (O10, O11). Recommended: A, the exact field at the numerical state, Euclidean projection; B rejected (it collapses in chaotic regions); C not available. Alternative to A: none usable without a read source for the modified Hamiltonian.
- P2. Metric of "orthogonal". Recommended: Euclidean in phase space, as the norm of the FLI. No source states it (F1). Alternatives: the symplectic (skew) complement of f, which is the tangent space of the energy surface and so removes an energy component instead; not defined in any read text.
- P3. Forms and number of columns. Recommended: a reducer generic in k from 1 to d; primary the full-basis form (k = d, the source definition of Darriba2012 and Maffione2013), secondary the first-column form (Fouchard2002, as reported); plus the companion unprojected maxima FLI_1 and FLI_B on the same columns (zero extra norm work), bitwise equal to the delivered FLI at k = 2 (criterion 10a), so that the paired comparison of F4 exists for F3. Alternative: no companion, and OFLI vs FLI comparisons left to a separate FLI on k = 4 which the delivered FLI does not support.
- P4. Running supremum versus instantaneous series (F2). Recommended: both emitted; the running supremum primary and the one scored; the instantaneous value reported (R4). Alternative: instantaneous only; rejected because it is not the delivered FLI's convention (fli Q1).
- P5. Conventions the sources do not print. Recommended: the s = 0 term included (value at most 0, fli P4 reading); the sup runs over handoffs only, tau = 20 steps by default for flows (one time unit at h = 1/20), tau = 1 gives the "every time step" of Maffione2013 p.5; a zero orthogonal component is a minus-infinity term counted, not an error; non-finite or zero field a health error.
- P6. The plateau loss on a periodic orbit (O11 ii, F3). Recommended: accept as a documented limit of a numerical flow and measure t* (criteria 4, 5, 11); report it with every periodic-orbit claim. Alternative: remove the loss by taking the modified field, which needs a read source and a derivation per method; rejected for this module.
- P7. OFLI2 and Barrio's sources (F1, F6). Recommended: row I7 stays catalog only; if the owner supplies Barrio2005 and Barrio2016 on disk, a first-order cross-check of O1 against Barrio's definition can be added as an amendment, not before.
- P8. Wording of catalog rows. Recommended: row I6 "1 or full basis", status `verified` at stage 05 with a note on the discrete-flow caveat (F3); the build-order row M5 already names `ofli`, nothing to rename; a benchmark row for the Darriba qualitative statements (F7), at stage 05, as "reported, no numbers".
- P9. Tolerances and horizons. All numbers marked proposal (criteria 4 to 7, 17; the 1e-6 levels, the 0.1 and 1.5 of criterion 5, the slope interval [0.7, 1.3], the allowance 4 / (window length)) are to be calibrated by a drafting run at stage 02; the closed forms and the algebra criteria (1, 2, 3a, 4a, 10, 14) are exact within rounding and are not negotiable.
- P10. Flow consistency. The reducer holds a flow; the map holds another. A mismatch gives wrong numbers silently. Recommended: the run record carries a flow identifier from each (criterion 14g) and the experiment layer compares them, at its stage, not here. Alternative: construct the reducer from the map.
- P11. Extended phase space integrators. Recommended: refuse (state dimension different from the flow's) with a clear error; no definition of the flow direction is attempted.
- P12. Study use. Thresholds (the source threshold t, the cap 1e20) are not applied by the module; a study pre-registers its own (`_config/numerics-policy.md`). The OFLI is delivered as a series; classification belongs to the study or analysis modules.
