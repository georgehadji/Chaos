# Spec: rli

Library layer: Indicators (`_config/architecture.md`, "Module map" row Indicators), with one additive construct in Systems under the recommended contract option (P1). Milestone M4 (decision D65). Status: approved at the stage 01 checkpoint, 2026-10-22 (D75): P1 (c) product map in Systems with a block frame and a small column reducer, P2 to P11 as proposed. Shared definitions are cited, not repeated: the Benettin spec as "L1 S1 to S8" (conventions, identical orbit S2, same-orbit bound B_S and amplification sum Psi_n S3 with Amendment D35, statistical rule S5, panel and starts S7, retained outputs S8) and "L1 E1 to E14" (equations); the lyap-two-trajectory spec as "L2 T1 to T9" (the shadow-orbit method, minimal-image separation T3, rounding model T5, the contract question and its answer D30); the sali spec as "sali A7", "sali criterion 16", "sali Amendments D67, D68" (frame check, D68 form of the GPU criterion, D_orb); the fli-ofli spec as "fli Q10", "fli Amendment D72" (schedule dependence, the replace option); the megno spec as "megno M6" (Abel form, memory), "megno Limits" (lifted arguments). Spec-local equation labels RL1 to RL13, findings F1 to F9, open points P1 to P11, recorded lines R1 to R8. Element types: Float64, Double64 (effective epsilon 2^-100) and BigFloat(256) for oracle runs; Float32 is out (D24). "n" counts map steps, handoffs fall at n = k tau, K = n / tau (L1 S1); "K_std" is the standard map parameter. "Dx" is the nominal initial separation, "Dx_r" the realized one (RL13). Page numbers are pages of the PDFs as fetched: arXiv PDF pages for SandorMaffione2016 (`data/literature/rli/arxiv_1501.07264.*`), printed pages (equal to the arXiv PDF pages) for Maffione2011a (`data/literature/fli-ofli/arxiv_1108.2196.*`).

## Scope

- Catalog row I9 (`_config/catalogs/indicators.md`, section A): the Relative Lyapunov Indicator, the absolute difference of the finite-time Lyapunov indicators of two neighbouring orbits and its running average, for the 2D symplectic maps of the P2 panel (Y1 standard map, Y5 linear maps). Delivered per orbit pair: the raw series DL_k at every handoff (RL2, RL4) and the smoothed value RLI at every handoff (RL3, RL4), the smoothed value being the primary. Section D of the catalog (Maffione2011a recommends FLI and RLI for wide phase-space scans) is tested by P2, not assumed.
- Prerequisites (catalog statuses read 2026-10-22): tangent-core, standard-map, precision, L1 and L2, sali (I2), fli-ofli (I5) and megno (I8) are `verified`. The stream, the seeded frame and its supplied-frame hook, health errors, scaled norms (Foundation, D69), compensated sums and the D66 / D71 / D72 chain rules are used as they are. New: a pair construct in Systems under P1(c), and its use only as a product of a verified map with itself.
- The reducer never changes the orbit pair. RL1 to RL4 hold for any dimension; criteria use d = 2.
- Out of scope: classification thresholds (pre-registered per study, `_config/numerics-policy.md`; the module never stops or caps an orbit; no floor is added to a zero RLI, the sources plot log10 of it and the study decides how to treat 0); the Hamiltonian flow case (Henon-Heiles and NFW examples of SandorMaffione2016, ODE integrators, M5); the 4D maps of the sources (SandorMaffione2016 Eqs. (3), (4); Maffione2011a vFSM), since the panel is 2D; a first-order form of the indicator through second variational equations (RL6 says why it would exist; no source defines it); sticky-orbit classification (F9); the normalised value RLI / Dx_r (a post-processing).
- Keys: no `numerics-defaults.toml` key proposed. The separation Dx is a recorded run parameter swept per study, never fixed (`_config/numerics-policy.md`, "two-trajectory perturbation size swept, not fixed"; the L2 precedent). tau stays `renorm_interval_tau`, default 1; the result depends on tau (RL10).

### Findings for the owner

- F1. Definition as printed. SandorMaffione2016 Eq. (1) p.3: DL(x*, t) = |L(x* + Dx*, t) - L(x*, t)|, with the finite-time Lyapunov indicator L(x, t) = (1/t) log(norm xi(t) / norm xi_0) (unnumbered display p.3), Dx* "the distance between the two initial condition vectors", called the RLI in the papers that introduced it. The smoothing is printed on p.4, unnumbered: <DL>(t) = (1/t) sum over i = 1..floor(t / Dt) of DL(x, i Dt), Dt the stepsize, and "in numerical experiments we always use the above smoothed value" (p.4). So the quantity the sources compute is the smoothed one; Eq. (1) is its raw series. The printed prefactor is 1/t, which equals the sample mean only when Dt = 1 (a map sampled at every iterate); for a sample spacing tau the literal formula is the sample mean divided by tau. This spec uses the sample mean over the handoffs (RL4, P4), which is the printed form for tau = 1.
- F2. What the sources do not fix, and what follows. (i) Whether both orbits start with the same deviation vector xi_0: not stated (p.3). (ii) The direction of Dx* and the norm: not stated; the interval 1e-14 to 1e-7 is given for norm(Dx*) (pp.3-4, 7), Maffione2011a p.4 uses 1e-12. (iii) The averaging convention for a sampling spacing other than one (F1). Derived consequence for (i): the printed regular-orbit law (RLI linear in norm(Dx*), p.4) needs the same xi_0. With independent initial vectors, DL does not tend to 0 as Dx* tends to 0, because the finite-time indicator itself depends on xi_0 at order ln(.)/n (L1 E5). With one xi_0, DL tends to 0 linearly (RL6). The same xi_0 is therefore the only reading in which the printed law is possible (RL5). Maffione2011a p.4 adds that the same initial deviation vectors must be kept along a whole sample; the vector used for the RLI there is not named.
- F3. Regular and chaotic behaviour as printed. Ordered orbits: the RLI is almost constant in time (SandorMaffione2016 p.5, Figs. 1(b), 2(b)) and linear in norm(Dx*) (pp.3-4, Fig. 4(a) p.6, Fig. 5(a) p.9; Maffione2011a Fig. 11 p.21, "the correspondence is linear" p.20, and a threshold that must scale with Dx, p.21). Chaotic orbits: the RLI grows fast, reaches a maximum or a constant, and is practically invariant in norm(Dx*) (SandorMaffione2016 pp.4-5, 7, 12-13, Fig. 4(b); Maffione2011a p.20); the stated reason is that for a chaotic orbit the RLI "practically measures the average width of the oscillation of the LI curve" (p.7). Numbers printed for maps are for the 4D maps and for flows: the gap between chaotic and ordered values is 9 to 10 orders of magnitude in the 2D and 4D examples of Figs. 1, 2 (p.5; the separation of those figures is not stated); for the Henon-Heiles flow at Dx = 1e-14 the chaotic orbit has RLI about 0.1 and the ordered ones 10^-13.5, 10^-12 and 10^-10 (p.8). No number printed for the 2D map can be matched exactly; the 2D map (2) is reproduced as recorded line R1.
- F4. Sandor2004 was not read. Its full text is not held (`data/literature/rli/` holds SandorMaffione2016 only). It is the catalog source of row I9. Every statement here that the sources attribute to it (the introduction of the RLI, the linear law, the choice of Dx, the threshold method) is taken only as reported in SandorMaffione2016 and Maffione2011a, and no equation or page of it is cited. Open point P9: the owner may supply the PDF to `data/literature/rli/`; a spec amendment would then confirm F1 and F2.
- F5. A difference of nearly equal numbers. For an ordered orbit DL is about c Dx with c of order 0.02 to 0.1 here (drafting check, K_std = 0.5, n = 1e2 to 2e3), while each L is of order ln(n)/n. At Dx = 1e-12 the subtraction therefore loses about 11 digits of the 16 of Float64; at 1e-14 about 13. Three separate numerical limits follow (RL11, RL12, RL13): the rounding of the two log sums (a same-orbit bound, L1 S3 per orbit), the rounding of the two orbits themselves (the pair separation is a quantity of order eps times the coordinate scale, 9e-16 per coordinate at the edge of the wrapped range; at Dx = 1e-14 that is 11 units in the last place), and the start itself (the nominal separation is not representable, so the realized one is recorded). The worst-case bounds are far above the typical error: in the drafting check the bound B_R of RL11 is 1.7e-12 at n = 2000 against a measured Float64 error of the RLI of 8e-17 (wrapped; 7e-18 lifted) and of DL at n = 2000 of 4e-16, and the first-order resolution number of RL12 calls Dx = 1e-12 unresolved at that n (Theta = 43) while the Float64 value is within 0.5 percent of a 60-digit value. The criteria therefore use the bounds as envelopes and add statistical lines against BigFloat orbits (criterion 7). Double64 lowers the floor by a factor of about 3e14 (epsilon 2^-100 against 2^-52), so Dx down to about 1e-26 is meaningful in wrapped mode; it does not remove the Double64 sine and cosine floor at large lifted arguments (megno Limits, L1 Amendment D35), which is not measured for the RLI.
- F6. Lifted against wrapped state is decisive here, not a sensitivity. A lifted Float64 coordinate of magnitude 1e4 (n = 1e4 steps of a rotational orbit at p = 1) has a unit in the last place of 1.8e-12, larger than Dx = 1e-12: the pair cannot hold the separation. The state held wrapped with winding counts (D28) keeps the coordinates in [0, 2 pi) where the spacing is at most 8.9e-16. The recommended default is wrapped (P5); the separation is read as the minimal-image difference (L2 T3).
- F7. The source map and the repository map are the same map. SandorMaffione2016 Eq. (2) p.4, q' = q + y, y' = y - nu sin q', is the repository standard map p' = p + K_std sin x, x' = x + p' with K_std = -nu, x = q, and p_{n+1} = y_n (derived; checked exactly over 50 iterates in the drafting check). K_std -> -K_std is the shift x -> x + pi. So the starts (2, 0) (ordered) and (3, 0) (chaotic) at nu = 0.5 of Fig. 1 (p.5) are, in the repository's (x, p) at K_std = -0.5, x_0 = q_0 and p_0 = y_0 + nu sin q_0; at K_std = 0.5 the same orbits shifted by pi in x. R1 does this reproduction.
- F8. Overlap with L1. Each L of the pair is the L1 value X of one orbit with the same unit start (L1 E2, E8), so s_A and s_B are the L1 partial sums of two orbits. The RLI is the running mean of |s_B - s_A| / (k tau) and, like MEGNO, carries memory of the whole path (RL8). What it adds for claim C3 is that it uses a second orbit: it measures how much the finite-time estimator varies between neighbouring initial conditions, which a single-orbit series cannot show. It is not a function of the L1 value at the final time of one orbit. Indicators may not import a Lyapunov module (peers), so the reducer keeps its own log sums, bitwise those of L1 (criterion 8).
- F9. Source-reported weaknesses, recorded for P2, not requirements: the threshold depends on Dx and must be set with a group of regular orbits close to a hyperbolic object (SandorMaffione2016 pp.8-9; Maffione2011a pp.20-22, "an iterative process might be advisable"); the RLI does not separate sticky from chaotic orbits in the 4D map (SandorMaffione2016 pp.15-16, 36); computing time ratio 0.89 (Henon-Heiles) and 0.44 (NFW) of a Lyapunov spectrum (p.24, Table 3, flows). Maffione2011a Table 1 p.4 gives the threshold 1e-12 at Dx = 1e-12 for the 4D map, Table 2 p.5 the percentage of chaotic orbits (RLI: 49.93, 71.77, 72.08, 72.34 at n = 1e3, 5e3, 1e4, 1e5); these are for the 4D map and are not comparable on the 2D panel.

### Contract question P1: how a second orbit enters the stream

Facts. (i) A reducer is called at the handoffs with the step count, the state and the deviation matrix of one orbit; it receives neither the system, nor the orbit id, nor the initial point (tangent-core Contract and protocol; "no reducer sees two orbits"). (ii) The stream advances the orbit independently of every reducer; a reducer may replace only the deviation matrix. (iii) The state may be longer than the tangent dimension (tangent-core Amendment D28, A5) and a supplied frame is allowed, one matrix per orbit (sali Amendment D67). (iv) There is exactly one per-step update implementation (tangent-core AC14). (v) L2 faced the same question (L2 P1, D30): the stream-amendment candidate was rejected, L2 got its own per-orbit loop in Lyapunov and D34 accepted the duplicated chunk loop. (vi) D66 and D71: indicators that need separate access to columns share one chain; other estimators run "in a separate pass over the bitwise identical orbit".
- (a) Experiments runs two ensembles, orbit and shifted orbit, with the verified L1 reducer on each (same seed and orbit ids, so the same xi_0, RL5), stores the log sums at every handoff, and pairs them afterwards (RL4). No new reducer, Tangent or Systems code. Costs: twice the steps; O(K) stored values per orbit per ensemble, about 1.6 MB per pair at n = 1e5 with all handoffs (about 1.6 TB for 1e6 pairs); a thinned schedule changes the smoothing convention (P4); a final value is impossible without the stored series; Experiments (verified) gets a pairing mode; Indicators cannot do the pairing (it may not import L1's module), so the module `rli` would be one pure function.
- (b) A reducer carrying its own shadow orbit and stepping the map. The protocol allows it only if the reducer value holds a copy of the system; the state would hold the shadow point and its vector (isbits, so a GPU kernel can run it, and it can allocate nothing). The orbit is unchanged, as required, because the shadow is the reducer's own state. Costs: the reducer has no initial point at its init and sees the orbit only from the first handoff, so the shadow would start at n = tau, not at n = 0 (a different definition, shifted by tau steps, with the base vector at that time as the shared start); the reducer runs tau steps inside each handoff, a second stepping loop (the single hot loop and AC14 are meant to forbid exactly this); the per-handoff cost is then O(tau), which the launch-budget model of D33 and D44 does not assume; two code paths to verify for rounding. Not recommended.
- (c) Recommended. The product of the map with itself as one system of twice the state length and block-diagonal tangent (RL5), run on the existing stream with k = 2 columns, a supplied block frame (column 1 is (xi_0, 0), column 2 is (0, xi_0)), and a small column reducer that reads the two column norms (RL4, Algorithm). Everything stays inside the protocol: no tangent-core change, one per-step update, one kernel, the pair is one "orbit" of the product system, each block bitwise the single-orbit evolution (criterion 8). Reducer state O(1). Costs: a new additive construct in Systems (the product of a verified map with itself; small spec and tests, no verified criterion changes); a caller-side frame check (sali Amendment D67 form, criterion 11); twice the orbit work and a doubled tangent dimension (a block-aware tangent action costs twice, a dense one four times; a stage 03 matter); the pair run is a pass of its own, so for P2 the single-orbit indicators (L1, SALI, FLI, MEGNO) and the RLI cost two passes over a bitwise identical base orbit, the D66 precedent (recorded line R7); chain rule below.
- (d) Amend tangent-core so a reducer may own extra state components (the L2 P1(a) candidate, rejected by D30). Re-opens a verified module. Not recommended.
Chain rule (D66, D71, D72). On the pair stream the RLI reducer reads both arriving columns before normalisation, keeps its own sums, and replaces the columns by unit vectors with a diagonal factor (each column stays in its own block) when alone or last; as a non-replacing reader it goes before the one replacer, as FLI and MEGNO do. The caller builds and records the chain; the reducer cannot check it. Which chains are meaningful on the pair stream: RLI, and MEGNO (its two columns are then orbit A and orbit B, each with xi_0); the FLI basis maximum mixes the two orbits and is not meaningful; SALI and GALI compare the two columns as vectors of one orbit and are not defined on it (the blocks are orthogonal by construction). Criteria 1 to 7, 9 to 11 and 13 hold under (a) and (c) with the pairing written for each; criteria 8 and 12 are specific to (c); this spec writes them all for (c).

### Open points

- P2. Initial vectors (F2). (a) The same seeded column xi_0 (the first column of the seeded frame of the pair id, L1 S2) for both orbits. Recommended: it is the only reading in which the printed law is possible and it makes the linear and K = 0 cases exactly 0. (b) Independent vectors per orbit: not recommended (F2). (c) A caller-supplied vector, the same for both: allowed through the supplied frame, same rule.
- P3. Separation direction and norm. The sources fix neither. Recommended: the direction u is xi_0 (the first seeded unit vector of the pair id, the L2 precedent), Euclidean norm of the minimal-image coordinate difference, unscaled (both standard-map coordinates are angle-like and of the same size); Dx is the nominal norm, Dx_r the realized one (RL13) is recorded and used for any normalisation. Alternatives: the second seeded column (orthogonal to xi_0), a coordinate axis. The direction enters only at the first order (RL6); R5 measures the spread.
- P4. Averaging convention. Recommended: the sample mean over the handoffs 1..K, equal to the printed form for tau = 1 (F1); the printed 1/t prefactor taken literally is the mean divided by tau and is not used. A sample at every step needs tau = 1.
- P5. State mode. Recommended default: wrapped with winding counts (D28); lifted is a sensitivity run with the resolution line R8 (F6).
- P6. Outputs. Recommended: both the raw DL_k and the smoothed RLI at every handoff (storage schedule chosen by the caller, L1 S8), linear values (no logarithm, no floor), plus the final log sums with compensation, Dx, Dx_r and the final pair distance read from the final states.
- P7. Bound constants and the resolution rule. The constants 8, 4, 4 eps in RL11, the coefficient (|K_std| + 4) in RL12 and the proposals of criteria 4, 5 and 7 are proposals; stage 04 reports the realized ratios. Recommended: keep the envelope bounds and the statistical criterion 7; the study also records a per-orbit Float64 against Double64 comparison on a subsample to flag unresolved orbits.
- P8. Chain composition on the pair stream (P1 chain rule) and the cost accounting of R7: recommended as written.
- P9. Sandor2004 (F4): recommended that the owner supplies the PDF; no stage is blocked by its absence.
- P10. Default Dx for P2. Recommended: a sweep (Float64 wrapped: 1e-12, 1e-10, 1e-8; Dx = 1e-14 flagged as quantised to about 10 percent, RL13), with 1e-12 as the comparison value with Maffione2011a p.4; Double64 sweeps down to 1e-20 at most until the trig floor is measured. Thresholds are pre-registered per study and scaled with Dx_r for ordered orbits (F3).
- P11. Where the pair construct lives. Recommended: Systems, as a generic product of a map with itself with its own short spec section appended to the standard-map family, written at stage 03 from RL5; the owner approves the addition to a verified layer.

## Equations

Notation. A and B are the two orbits, x*_A the base point (the ensemble's initial point), x*_B = x*_A + Dx u the shadow start; xi_0 the unit deviation vector of both; w_i(m) the deviation vector of orbit i after m steps (one-step tangent at its own point, L1 E1); alpha_{i,k} the scaled Euclidean norm of the arriving column of orbit i at handoff k (L1 E3), a_{i,k} = ln alpha_{i,k}; s_i(k) = ln norm(w_i(k tau)) = sum of a_{i,j}, j <= k (L1 E2, E3, E8), s_i(0) = 0; t_k = k tau; L_i(k) = s_i(k) / t_k. L_sigma = ln sigma(K_std) of L1 E14. "ref" = BigFloat(256) at the realized orbit points (L1 S3).

The relative error of the separation vector at step n is at most Theta_n = (|K_std| + 4) M_state eps Phi_n / Dx_r, Phi_n = sum over j = 1..n of norm(M_n ... M_j) / norm(w^(u)(n)), w^(u) the tangent evolution of u; the coefficient (|K_std| + 4) is the two-orbit constant of L2 T5. For a hyperbolic orbit Phi_n is O(1); for a shear-like orbit Phi_n grows like n (about 3.4 n at n = 2000 in the drafting check, against n / 2 for an ideal shear). The pair "realizes" Dx_r at n when Theta_n <= 1/2 (the validity limit E_ro <= 0.5 of L2 T5). Worst case, pessimistic by about 4 orders for ordered orbits (F5) | derived; precision spec Equations item 3; L2 T5 |
| RL13 | Realized separation (derived). In the element type x*_B = x*_A + Dx u is rounded per coordinate, so Dx_r = norm of the minimal-image difference of the stored initial states (exact for close points, Sterbenz) differs from Dx by at most sqrt(2) eps M_state (two roundings: the sum and, in wrapped mode, the reduction; M_state of L1 S1). At M_state = 2 pi this is 2e-15: 0.2 percent of 1e-12 and 20 percent of 1e-14. Dx_r is what the indicator uses and records. In wrapped mode the plain coordinate difference is wrong when the pair straddles 0 mod 2 pi; the minimal-image reduction is correct (L2 T3) | derived; L2 T3; precision spec Equations item 3 |

## Algorithm

Per orbit pair (pair id j, run seed s), on the stream with the pair construct (P1(c)), k = 2, d = 4 (state length 8 in wrapped mode):
1. Initialisation. xi_0 is the first seeded unit deviation vector of (s, j) (tangent-core, "Initial deviation vectors"; L1 S2; it does not depend on k). The shadow start is x*_A + Dx u, u = xi_0 (P3), on the lifted coordinates in the element type; in wrapped mode it is reduced and the winding counts are set so that the lifted separation is exactly the minimal-image one (L2 T3). Dx_r is computed from the stored states (RL13). If it is zero, not finite, or outside [Dx/2, 2 Dx], the run is rejected before any work with a numerical health error of kind separation, naming the pair id (L2 Algorithm item 1). The supplied frame is the block frame of RL5; the caller checks it before the stream (criterion 11, sali Amendment D67, fli Amendment D72 item 7): both columns unit within 8 eps, each supported in one block, the two block vectors equal.
2. At each handoff k (n = k tau), for i in {A, B}: alpha = scaled Euclidean norm of the arriving column i; if zero or not finite, a numerical health error naming the deviation matrix, the pair id, the column (which identifies the orbit) and the handoff step (as sali Algorithm item 2). a = ln alpha. Add a to the compensated sum s_i (the same operations as L1, so s_i is bitwise the L1 sum of that orbit). DL_k = abs((s_B + comp_B) - (s_A + comp_A)) / t_k. Add DL_k to the compensated sum Q; RLI = Q / K.
3. Replacement per the chain rule: columns replaced by unit vectors with a diagonal factor when alone or last; a non-replacing reader otherwise.
4. Emit DL_k and RLI at every handoff (schedule chosen by the caller, L1 S8). At the end: s_A, s_B, Q with compensation terms, the handoff count, tau, Dx, Dx_r, the final pair distance (from the final states), the chain position.

Representation. DL_k and RLI are stored as computed, never clipped, capped, floored or replaced; zero is a valid value. No early stop. Parameters recorded per run: Dx, Dx_r, the direction rule, tau, element type, state mode (wrapped with counts, or lifted), seed, frame source, chain position, and the convention tag "same xi_0, shadow along xi_0, mean over the handoffs 1..K" (P2 to P4).

## Invariants

- I1 Range: 0 <= DL_k <= 2 L_sigma (K_std) and 0 <= RLI <= max DL_k (RL8); RLI carries memory and is not a function of DL at the final handoff (RL8).
- I2 Constant tangent: for Y5 and the K_std = 0 shear every DL_k and RLI is exactly 0, bitwise (RL5(d)).
- I3 Symmetry: exchanging the two orbits (start points and block order) gives bitwise equal DL_k and RLI; a sign change of xi_0 changes nothing bitwise (norms).
- I4 The two initial columns are bitwise the same vector in their blocks (RL5).
- I5 The pair does not change either orbit; each block is bitwise the single-orbit evolution of its point, and s_A, s_B are bitwise the L1 sums of single-orbit runs with that xi_0 (L1 S2).
- I6 Regular law: for an ordered orbit RLI / Dx_r is independent of Dx up to O(Dx_r) (RL6). Chaotic: RLI independent of Dx up to the n* term (RL7).
- I7 Schedule: RLI_tau and RLI_1 differ by at most the bound of RL10.
- I8 Orbit independence and execution invariance: pair j's series depends only on (system, parameters, initial point, Dx, direction rule, seed, j, tau, N, state mode, frame), not on threads, chunk or position in the ensemble; across backends only to criterion 15. The hot loop allocates nothing after warm-up (BigFloat excepted).
- I9 Error: RLI is within B_R of the reference at every n (RL11); the realized separation is recorded (RL13); the pair realizes Dx_r where Theta_n <= 1/2 (RL12).

## Compute

Policy `_config/architecture.md`, "Compute" (D17, D25). Per pair, data-parallel, fused into the stream's single kernel: twice the orbit work of a single orbit, two scaled norms, two logs, three compensated adds and a division per handoff; no dense factorization, so no "reducer is CPU-only" reason. Memory per pair from the tangent-core formula with the pair state and d = 4, k = 2: 144 bytes in Float64 (state 4, W 8, sums 4, plus 16) for the lifted standard map, 176 in wrapped mode (state 8); a single orbit with k = 2 is 96 and 112. Reducer state, proposed: three compensated sums (6 values) and the handoff count and a failure code: 64 bytes in Float64, 112 in Double64, added to the formula as reducer bytes (megno Compute; stage 03 reads the real size). GPU candidate for Float64 and Double64 (isbits); BigFloat is CPU-only with the reason "kernel does not compile for the element type" on a GPU request; GTX 1050, compute capability 6.1, weak Float64 (K15): GPU is the default for an element type only after the benchmark records `gpu_faster` (tangent-core AC13; per-method record D54, taken on the pair chain). The step-chunked relaunch of tangent-core Amendment D44 carries the pair's states, both columns and the reducer state, all of which the relaunch already carries. CPU-vs-GPU agreement: criterion 15 (D68 form). CI has no GPU: CPU backend.

External oracle, verification environment `verify/` (D21, D27).
