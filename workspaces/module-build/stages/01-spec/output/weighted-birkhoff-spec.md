# Spec: weighted-birkhoff

Library layer: Indicators (`_config/architecture.md`, "Module map" row Indicators, which lists WBA). Milestone M4 (`_config/build-order.md`, row M4). Status: approved at the stage 01 checkpoint, 2026-10-27 (D83), with open points P1 to P10 settled as recommended. Decision D80 made I13 its own module after naff-fma. Shared definitions are cited, not repeated: the naff-fma spec as "NF" (weight and normalised sums NF4, weighted rotation number NF5, weighted phase advance NF6, its criteria 8, 10, 15, Amendments D81, D82), the Benettin spec as "L1 S1 to S8" (panel, starts, regular-class screening S7), the standard-map spec (items 9, 10, Amendment D28), the rli spec (recorded lines, D77), the sali spec (CPU-GPU form, D68). Spec-local labels: WB1 to WB18, F1 to F10, P1 to P10, I1 to I9, R1 to R8. Element types: Float64, Double64 (effective epsilon 2^-100), BigFloat(256) for oracles; Float32 is out (D24). "eps" is the machine epsilon of the element type; "N" the samples of one window; a sample is taken at a handoff, tau map steps apart.

Texts, fetched for this draft into `data/literature/weighted-birkhoff/` (git-ignored, `arxiv_<id>.pdf` and `.txt`; titles and authors checked against the bibliography; published versions not compared): Das2016 (arXiv:1512.07286 v2; read in full; "p.N" is the PDF page); SanderMeiss2020 (arXiv:2001.00086 v1; read: Sect. 1, 2, 3.2, 6; the Farey algorithm and Sect. 4, 5 not read; printed pages equal PDF pages); DuignanMeiss2023 (arXiv:2212.10783 v1; read: Sect. 1, 2, 4, 5.1; Sect. 5.2, 5.3, 6 only searched; proofs not read); DasYorke2018 (arXiv:1506.06810 v5; read in full); TongLi2025 (arXiv:2408.09398 v3; read: abstract, Sect. 1, 4, the statement of 6, 7; proofs not read). Das2017 and Bazzani2023 are the held naff-fma texts (arXiv v3 and v1; page numbers as in the naff-fma spec, published Das2017 pages in its Amendment D81 table). Everything is "as printed" unless marked "derived" or "reader inference"; derived items are not citable. "Dry run" numbers come from throwaway scripts in the scratchpad (not in the repository, not sources); they decided the tolerances.

## Scope

- Row I13 (weighted Birkhoff averages, convergence rate). Its source column lists Das2017 only, which prints no comparison of two segments (F1); stage 05 should add Das2016, SanderMeiss2020, DuignanMeiss2023, DasYorke2018.
- Prerequisites (catalog statuses read 2026-10-27): tangent-core, standard-map (Y1 lifted and wrapped, Y5), precision, experiments, the verified Indicators reducers and naff-fma (I12, I12b) are `verified`. Used as they are: the stream and handoff protocol, winding counts (D28, D31), the one-replacer rule (D71, D72), the verified weight in its mirrored-index form (NF4, naff-fma D81 item 10), compensated sums, and the weighted phase advance reducer (E2), which already is the weighted average of one observable (F3).
- New (P1): (n1) a stream reader that forms, for K caller-chosen smooth observables of the orbit point and H horizons, the weighted averages of the windows [0, N) and [N, 2N) in O(1) state; (n2) the same sums as a pure function of a stored series, any float type, optional weight vector (recorded lines); (n3) the pair index: signed difference, absolute and relative digits, floor and flag; (n4) the observable rules (P6) and the kinds the study needs. Nothing new in Foundation, Systems, Integrators, Tangent; no series feed (P10).
- Never changes the orbit or the deviation matrix; replaces no column (I7).
- Out of scope (P3, P4): weighted FLI (Bazzani2023 Eq. (12)), extrapolation and filtering (RuthBindel2024), the parameterization method (BlessingMirelesJames2024), theory papers, the rationality test of the rotation number, the multiscale profile, abstention and the coarse-graining arm (research idea 25: a study), flows (M5), thresholds (preregistered per study, catalog header rule).
- Keys: none. Constants here (minimum window 16, floor multiplier 32, tolerance factors) are proposals (P9), not keys.

### Findings for the owner

- F1. Who prints what. Das2017 prints the weight (Eq. (3), p.2), the normalised average (Eq. (4), p.3), five weights and their orders (Eqs. (15), (16), pp.10 to 11), the rotation number as a weighted average (Eqs. (13), (14), p.10) and weighted Lyapunov exponents (Sect. 3.6, pp.20 to 21); a search finds no two-segment comparison and no chaos test in it. The indicator is printed by Das2016 (Delta_N, zeros_N, Eq. (3), p.3), SanderMeiss2020 (dig_T, Eq. (9), p.7) and DuignanMeiss2023 (absdig, reldig, dig, Eqs. (15) to (17), p.10). The brief and the catalog name Das2017 for it; that is wrong.
- F2. Window pairs, as printed. Das2016 p.3: "the first N iterates" and "the second N iterates", Delta_N = WB_N(f)(x) - WB_N(f)(T^N x), zeros_N = -log10 |Delta_N|; f = sin(x + y), N = 20 000 (Fig. 1, p.2), three observables in Fig. 2 (p.2); of 12 086 diagonal starts with zeros_N > 18 at N = 20 000, 99.8 percent were quasiperiodic by zeros_N >= 30 at N = 10^8 (p.3). SanderMeiss2020 p.7: segments "{1, ..., T} and {T + 1, ..., 2T}" in the text, while Eq. (9) uses the start f^T(z) for the second (iterates T to 2T - 1 by their Eq. (6)): one sample apart (R8); h = cos(2 pi x), T = 10^4 (Fig. 3, p.8), 2 x 10^4 (Fig. 4, p.9); over 500 000 starts dig has peaks near 2 and 15 and a minimum near 5, cutoff 5.5 "to err on the side of false positives of chaos" (p.7, Eq. (23), p.19); the three tests agree R >= 98 percent, TSS 0.96, doubling T changes 0.4 percent of labels (Lyapunov: 0.8) (p.12). DuignanMeiss2023 pp.9 to 10: averages from x_0 and from x_T over equal intervals; absdig (15), reldig = the same difference over half the sum of the two absolute averages (16), dig = their maximum (17); flows, h = p; dig_1000 < 5 (Eq. (21), p.12; chaotic about 2 to 3, regular above 7). Reader inference: every pair is two consecutive equal segments of one orbit; no source prints a gap, an overlap, or a rule combining several observables.
- F3. E2 already is a weighted Birkhoff average, of one observable: the lifted increments with the weight of NF4 are Das2017 Eq. (14) (p.10), SanderMeiss2020 Eqs. (3), (21) (pp.2, 18; Omega(y) = y) and DuignanMeiss2023 Eq. (20) (p.11; h = p). For h = p the sources' Delta is 2 pi times the E2 frequency difference before circle reduction (derived; dry run: the two sums agree to 0.5 eps S). E2 cannot average another observable (circle-reduced frequency, floor in frequency units, verified code): a new reader (P1), not a flag on E2.
- F4. Thresholds printed, none adopted: 5.5 (SanderMeiss2020, T = 10^4), 4 as best agreement with the Lyapunov test and 3.5 to 6 giving R about 98 percent (p.12), 5 (DuignanMeiss2023, flows), 18 and 30 (Das2016). They depend on observable, T, precision; they are inputs to a study's preregistration only. The module returns digits and flags.
- F5. Observables. DasYorke2018 Theorem 1.1 (p.4) and Theorem 3.1 (p.9): T, f and the weight C-infinity on the manifold of the orbit, f with values in a finite-dimensional vector space (so K observables are one statement), Diophantine rotation, no gain for chaos; periodicity is not a hypothesis. Used: cos(2 pi x) (SanderMeiss2020), sin(x + y) (Das2016), p (DuignanMeiss2023 Eq. (20); SanderMeiss2020 Omega(y) = y), complex exponentials for Fourier coefficients (Das2017 Eq. (20), p.14). Dry run, rotation number sqrt 2 - 1, Float64, regular orbit: cos 2 pi theta gives 17.8 digits at N = 16 000; the wrapped angle (a sawtooth, discontinuous) gives 2.3, 2.8, 3.5, 6.0 digits at N = 250, 1000, 4000, 16 000; the lifted angle (non periodic) has an average growing with N and digits of -2 to -4. So the wrapped coordinate is not an observable, sine and cosine of a coordinate are, and a lifted coordinate is where the map lives on a cylinder (the momentum): naff-fma F5 and P4 again.
- F6. Double precision. Printed: w^[2] beats w^[1] at 30 digits "but not in 15-digit precision" (Das2016 p.3; Das2017 p.11); in quadruple arithmetic the error falls below 1e-31 then grows with N "apparently due to accumulated round-off" (Das2017 p.11, Fig. 6 caption p.12), plateau about 1e-30 (Fig. 9, p.15); in double the error reaches 1e-15 at T = 10^4, no super-convergence, rates n = 2 to 5.5 (SanderMeiss2020 p.6); regular dig centred near 14, "nearing the maximum possible for a double precision computation" (p.7); flow rotation number exact to double by T = 2000, integration in multiple precision (DuignanMeiss2023 pp.10, 11). Dry runs: (a) exact samples (BigFloat(256)), rotation sqrt 2 - 1, cos 2 pi theta: |WB_N| = 1.2e-11, 1.6e-16, 8.7e-26, 4.9e-35, 5.6e-65 at N = 125, 250, 500, 1000, 4000 with w^[1] (the exp(-c sqrt N) law of a trigonometric polynomial, TongLi2025 Theorem 6.1(III), p.21), 2.9e-19 at N = 125 with w^[2]; (b) Float64 sums differ from BigFloat sums of the same samples by at most 1.0 eps S with compensated (Neumaier) summation up to N = 4e5 and by 8 to 46 eps S, growing with N, with a plain sum: the printed growth is reproduced by plain summation and absent with compensation (derived; the cause in Das2017 is not stated); (c) on standard-map orbits the Float64 regular digits plateau at 14 to 17 because the orbit is rounded, not the sums (the BigFloat sum of the same samples gives the same digits to 0.1 below 16). SanderMeiss2020 Fig. 3 reproduced (1000 starts on x = 0.321, h = cos q, T = 10^4, Float64): k = 1.0 bimodal, modes at 1 to 2 and 13 to 15, 53 percent below 5.5; k = 2.0, 99.9 percent below 5.5.
- F7. Weights. Das2017 Eq. (15): equal, t(1 - t), sin^2(pi t), exponential; p.11 orders N^-1, N^-2, N^-3, faster than any power; for sin^2 the exponent is printed N^-3 (p.11), N^-2.5 (Fig. 7, p.13), N^-3.0 (Fig. 9, p.15) and O(T^-4) by SanderMeiss2020 (p.6); DasYorke2018 p.9: sin^2 is C^1 only. TongLi2025 p.14 lists sin^2 (Laskar), x(1 - x) (Das et al.), the "tuned filter" of Ruth and Bindel (as reported, not read) and w_{p,q}, p = q = 2 used by Das et al. and Calleja, and says weights that are not C-infinity give polynomial rates. DuignanMeiss2023 width: g_w(s) = C exp(-w / (s (1 - s))) (Eq. (22), p.14, checked on the rendered page; C = 142.2503758 at w = 1, which I checked as 1 over the integral of the weight): w = 1 "a reasonable choice", isolated spikes of dig in w that move with T and rotation number (Fig. 7, p.15), no optimum; TongLi2025 p.16 repeats the family, notes the control coefficient blows up for large w. Das's p is the exponent of [t(1 - t)]^-p, a different family.
- F8. Weighted FLI. Bazzani2023 Eq. (12) (p.5, checked on the rendered page): FLI^WB_n = sum_{i=0}^{n-1} w(i/n) ln ||y_i - x_i||, w of Eq. (11) (p.4); "converges faster ... at least in the case of regular orbits"; slopes -0.91 against -0.78 (regular) and -0.72 against -0.60 (chaotic) on the 4D map (Fig. 3, p.10): a gain of about 0.13, not super-convergence. Eq. (12) has no normalising denominator; with the printed w (integral 0.00703) it is 0.007 n times the weighted mean and has no "common limit" with FLI/n (reader inference; the normalised sums of Das2017 Eq. (4) are meant). Das2017 pp.20 to 21: weighted Lyapunov exponent of 2D torus maps, rates N^-20.0 and N^-16.7, "without proof". It reads the deviation matrix, not the orbit point (P3).
- F9. Chaotic rate: O(N^-1/2) or slower (Das2016 p.3; SanderMeiss2020 pp.3, 6), "at most O(N^-1)" (TongLi2025 p.3), "much slower" (DuignanMeiss2023 p.9). Dry runs give 1 to 3 digits for chaotic starts from N = 10^3 to 2 x 10^4 for every observable; the statements are not used as a law.
- F10. Corrections to the brief and the catalog. (a) Das2017 is not the source of the comparison (F1). (b) "Super-convergence on tori" is not seen in Float64 (F6): only a plateau at 14 to 17 digits; the printed statements are for exact or 30-digit arithmetic. (c) The "digits threshold" has four printed values (F4). (d) The weighted FLI needs the deviation matrix; it is not an orbit-only reader (F8). (e) "Weight width in DuignanMeiss2023" is Eq. (22), not Das's p.

### Open points

- P1. Reducer or reuse. Facts: E2 hard-wires increments, circle reduction and a floor in frequency units (F3); the pure function alone needs the series, 8 K 2N bytes per orbit (67 MB at K = 4, N = 2^20; naff-fma F8); the stream still carries one deviation column (naff-fma P1 fact iii). Recommended: (a) a new reader reducer for K observables and H horizons (n1), plus the pure function (n2), the index (n3) and the observable rules (n4); E2 and its code are not edited; the weight, the compensated sums and the run protocol are reused, the update of A and B is a few lines. Alternatives: (b) generalise E2 (rejected: the circle reduction is wrong for a general average and verified code is reopened); (c) pure function only (rejected: memory). Dry run: for the lifted standard map at tau = 1, the average of f(x) = p + K sin q (lifted p) equals the E2 sum of lifted increments to 0.07 eps S (K = 0.5) and 0.5 eps S (K = 0.9), N = 500 to 4000.
- P2. The comparison. Recommended: the pair [0, N), [N, 2N) of the handoff series (handoffs 1 to 2N; this is the text reading of SanderMeiss2020 p.7); per observable and horizon return avg_1, avg_2, Delta = avg_1 - avg_2 (signed, never clipped), absdig and reldig and their maximum (WB15), the scale S, the flag. Floor phi = 32 eps S with S the largest |f| of the 2N samples of that horizon (a running maximum, O(1)): the same multiplier as the naff-fma index floor (P8 there); it sits at 14.15 digits for S = 1 in Float64, inside the observed plateau of 14 to 17 (F6), and above the realized algebra error of 1.0 eps S (P9). Digits against the floor, never a stop or a cap above. reldig only where m > phi (DuignanMeiss2023 p.12: poor where the average is near 0). Alternatives: absolute digits only (Das2016, SanderMeiss2020); no floor and Delta = 0 giving infinite digits (rejected: breaks tables and quantiles); a window gap or start offset (a study choice, the pure function forms any pair from a stored series). No threshold in the module (F4).
- P3. Weighted FLI. Recommended: not in this module and not in this run. It needs the column norms before renormalisation (reader, chain rule D71, D72), is the weighted mean of the per-step log growth that L1 and FLI already form, so it is a weighted finite-time exponent (claims C1, C2: the Lyapunov side), its printed evidence is one 4D-map figure with a gain of 0.13 in the exponent and an unnormalised formula (F8), and SanderMeiss2025 (weighted exponents; not held) is its natural source. Alternative: a second reducer here (same weights, one more state, chain tests, a normalisation decision), worth it only if the owner wants the weighted FLI as a P2 comparator. If built later, it must use the normalised weights.
- P4. Out of scope, with reasons. RuthBindel2024 (reduced rank extrapolation; "tuned filter" as reported by TongLi2025 p.14; abstract only): a different estimator, a candidate baseline module. BlessingMirelesJames2024 (parameterization method, Newton solve; abstract only). TongLi2025 (read; rates quoted), TongLi2026a, TongLi2026b (abstracts only; the latter belongs to naff-fma I12): theory. The rationality test of SanderMeiss2020 Sect. 3 (Eq. (22), cutoff 0.3375, p.19): a classifier on the E2 rotation number, not a rate indicator. Fourier coefficients and conjugacy (Das2017 Sect. 3.4; DasYorke2018 Corollary 2.2) and the embedding continuation (Das2016 p.4): other products of the same average. Idea 25 (multiscale profile, slope alpha_N of log E_N, abstention, low-pass arm): a study; the module supplies Delta per observable and horizon in one pass with floors flagged, so the study may truncate a slope at the floor; nothing more. Flows (DuignanMeiss2023): M5; the handoff weights are a quadrature, not their integrated form (Eq. (18), p.10).
- P5. Weights. Recommended: the verified family w^[p] (NF4), default p = 1, p >= 1 an option as in E2; the normalisation is the sum of the sampled weights (Das2017 Eq. (4)); the same weights in both windows, so both share B. The pure function also takes a weight vector, so equal, t(1 - t), sin^2 and the width form of Eq. (22) are recorded lines (R2, R7), never reducer options. Alternatives: build the width w (one real parameter; no optimum is known, F7); build sin^2 and t(1 - t) (not C-infinity; polynomial rates only).
- P6. Observables. Recommended: a caller-supplied pure function of the orbit point returning K values of the element type, plus kinds for the study's needs: sine and cosine of a coordinate with a frequency (exact on the wrapped coordinate, whose circle is the rounded 2 pi: standard-map spec W-E6, error at most pi eps per wrap) and a lifted coordinate rebuilt from the winding counts (naff-fma P4). The caller must guarantee smoothness (F5); the module cannot check it, records the observable names, and the dry run shows a discontinuous observable is accepted and read as chaos-like (R3). Device demands: isbits, allocation-free, no throw that builds a string, type-stable (a dry-run kernel built from a closure over tuples was not inferred and failed to compile, "dynamic function invocation"); functions the device compiler supports in Float64 (sin, cos, exp). Alternative: ship no kinds (smaller, but every caller re-derives the lifted rule).
- P7. Interface, state, chain. Reader of the orbit point at every handoff, returns the deviation matrix unchanged (so any position in a chain, no replacer role), O(1) isbits state: Float64 16 + 8 H (2 + 5 K) bytes, Double64 16 + 16 H (2 + 5 K) (B once per horizon, two compensated sums and S per observable and horizon, handoff count, failure code); K = 1, H = 1: 72 and 112; K = 4, H = 1: 192 and 368; K = 4, H = 11: 1952 and 3888 (derived; stage 04 reports sizeof). A check before the stream, in the host and not in the kernel: windows, order, K, run length 2 max(window) handoffs, every observable evaluated once at the supplied initial point (finite, K values); a throw reachable from the device reducer makes the kernel uncompilable (naff-fma D82 items 2, 3). A large state may fail to compile or run slower than the CPU at (K, H) = (4, 11): the recorded fallback reason then applies (Compute).
- P8. Windows, precision, horizons. N is a preregistered run parameter, never a default; minimum window 16 (the E2 value, one home; N below it is an argument error); start offset zero; tau recorded (the average of T^tau is the same limit, the Diophantine condition changes); a horizon grid is one orbit pass with H nested pairs of the same start (the study's N = 2^10 to 2^20 is H = 11, total 2^21 handoffs). Float64 default, Double64 arm (GPU: Float64 only, as E2), each type against its own BigFloat(256) reference; precision recorded.
- P9. Constants and the form of the numerical criteria. Float64 window averages differ from the independent BigFloat sums by at most 1.0 eps S (weights p = 1; 7.2 eps S at p = 2), Delta by 1.0 and 8.2; the device by 1.0 eps S (dry runs). The weight rounding scales with the exponent magnitude at the window centre, 4^p, so the tolerances are c_p eps S per average and 2 c_p eps S per Delta with c_p = 4^p (derived), realized ratios reported at stage 04 (rli P7 form). Gated: exactness, algebra, bitwise equalities. Reported, never gated: every empirical separation and every printed order (R1 to R8; D77).
- P10. Outputs and obligations. Per orbit, observable and horizon: avg_1, avg_2, S, the flags (below floor, zero, incomplete), about 4 values and a byte; the average, Delta and digits follow; identifiers: observable kinds and parameters, weight order, N, tau, element type, floor multiplier. The study (idea 25) reads Delta per N per observable; slopes and labels are its own. No series feed. Experiments records the chain, its order and the reducer options (the reducers cannot; provenance keys collide in a chain, naff-fma M4) and runs the host check before the stream. 1.4 kB per orbit at (4, 11) is a retention decision for the study, not the module.

## Algorithm

Host check before the stream (an invalid argument is an argument error naming the parameter, before any work): each N_h >= 16; order p >= 1; K >= 1 observables; run length 2 max(N_h) handoffs (tau divides the run); every observable finite at the supplied initial point and of K values.

Reducer, per handoff s (1-based, j = s - 1): form f_k(x) for the K observables, once for all horizons; a non-finite value sets a failure code (observable index) in the state and nothing else changes (the host raises with the orbit id; no throw in the kernel). For each horizon h with j < 2 N_h: i = floor(j/N_h), jj = j - i N_h, w = w^[p](min(jj, N_h - jj)/N_h) in the element type (no table); if w != 0, add w to B_h (i = 0 only) and w f_k to A_{k,h,i} (Neumaier); S_{k,h} = max(S_{k,h}, |f_k|). A horizon is frozen after 2 N_h handoffs. Final, per (k, h): avg_1 from N_h handoffs, avg_2 from 2 N_h; Delta, digits, flags (WB15); NaN before completion; `complete` once the largest horizon is done.

Pure function: the same sums for a series of any float type (BigFloat included), horizons as the reducer, optional weight vector for the recorded lines; the reducer and the function share the update, so the reducer is bitwise the function on the series sampled at the handoffs.

## Invariants

- I1. Constants: avg = c within 2 eps |c|; Delta = 0 bitwise; the flag is set (WB16).
- I2. Covariances: scaling by 2^k is exact (average, Delta, S bitwise scaled); avg(a f + b) = a avg(f) + b within c_p eps (|a| S + |b|); Delta(f + b) = Delta(f) within 2 c_p eps (S + |b|); exchanging the windows negates Delta and leaves the digits bitwise (symmetric by construction).
- I3. Normalisation: the weights sum to 1 within 4 eps (NF I5) and are bitwise symmetric (NF D81 item 10); both windows share B.
- I4. Independence: the result of one (observable, horizon) is bitwise independent of the other observables and horizons and of their order; a completed horizon never changes.
- I5. Index: Delta unclipped; digits never above -log10 phi; the flag holds exactly when |Delta| <= phi.
- I6. Reducer and function agree bitwise.
- I7. Orbit transparency: orbit points, deviation matrix and the outputs of the other reducers are bitwise those of the run without it; no column is replaced.
- I8. Execution invariance: bitwise identical for 1 or N threads and any chunk size on the CPU; no random numbers.
- I9. Health: a failure is recorded with the observable index, the host raises with the orbit id; a constant observable is a status, not a failure.

## Compute

Policy `_config/architecture.md`, "Compute" (D17, D25). Data-parallel over orbits (one thread per orbit, independent). One reader in the stream's single kernel: per handoff K observable evaluations (shared), H weights (one exponential and two divisions each), 2 H K compensated adds; no allocation after warm-up, no dense factorization, so no CPU-only fallback reason. Memory per orbit: the state of P7 inside the reducer bytes of the tangent-core capacity formula; no series buffer, no workspace. GPU candidate in Float64; Double64 only if the kernel compiles, else the recorded fallback reason (E2 precedent: Double64 falls back). Hazards from naff-fma D82: no string-building throw, allocation or unstable inference reachable from the device path; checks live in the host check and constructors; observables obey P6; only the GPU tag catches a regression, so it is rerun after every review fix. BigFloat and the pure function run on CPU threads. Backend agreement (D68 form, D82): each backend against its own BigFloat(256) at its own realized samples, and the device reducer against the CPU function on the same read-back samples, on the algebra only, never on trajectories; the orbit-level gap is reported. Cost orientation, not measured here: E2 at one horizon added 6 to 9 percent over the Benettin stream (naff-fma R5); this reader adds H exponentials per handoff (R5).
