## Oracle lines (criteria 14 and 15), as the spec states them

ChaosTools 3.5.4 has no stretching number, spectrum or spectral distance routine (spec Oracle). Criterion 14 is the oracle's independent tangent evolution: one `TangentDynamicalSystem` with k = 1 per seeded column and the hand-coded Jacobian of the lifted standard map, `step!` tau times, the deviation read, its log norm taken in BigFloat(256), the deviation renormalised in Float64; z_k is the log norm over tau; the histogram and D are formed by the plain formula in BigFloat (`sn_prefix_counts`, `sn_D_ref`) and compared with the module's counts and D (the module run is the serial per-orbit body of the suite on the lifted `StandardMap`, supplied seeded frame, snapshots at n). First the oracle orbits equal the stream's lifted orbits bitwise at every step (0 differences in 400 000 + 4 000 000 + 400 000 orbit-steps of two columns), so no orbit divergence term is needed and the oracle's own envelope has the width of ours (the bound is the doubled form). The line tests the stretch series, the windowing and the cell rule against an independent code path, not the definition of the spectrum or of D, which no independent implementation has for maps; the definition is tested by the closed forms and the algebra of D (criteria 1, 2, 3, 8). Criterion 15 is the local growth rate of ChaosTools (`local_growth_rates(ds, [u]; S = 1, Δt = 1, perturbation = ...)`, finite perturbation of the full nonlinear map over one step, no rescaling) with e = 1e-6 along the unit column from the independent tangent line at each window start, compared with the module's z_k (differences of its compensated totals, BigFloat) and, as a side check, with the oracle's own a_k; M_state is the maximum of 1, abs(K) and the largest coordinate on the lifted orbit up to the end of the window (the cumulative reading of the spec's definition).

## Limits

- The R1 and R2 lines are reports on one set of starts per class (1000 and 100) and one seed; no confidence intervals are given.
- Criterion 16 is gated on Float64 only. The chaotic class in (a) is n = 1e4 against the regular class n = 4e4; the D at these n is far below the 100 B points per cell reference level (under-sampled flag set), so the chaotic D rows test the arithmetic of the integer counts, not a physical separation.
- The CPU and GPU kernel timings (R6: Float64, 20 000 orbits x 1000 steps, K = 2 pi, wrapped, best of 3 for the GPU, 2 for the CPU, 4 threads, the machine was not otherwise idle): StretchingNumbers B = 100 GPU 5.62 ns per orbit-step against CPU 11.5 (speedup 2.05); B = 1000 GPU 14.4 against CPU 18.0 (1.25); L1 single (k = 1) GPU 2.54 against CPU 11.4 (4.46); MEGNO single (k = 2) GPU 5.17 against CPU 14.2 (2.74). So the two-column stretching run costs 2.2 times the MEGNO run on the GPU at B = 100 and 2.8 times at B = 1000 (the scattered integer increments), the memory traffic F8 predicted; the GPU is faster than the CPU at both cell numbers. Reducer state 104 bytes (Float64) and 176 (Double64) plus 816 or 8016 workspace bytes per orbit at B = 100 or 1000. These timings are not benchmarks.
- Review limits 2, 5 and 6: a tie on an interior cell edge follows the rounding of the cell width; the provenance cell width is in the range type; the standard deviation resolves about sqrt(eps) (SN7). The reducer does not run inside Experiments until the recorder allocates a workspace (stage 05 obligation).
- Not measured here: coverage and size claims of the impl notes, `bench/`, the Experiments chain wiring (the recorder obligation of Amendment D79 P9 is for stage 05), the `docs/` build, R3 (weak chaos, deferred to coupled-maps), and the whole-package suite.
